\title{Binius64 Blueprint}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This document specifies Binius64, a hash-based zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge (ZK-SNARK) tailored to computations expressed over $64$-bit machine words. Binius64 arithmetizes a computation as a circuit built from four native constraint forms---vanishing, bitwise AND, $64$-bit unsigned integer multiplication, and multiplication in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$---acting on XOR-accumulations of bit-shifted words. This word-level constraint language matches the instruction set of modern processors and avoids the roughly $64$-fold blowup that bit-level circuit encodings incur. The protocol works entirely in characteristic $2$ and draws its verifier challenges from the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$, so that the prover's field arithmetic is carried by the carryless-multiplication instructions (\texttt{CLMUL}, \texttt{PMULL}) already present on x86-64 and ARM64 processors for the AES-GCM and GMAC modes. Binius64 has a transparent setup, rests only on the collision resistance of a hash function modeled as a random oracle, and is plausibly post-quantum secure. Its proofs are succinct in size. We obtain zero knowledge by composing the core protocol with a Spartan-based outer argument, and treat the construction in full. This document is intended as the canonical protocol specification: it develops the construction in detail while deferring the proofs of standard cryptographic primitives to the peer-reviewed literature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

A \emph{succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge}, or SNARK, lets a verifier check the result of a computation far more cheaply than re-executing it, while remaining convinced---up to a negligible soundness error---that the prover holds a witness for which the computation was carried out correctly. SNARKs are the central primitive of \emph{verifiable computing}~\cite{Setty:2020aa}: a small, trusted device can audit the output of an untrusted, possibly faulty, and far more powerful one. When the argument additionally reveals nothing about the witness beyond the truth of the statement, it is \emph{zero-knowledge}, the property that underpins privacy-preserving applications such as verifiable credentials and confidential transactions.

Many of the computations one most wants to prove---hash functions such as SHA-256, SHA-3, and Blake3, block ciphers, and the bit manipulation at the core of cryptographic primitives---are naturally expressed as operations on machine words: $64$-bit XOR, AND, shifts, rotations, and integer multiplication. General-purpose SNARKs built over large prime fields can prove such operations only after an expensive bit-by-bit decomposition, and even most binary-field systems pay a cost per individual bit. Binius64 instead takes the $64$-bit word as its unit of arithmetization. The constraint system (\S\ref{sec:constraint-system}) offers exactly four native constraint forms---\emph{Zero}, the vanishing of an XOR-accumulation of shifted words; \emph{BitAnd}, a bitwise AND between two such accumulations; \emph{IntMul}, an unsigned $64$-bit integer multiplication; and \emph{BinMul}, a multiplication in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$---and derives the remaining common operations (NOT, OR, integer addition and subtraction; \S\ref{sec:common-ops}) from them. The key device is the \emph{shifted value index}, which fuses a reference to a prover word with a pair of shift operations applied in sequence, so that shifts and rotations are absorbed into the operands of the AND and multiplication constraints rather than charged as separate constraints. Operating on words rather than bits reduces the number of constraints by up to a factor of $64$ relative to a bit-level encoding, while retaining the efficiency of binary-field arithmetic.

That efficiency is rooted in the choice of field. Binius64 works entirely in characteristic $2$. Binary-field addition is bitwise XOR; the prime field $\FF{2}$ is the natural home of a single bit; and---decisively for performance---multiplication in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$ is accelerated in hardware on precisely the processors we target. The carryless-multiplication instructions \texttt{CLMUL} on x86-64 and \texttt{PMULL} on ARM64, shipped to support the AES-GCM authenticated-encryption mode and the GMAC message-authentication code, supply exactly the field multiplication the prover needs. We accordingly take $K \coloneqq \FF{2^{128}}$, with security parameter $\lambda = 128$, as the field from which the verifier draws its random challenges (\S\ref{sec:binary-fields}). The protocol is designed throughout for high throughput on commodity x86-64 and ARM64 CPUs, including the SIMD units of modern mobile processors, which makes it well suited to local proving.

The construction is hash-based. Its only cryptographic assumption is the collision resistance of a hash function, modeled in the security analysis as a random oracle; there is no trusted setup and no structured reference string, and the scheme is plausibly secure against quantum adversaries. These are conservative assumptions by the standards of the SNARK literature, and they are inherited directly from the polynomial commitment layer rather than bolted on.

At present, Binius64 is succinct in \emph{proof size}: a proof is polylogarithmic in the size of the computation being proved. Verification is not yet succinct---the verifier reads the circuit description in the course of checking a proof---and reducing verification cost is ongoing work. We are careful to state this distinction precisely where it matters (\S\ref{sec:binius64-snark}); the generic compiler of \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler} is succinct in both proof size and verifier time whenever the source protocol's queries are succinct, a condition the Binius64 verifier does not currently meet because it evaluates the constraint matrices directly.

\subsection{Why Not Binary Spartan?}

A natural way to build a multilinear SNARK over binary fields would be to adapt Spartan~\cite{Setty:2020aa} to $\FF{2}$. The witness becomes a single multilinear polynomial over $\FF{2}$, which the binary-field polynomial commitment of Diamond and Posen~\cite{Diamond:2024ab} commits efficiently, and the rank-one constraints are checked by a sumcheck. The approach is appealing, but it founders on the \emph{wiring check}. A rank-one constraint system over $n$ witness bits has matrices $A, B, C$ with $O(n)$ nonzero entries, and the prover's partial evaluation of these matrices entails $O(n)$ multiplications between elements of the large challenge field $K$ and bits of $\FF{2}$. With $\lambda = \log_2 |K|$ the security parameter, this is $O(\lambda n)$ bit operations, and proving the sparse-matrix openings incurs a second factor of $\lambda$. The large field infects what ought to be a bit-level computation.

Binius64 removes this bottleneck by working at word granularity. Packing $64$ bits into a word shrinks the constraint-matrix entry count by up to $64\times$; modeling the word width as scaling with $\lambda$, the prover's wiring cost falls from $O(\lambda n)$ back to $O(n)$ bit operations. The native BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul constraints, together with the shifted value indices, are what let the system express realistic computations at this granularity without reintroducing per-bit overhead.

We do nonetheless return to Spartan, in a different guise. The zero-knowledge construction of \S\ref{sec:zk-compiler} uses \emph{IronSpartan} (\S\ref{sec:ironspartan}), a commit-and-prove adaptation of Spartan, as an \emph{outer} argument---applied not to the word-level witness but to the small circuit that describes the Binius64 verifier. There the assignment is already polylogarithmic in the size of the original computation, so the $O(\lambda n)$ bottleneck above never arises, and Spartan's simplicity and direct support for zero knowledge become exactly the right tools.

\subsection{Outline of the Construction}

The document develops Binius64 in layers, separating the information-theoretic protocol from its cryptographic compilation.

\S\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and recalls the building blocks: multilinear polynomials and the equality indicator, the binary fields used (the prime field $\FF{2}$, the Rijndael field $\FF{2^8}$, and the challenge field $K = \FF{2^{128}}$), the sumcheck protocol and its variants (the MLE-check, the zerocheck, batching, and the univariate skip), and the abstraction of a \emph{linear interactive oracle proof} (LIOP) that organizes the rest of the document.

\S\ref{sec:constraint-system} defines the constraint system as a self-contained combinatorial object: prover data laid out as $64$-bit words, the eight shift operations and the shifted value indices that name shifted words, and the BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul constraints built from XOR-accumulations of these. Appendix~\ref{sec:common-ops} shows how the everyday operations of a $64$-bit instruction set reduce to this language.

\S\ref{sec:binius-liop} specifies the \emph{Binius64 LIOP}, the heart of the protocol. The prover commits a single oracle---the packed witness---and a sequence of sumcheck-based reductions (the GKR-based IntMul reduction, the BinMul reduction, the BitAnd reduction, the shift reduction, ring-switching to the packed witness, and the public-input check) collapse satisfaction of the entire constraint system into a single linear query on that oracle.

\S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler} gives a generic compiler turning any LIOP into a SNARK, through the chain $\text{LIOP} \xrightarrow{\text{BaseFold}} \text{IOP} \xrightarrow{\text{BCS}} \text{IP} \xrightarrow{\text{Fiat--Shamir}} \text{SNARK}$. Each LIOP linear query is discharged by a BaseFold~\cite{Zeilberger:2024aa} proximity test on a constrained Reed--Solomon code over $K$, the oracles are replaced by Merkle commitments via the BCS transform~\cite{Ben-Sasson:2016aa}, and the interaction is removed by Fiat--Shamir. Applied to the Binius64 LIOP, this yields the (non-zero-knowledge) Binius64 SNARK of \S\ref{sec:binius64-snark}.

\S\ref{sec:ironspartan} specifies the \emph{IronSpartan LIOP}, a second, zero-knowledge LIOP for rank-one constraint systems, recast as a commit-and-prove argument. \S\ref{sec:zk-compiler} then assembles the zero-knowledge SNARK by \emph{proof composition}: the Binius64 LIOP is run as an inner protocol with its messages encrypted under one-time pads, and IronSpartan certifies in zero knowledge that the inner verifier would have accepted. The accompanying \emph{Batched ZK BaseFold} compiler masks the polynomial-commitment layer, so that the composed argument is zero-knowledge end to end.

This is a protocol specification rather than a self-contained cryptographic treatise. Our aim is to pin down the construction precisely enough to serve as the reference for an implementation, and to fill in the design choices and engineering details that sit between the academic results we build on and a working system. Where a security property follows from established, peer-reviewed work, we cite it and state how it is used rather than reprove it; the novel reductions are developed in full.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
\label{sec:notation}

This section fixes notation and recalls the polynomial-algebra and protocol building blocks used throughout the document.

\begin{description}
\item[Boolean hypercube] For $n \geq 0$, we write $\BB_n \coloneqq \set{0, 1}^n$ for the $n$-dimensional Boolean hypercube. We will frequently identify $\BB_n$ with the set $\set{0, \ldots, 2^n - 1}$ of nonnegative integers via the little-endian bit decomposition $i \mapsto (i_0, \ldots, i_{n - 1})$, where $i = \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} i_j \cdot 2^j$.

\item[Constant vectors] For a length $n \geq 0$, we write $0^n$ and $1^n$ for the all-zeros and all-ones vectors, identified with the corresponding vertices of $\BB_n$. We use the same string-power notation for any constant block of $n$ identical coordinates within a longer argument list.

\item[Functions] For sets $S$ and $V$, the set of functions $S \to V$ is denoted $V^S$. We use the same symbol $f$ both for a function $f \in V^{\BB_n}$ and for its corresponding length-$2^n$ list of values; context will distinguish.

\item[Inner product] For any field $K$ and vectors $u, v \in K^n$, we write $\langle u, v \rangle \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{n - 1} u_i \cdot v_i$ for the standard inner product.

\item[Tensor product expansion] For vectors $u \in K^a$ and $v \in K^b$, the \emph{tensor product} $u \bigotimes v \in K^{a \cdot b}$ is the vector whose entry indexed by $(i, j) \in \set{0, \ldots, a - 1} \times \set{0, \ldots, b - 1}$ is $u_i \cdot v_j$, under the lexicographic enumeration of pairs. For $n \geq 0$ and $x = (x_0, \ldots, x_{n - 1}) \in K^n$, the \emph{tensor product expansion} is the map $\textsf{TensorExpand}_n \colon K^n \to K^{2^n}$ defined by
\[
  \textsf{TensorExpand}_n(x) \coloneqq \bigotimes_{i = 0}^{n - 1} (1 - x_i, x_i),
\]
where each factor $(1 - x_i, x_i)$ is in $K^2$. The entry of $\textsf{TensorExpand}_n(x)$ indexed by $w \in \BB_n$ equals $\prod_{i = 0}^{n - 1} \left( (1 - x_i)^{1 - w_i} \cdot x_i^{w_i} \right)$, which is precisely $\mle{\eq}_n(x, w)$ for the equality indicator defined below. Thus $\textsf{TensorExpand}_n(x) = \bigl( \mle{\eq}_n(x, w) \bigr)_{w \in \BB_n}$ is the table of values of the partial evaluation $\mle{\eq}_n(x, \cdot)$ on the hypercube. The expansion can be computed in $O(2^n)$ field operations~\cite[Lem.~3.8]{Thaler:2022aa}.

\item[Multilinear polynomials] For a ring $R$ and indeterminates $X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}$, we write $R[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]^\mlin$ for the subspace of polynomials of individual degree at most one in each indeterminate; these are the \emph{multilinear} polynomials. The evaluation map $t \mapsto (t(u))_{u \in \BB_n}$ is a $K$-linear bijection between $K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]^\mlin$ and $K^{\BB_n}$; for a function $f \in K^{\BB_n}$, we write $\mle{f} \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]^\mlin$ for the unique multilinear whose restriction to $\BB_n$ equals $f$, the \emph{multilinear extension} of $f$. When $f$ takes values in the subfield $\FF{2} \subset K$, $\mle{f}$ has $\FF{2}$-coefficients. See \cite[Fact 3.5]{Thaler:2022aa} for an alternative direct proof of uniqueness.

\item[Equality indicator] The \emph{equality indicator} is the bivariate multilinear $\mle{\eq}(X, Y) \coloneqq (1 - X)(1 - Y) + X Y$, which in characteristic $2$ simplifies to $1 + X + Y$. For $n \geq 1$ its \emph{$n$-fold} version is the $2n$-variate multilinear
\[
  \mle{\eq}_n(X, Y) \coloneqq \prod_{i = 0}^{n - 1} \mle{\eq}(X_i, Y_i), \qquad X, Y \in K^n,
\]
taking value $1$ on the diagonal of $\BB_n \times \BB_n$ and $0$ elsewhere; the multilinear extension of any $f \colon \BB_n \to K$ decomposes as $\mle{f}(X) = \sum_{u \in \BB_n} f(u) \cdot \mle{\eq}_n(u, X)$. The characteristic-$2$ form uses only $n - 1$ multiplications, so $\mle{\eq}_n(r, r')$ can be evaluated in $O(n)$ field operations at any pair of points $r, r' \in K^n$. We abbreviate $\mle{\eq}_1$ as $\mle{\eq}$, and also write the bare symbol $\mle{\eq}$ when referring to the family generically; an \emph{applied} indicator of arity $n \geq 2$ always carries its subscript.

\item[Infinity hypercube] The \emph{infinity hypercube} $\set{0, \infty}^n$~\cite[\S 3]{cryptoeprint:2026/762} is the product set whose coordinates are $0$ or $\infty$, where evaluating a polynomial of individual degree at most $d$ at $\infty$ in a coordinate reads the coefficient of that coordinate's top power $X_j^d$. A multilinear evaluated on $\set{0, \infty}^n$ yields its monomial coefficients, so $\sum_{x \in \set{0, \infty}^n} \mle{u}(x) \cdot \mle{w}(x)$ is the inner product of the monomial coefficient vectors of $\mle{u}$ and $\mle{w}$, and summing out a coordinate a polynomial does not depend on leaves it unchanged. A sumcheck over $\set{0, \infty}^n$ is Protocol~\ref{prot:sumcheck} with the consistency check $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(\infty) = s^{(i)}$, where $R^{(i)}(\infty)$ is the leading coefficient of $R^{(i)}$; its soundness error is that of the sumcheck over $\BB_n$.

\item[Vector arguments] We routinely flatten vector arguments into scalar ones: when a function $g$ expects $a + b$ scalar inputs and $u \in K^a$, $v \in K^b$, we write $g(u, v)$ for $g(u_0, \ldots, u_{a - 1}, v_0, \ldots, v_{b - 1})$, listing the coordinates of each vector in order, and we group consecutive scalar arguments back into vectors when convenient. This abuse is most frequent for the equality indicator $\mle{\eq}_n$, whose two length-$n$ vector arguments are often written out coordinate-wise (as in $\mle{\eq}_3(\rho_0, \rho_1, \rho_2, u_0, u_1, u_2)$).

\item[GKR] The \emph{GKR protocol}~\cite{Goldwasser:2015aa} is an interactive reduction that, given a layered arithmetic circuit and an evaluation claim on the multilinear extension of its output layer at a point in $K^n$, recursively reduces, layer by layer via sumcheck on the wiring polynomials, to evaluation claims on the multilinear extension of the input layer.
\end{description}

\subsection{Binary Fields}
\label{sec:binary-fields}

We work exclusively in characteristic $2$. Three binary fields recur throughout: the prime field $\FF{2}$, in which the witness lives; the \emph{Rijndael field} $\FF{2^8}$, used by the prover in the BitAnd reduction (\S\ref{sec:binand}); and a cryptographically large extension field $\FF{2^\lambda}$, for a power-of-two security parameter $\lambda$, from which the verifier draws random challenges. We abbreviate $K \coloneqq \FF{2^\lambda}$ in protocol specifications and elsewhere when convenient, and write $K^*$ for its multiplicative group of units; in practice we take $\lambda = 128$. We require $8 \mid \lambda$, so that $\FF{2^8}$ embeds into $K$ (see \emph{Subfield embedding} below). The construction imposes two further divisibility conditions relating $\lambda$ to the padded witness and public-segment sizes; these are stated, and arranged by padding, in \S\ref{sec:protocol-overview}.

\paragraph{Freshman's dream.}
In characteristic $2$ the binomial cross-term vanishes, so squaring is additive: $(\alpha + \beta)^2 = \alpha^2 + \beta^2$ for all $\alpha, \beta \in \FF{2^n}$. Iterating, $(\alpha + \beta)^{2^j} = \alpha^{2^j} + \beta^{2^j}$ for every $j \geq 0$. This is the elementary form of the Frobenius linearity revisited below, and underlies the Frobenius-twisting manipulations of \S\ref{sec:intmul}.

\paragraph{Construction.}
For any $n \geq 1$, fixing an irreducible polynomial $f(X) \in \FF{2}[X]$ of degree $n$, the quotient ring $\FF{2}[X] / (f(X))$ is a field of size $2^n$, which we denote $\FF{2^n}$. Each $\alpha \in \FF{2^n}$ is represented as a polynomial of degree less than $n$, so the monomials $1, X, \ldots, X^{n - 1}$ form an $\FF{2}$-basis of $\FF{2^n}$; concretely, elements of $\FF{2^n}$ are $n$-bit strings, and those of $K = \FF{2^\lambda}$ are $\lambda$-bit strings. Two binary fields from cryptographic practice serve as running examples: the \emph{Rijndael field} $\FF{2^8} \cong \FF{2}[X] / (X^8 + X^4 + X^3 + X + 1)$, the base field of the AES block cipher, and the \emph{GHASH field} $\FF{2^{128}} \cong \FF{2}[X] / (X^{128} + X^7 + X^2 + X + 1)$, used in the AES-GCM authenticated-encryption mode and the GMAC message-authentication code.

\paragraph{Subfield embedding.}
When $m \mid n$, the field $\FF{2^m}$ occurs inside $\FF{2^n}$ as the unique subfield of size $2^m$. We exploit this for the Rijndael field $\FF{2^8}$, which embeds into $K$ under the assumption $8 \mid \lambda$ via a fixed embedding $\iota \colon \FF{2^8} \hookrightarrow K$. We refer to \cite{Diamond:2024ab} for further background on representing and computing in binary fields.

\paragraph{Algebraic properties.}
The multiplicative group $\FF{2^n}^*$ is cyclic, so we may fix a generator $g \in \FF{2^n}^*$ whose powers $g^a$, for $a \in \set{0, \ldots, 2^n - 2}$, exhaust $\FF{2^n}^*$. The \emph{Frobenius endomorphism} $\varphi \colon \FF{2^n} \to \FF{2^n}$ sending $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^2$ is $\FF{2}$-linear, and in finite fields it is invertible. The $n$-fold iterate $\varphi^n$ acts as the identity on $\FF{2^n}$; we use this in \S\ref{sec:intmul} to manipulate evaluation claims involving Frobenius twists on $K$.

\subsection{Sumcheck}
\label{sec:sumcheck}

The \emph{sumcheck protocol}~\cite{Lund:1992aa,Thaler:2022aa} is an interactive reduction that transforms a sum claim $s \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{v \in \BB_n} F(v)$ over a polynomial $F \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ into a single evaluation claim $s' \stackrel{?}{=} F(r)$ at a verifier-chosen point $r \in K^n$, with soundness error $n \cdot d / |K|$, where $d$ is the maximum individual degree of $F$. In our applications $F$ is a \emph{composite of multilinears},
\[
  F(X) = C\bigl(\mle{f}_0(X), \ldots, \mle{f}_{m - 1}(X)\bigr),
\]
for multilinears $\mle{f}_0, \ldots, \mle{f}_{m - 1} \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]^\mlin$ and a \emph{composition polynomial} $C$ of total degree $d$ (so $F$ has individual degree at most $d$). For such $F$ the prover runs in $O(2^n \cdot m \cdot d)$ field operations and the verifier in $O(n \cdot d)$ time~\cite[\S 3]{Gruen:2024aa}. The reduction is given in Protocol~\ref{prot:sumcheck}; several variants recur throughout the document. We bind variables from the highest index to the lowest---in round $i$ binding $X_{n - i - 1}$ and sampling the challenge $r_{n - i - 1}$---a convention we use throughout by default, as it lends itself to a more SIMD-efficient prover algorithm.

\begin{protocol}[Sumcheck]
\label{prot:sumcheck}
\textbf{Claim:} $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} F(x) = s$, for $F \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ of individual degree $\le d$.

\textbf{Rounds:} Set $s^{(0)} \coloneqq s$. For $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$, write $k \coloneqq n - i - 1$ (so round $i$ binds $X_k$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item The prover sends the \emph{round polynomial}
\[
  R^{(i)}(X) \coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_k} F(v, X, r_{k + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) \in K[X], \qquad \deg R^{(i)} \le d,
\]
where $v$ ranges over the low variables $X_0, \ldots, X_{k - 1}$ and $r_{k + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}$ are the previously sampled challenges; $d$ of its $d + 1$ coefficients are transmitted, the verifier recovering the last from the check below.
\item The verifier checks $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$, samples $r_k \gets K$, and sets $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_k)$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Output:} the evaluation claim $F(r) = s^{(n)}$ at the point $r \coloneqq (r_0, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) \in K^n$.
\end{protocol}

\paragraph{MLE-check.}
A \emph{multilinear extension check}, or \emph{MLE-check}, is a sumcheck specialized to summands of the form $\mle{\eq}_n(\tau, \cdot) \cdot g(\cdot)$ for a challenge $\tau \in K^n$; since $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} \mle{\eq}_n(\tau, x) \cdot g(x) = \mle{g}(\tau)$, an MLE-check reduces an evaluation claim on $\mle{g}$ at $\tau$ to an evaluation claim on $g$ (Protocol~\ref{prot:mle-check}). The tensor structure of $\mle{\eq}$ enables a more efficient prover algorithm~\cite[\S 3]{Gruen:2024aa}.

\begin{protocol}[Multilinear-extension check]
\label{prot:mle-check}
\textbf{Claim:} $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} \mle{\eq}_n(\tau, x) \cdot g(x) = s$, for a point $\tau \in K^n$ and $g \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ of individual degree $\le d$.

\textbf{Rounds:} Set $s^{(0)} \coloneqq s$. For $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$, write $k \coloneqq n - i - 1$ (so round $i$ binds $X_k$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item The prover sends the \emph{round polynomial}
\[
  R^{(i)}(X) \coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_k} \mle{\eq}_k\bigl( (\tau_0, \ldots, \tau_{k - 1}), v \bigr) \cdot g(v, X, r_{k + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) \in K[X], \qquad \deg R^{(i)} \le d,
\]
where $v$ ranges over the low variables $X_0, \ldots, X_{k - 1}$ and the equality-indicator weight on those unbound variables has been pulled out; $d$ of its $d + 1$ coefficients are transmitted.
\item The verifier checks $s^{(i)} = (1 - \tau_k)\, R^{(i)}(0) + \tau_k\, R^{(i)}(1)$, samples $r_k \gets K$, and sets $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_k)$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Output:} the evaluation claim $g(r) = s^{(n)}$ at the point $r \coloneqq (r_0, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) \in K^n$.
\end{protocol}

\paragraph{Zerocheck.}
A \emph{zerocheck} reduces a claim that a polynomial $t \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ vanishes on $\BB_n$ to an MLE-check: the verifier samples $\tau \gets K^n$ and the parties run an MLE-check on $\mle{\eq}_n(\tau, \cdot) \cdot t(\cdot)$ with claimed sum $0$. Soundness error is $n / |K|$ above the underlying MLE-check.

\paragraph{Batching.}
Several sumcheck claims $s_i \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{v \in \BB_n} F_i(v)$ over polynomials $F_0, \ldots, F_{k - 1} \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ on a \emph{common} number of variables can be discharged by a single sumcheck~\cite{cryptoeprint:2017/305}: the verifier samples a batching challenge $\alpha \gets K$, and the parties run sumcheck on
\[
  \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha^i s_i \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{v \in \BB_n} \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha^i F_i(v).
\]
Soundness of the reduction follows from Schwartz--Zippel applied to the batching polynomial in $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Batching MLE-checks at different points.}
\label{sec:batching-mle-checks}
The equality indicator of an MLE-check can be pulled out of the round polynomial, as in Protocol~\ref{prot:mle-check}, only when every summand in the batch carries the same one. Batching MLE-checks on $g_0, \ldots, g_{p - 1}$ at \emph{different} points $\tau_0, \ldots, \tau_{p - 1}$ therefore keeps the indicators inside the summand. In round $i$, binding $X_k$ with $k \coloneqq n - i - 1$, the batched round polynomial is
\begin{align*}
  R^{(i)}(X) &= \sum_{m = 0}^{p - 1} \alpha^m \cdot E_m \cdot \mle{\eq}(\tau_{m, k}, X) \cdot P_m(X), \\
  P_m(X) &\coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_k} \mle{\eq}_k\bigl( (\tau_{m, 0}, \ldots, \tau_{m, k - 1}), v \bigr) \cdot g_m(v, X, r_{k + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}),
\end{align*}
with $E_m \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{n - k - 1}\bigl( (\tau_{m, k + 1}, \ldots, \tau_{m, n - 1}), (r_{k + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) \bigr)$ the value of claim $m$'s indicator on the already-bound variables. The batched round polynomial therefore has degree one above the largest $\deg g_m$, and the verifier reverts to the plain check $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$ of Protocol~\ref{prot:sumcheck} against the combined target. The prover keeps the efficient algorithm nonetheless: its pass over the hypercube computes the factored $P_m$, of the composite's own degree, and only afterwards is $P_m$ multiplied by the linear factor $\mle{\eq}(\tau_{m, k}, X)$ and the scalar $E_m$ and accumulated into the batch. Relative to a single MLE-check with its indicator factored out, the batch thus costs one extra coefficient per round and no extra hypercube work.

\paragraph{Variable padding.}
\label{sec:variable-padding}
The batched sumcheck above assumes the $F_i$ share a common number of variables; when they do not, we pad each up to the common count. To raise an $n$-variate claim $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} F(x) = s$ to $n + \nu$ variables, the parties instead sumcheck the padded summand $F(X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_\nu\bigl( 0^\nu, (X_n, \ldots, X_{n + \nu - 1}) \bigr)$ over $\BB_{n + \nu}$, with the same target $s$. The extra factor $\mle{\eq}_\nu(0^\nu, \cdot)$ is supported on the single point $X_n = \cdots = X_{n + \nu - 1} = 0$, where it equals $1$, so summing over the $\nu$ padding variables contributes a factor of $1$ and the padded claim holds iff the original one does.

Since we bind variables from the highest index down, the $\nu$ padding variables $X_n, \ldots, X_{n + \nu - 1}$ are bound first---in rounds $i = 0, \ldots, \nu - 1$---and the original variables only afterwards. The padding rounds are essentially free, and the remaining rounds reproduce the unpadded sumcheck up to a constant:
\begin{itemize}
\item In a padding round $i < \nu$ (binding $X_{n + \nu - 1 - i}$), every original variable is still unbound, so summing over them returns the original target $s$, and the round polynomial is
\[
  R^{(i)}(X) = s \cdot \mle{\eq}(0, X) \cdot \mle{\eq}_i\bigl( 0^i, (r_{n + \nu - i}, \ldots, r_{n + \nu - 1}) \bigr),
\]
the bivariate $\mle{\eq}(0, X) = 1 - X$ for the current variable times the constant accumulated from the already-bound padding challenges. The prover touches $F$ not at all.
\item In a round $i \ge \nu$, all padding variables are bound and contribute the constant $c \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_\nu\bigl( 0^\nu, (r_n, \ldots, r_{n + \nu - 1}) \bigr)$, so the round polynomial is the unpadded one scaled by $c$,
\[
  R^{(i)}(X) = c \cdot R_F^{(i - \nu)}(X),
\]
where $R_F^{(i - \nu)}$ is the round polynomial of the original $n$-variate sumcheck on $F$ in its round $i - \nu$.
\end{itemize}
A polynomial entering the batch below the common variable count therefore costs only a string of trivial padding rounds before its ordinary sumcheck begins. Claims of differing lengths align on their \emph{low} variables: a claim on $\ell$ variables has them bound in the last $\ell$ rounds and closes at $r|_\ell \coloneqq (r_0, \ldots, r_{\ell - 1})$, the low $\ell$ coordinates of the common challenge vector $r$, so the output points of any two claims are nested, the shorter being a prefix of the longer. The document uses this padding twice: the constraint-index unification of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} batches the constraint reductions' sumchecks this way, and its nested output points are what let the shift reduction serve every constraint array from a single tensor expansion; and the $\textsf{ZeroPadMSB}$ lift of \S\ref{sec:lifted-basefold} is the same padding applied to an oracle, by identity \eqref{eq:zeropad-msb-mle}, which is what lets Batched BaseFold (\S\ref{sec:batched-basefold}) bring oracles of unequal size to a common count.

The multiplicative analog of this padding---batching binary-tree grand-product arguments over trees of unequal depths---is developed in Appendix~\ref{app:padded-prodcheck}.

\subsubsection{Univariate Skip}
\label{sec:oblong}
The \emph{univariate skip}~\cite[\S 5]{Gruen:2024aa} cuts the round count, and lets the prover work over a subfield, when the composite's inputs are defined over a subfield of the challenge field.

\paragraph{The long axis.}
Fix a $k$-dimensional $\FF{2}$-linear subspace $D \subset K$, so $|D| = 2^k$, and choose an $\FF{2}$-basis $(\zeta_0, \ldots, \zeta_{k - 1})$ identifying $\BB_k \cong D$ via $i \mapsto \widehat{i} \coloneqq i_0 \zeta_0 + \cdots + i_{k - 1} \zeta_{k - 1}$. We call this distinguished, size-$|D|$ dimension the \emph{long axis}, and decorate quantities living on it with hats.

\paragraph{Oblong multilinears.}
An \emph{oblong-multilinear} polynomial is multilinear in all but one variable, in which it instead has univariate degree below $|D| = 2^k$ over the domain $D$. The \emph{oblong-multilinearization} of a multilinear $t$ on $\BB_k \times \BB_\ell$ is
\[
  \widehat{t}(\widehat{I}, X) \coloneqq \sum_{i \in \BB_k} \delta_D(\widehat{I}, \widehat{i}) \cdot t(i, X),
\]
where $\delta_D$ is the bivariate equality-indicator on $D$, of individual degree below $2^k$.

\paragraph{The reduction.}
The univariate skip runs sumcheck on a composite $C(\widehat{f}_0, \ldots, \widehat{f}_{m - 1})$ of oblong multilinears. The first round, ranging over the size-$|D|$ domain in place of $\set{0, 1}$, is discharged by a single univariate polynomial send rather than $k$ binary rounds; the partial specialization
\[
  \widehat{t}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X) = \sum_{i \in \BB_k} \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \cdot t(i, X)
\]
at the resulting challenge $r_{\widehat{i}} \in K$ recovers an ordinary multilinear for the remaining rounds. Since the first-round univariate has degree below $d \cdot |D|$, where $d$ is the composition degree, the prover can carry out its arithmetic entirely within any subfield $\mathbb{F} \subseteq K$ with $|\mathbb{F}| \geq d \cdot |D|$, embedding into $K$ only once $r_{\widehat{i}}$ is drawn; this is the source of the prover speedup when the inputs $\widehat{f}_j$ take values in a small subfield.

\subsection{Linear Interactive Oracle Proofs}
\label{sec:liop}

A \emph{linear interactive oracle proof} (LIOP) is an interactive protocol between a prover $P$ and a verifier $V$ for a relation $R \subset \set{0, 1}^* \times \set{0, 1}^*$. Both parties take as input a public instance $x$; the prover additionally holds a witness $w$ with $(x, w) \in R$. The protocol proceeds in rounds.

\begin{itemize}
\item In each round, the prover may send one or more \emph{oracle messages} together with a possibly empty non-oracle message. The $i$-th oracle message is a function $\pi_i \colon \BB_{n_i} \to K$ for some $n_i \geq 0$ fixed by the protocol; equivalently, $\pi_i$ is a vector in $K^{2^{n_i}}$. The verifier does not access $\pi_i$ directly during the protocol. The protocol additionally fixes, for each oracle, a \emph{randomizable support} $I_i \subseteq \BB_{n_i}$, a (possibly empty) subset of coordinates whose values the prover does not choose (see below); unless stated otherwise $I_i = \emptyset$.
\item The verifier responds with a challenge sampled uniformly from $K$ (or, when convenient, from some fixed challenge space).
\item After all rounds, the verifier issues a set of \emph{linear queries}. The $j$-th linear query is specified by one multilinear polynomial $t_{j, i} \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n_i - 1}]^\mlin$ per oracle $\pi_i$, together with a target value $s_j \in K$, and is \emph{satisfied} if
\[
  \sum_i \sum_{v \in \BB_{n_i}} t_{j, i}(v) \cdot \pi_i(v) = s_j;
\]
i.e., the sum over the oracles of the inner product of each with the table of hypercube evaluations of its operand $t_{j, i}$ equals the target. Most queries involve a single oracle $\pi_{i_j}$; we then write the query as $(t_j, s_j)$ on $\pi_{i_j}$ and omit the vanishing operands. The verifier accepts iff all linear queries are satisfied. The list of queries is a deterministic function of the transcript.
\end{itemize}

A \emph{padding assignment} is a tuple $\textsf{pad} = (\textsf{pad}_i)_i$ of maps $\textsf{pad}_i \colon I_i \to K$, one for each oracle. The honest prover takes a padding assignment as an additional input and is \emph{constrained} by it: every oracle it sends satisfies $\pi_i(v) = \textsf{pad}_i(v)$ for all $v \in I_i$, and its remaining messages are computed consistently with these values. When every $I_i$ is empty, the only padding assignment is the empty one and the constraint is vacuous.

The LIOP is \emph{complete} if for every $(x, w) \in R$ and every padding assignment $\textsf{pad}$, the honest execution of $P(x, w, \textsf{pad})$ with $V(x)$ makes $V$ accept with probability $1$, and \emph{sound with error $\varepsilon$} if for every $x$ such that $(x, w) \notin R$ for all $w$ and every (possibly cheating) prover $P^*$, the probability that $V$ accepts is at most $\varepsilon$. Knowledge soundness is defined analogously via an efficient extractor. A cheating prover is subject to no padding constraint.

\paragraph{Randomizable support.}
Because completeness holds for every padding assignment, the values of $\pi_i$ on $I_i$ are not the prover's to choose: whoever runs the honest prover may fix them arbitrarily, in particular uniformly at random, without affecting acceptance, and soundness already quantifies over all provers. The zero-knowledge compiler of \S\ref{sec:zk-compiler} relies on this flexibility, both to hide the codeword openings and to merge oracles efficiently. The LIOPs of this document take $I_i = \emptyset$; the compiler introduces the supports it needs.

\paragraph{Succinct queries.}
A linear query is \emph{succinct} if each of its operands $t_{j, i}$ can be evaluated at an arbitrary point of $K^{n_i}$ in $\text{poly}(n_i)$ operations over $K$. All linear queries appearing in our LIOPs are succinct. The succinct queries used here are closely related to, but strictly more restrictive than, the \emph{succinct linear forms} of Chiesa, Fenzi, and Weissenberg~\cite[\S 5]{cryptoeprint:2026/391}, which permit arbitrary $K$-linear functionals admitting polylogarithmic-time evaluation; in our setting the functional is always the inner product against the hypercube evaluation table of a succinctly evaluable multilinear polynomial.

The basic example is the \emph{multilinear-evaluation query} at a point $r \in K^{n_i}$: taking $t(X) \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{n_i}(r, X)$, the constraint $\sum_{v \in \BB_{n_i}} \mle{\eq}_{n_i}(r, v) \cdot \pi_i(v) = s$ is precisely the assertion that the multilinear extension $\mle{\pi}_i$ takes value $s$ at $r$, and the succinctness of $\mle{\eq}$ makes the query succinct. LIOPs whose linear queries are all of this form are sometimes called polynomial IOPs (PIOPs); we use the broader term LIOP to admit other succinct multilinear $t_j$ where convenient.

The compilation of an LIOP into a SNARK using a polynomial commitment scheme is recalled in \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}.

\section{The Constraint System}
\label{sec:constraint-system}

The Binius64 constraint system operates on $64$-bit words and supports four native constraint forms: \emph{Zero constraints}, which express $\FF{2}$-linear relations among shifted prover words; \emph{BitAnd constraints}, which express bitwise-AND relations between XOR-accumulations of shifted prover words; \emph{IntMul constraints}, which express unsigned-integer multiplication on $64$-bit operands; and \emph{BinMul constraints}, which express multiplication in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$ on operands each carried by a pair of $64$-bit words. This section defines the constraint language as a combinatorial object, independent of any protocol that verifies it. Appendix~\ref{sec:common-ops} sketches how this language expresses common derived operations (bitwise NOT, OR; integer addition and subtraction).

\subsection{Prover Data Layout}
\label{sec:prover-data}

At runtime, the prover supplies a list of $64$-bit words. The list is divided into three contiguous \emph{stretches} of compile-time-fixed lengths $n_\text{const}$, $n_\text{inout}$, and $n_\text{witness}$, denoted respectively the \emph{constant}, \emph{input--output}, and \emph{witness} parts. We write $w$ for the concatenation of all three, so that $w$ has total length
\[
  n_\text{words} \coloneqq n_\text{const} + n_\text{inout} + n_\text{witness},
\]
and we write $w[y] \in \BB_{64}$ for the $y$-th word, indexed by $y \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{words} - 1}$.

The constant and input--output stretches are public: the verifier holds them. The constant stretch is fixed at compile time and is shared across all proofs; we record its values via a map $C \colon \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{const} - 1} \to \BB_{64}$. The input--output stretch is the public statement for the current proof, supplied as a map $u \colon \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{inout} - 1} \to \BB_{64}$. The remaining witness stretch is private to the prover and impacts the verifier's runtime only polylogarithmically. Throughout, we use the term \emph{prover data} (rather than \emph{witness}) for $w$ to emphasize that only part of $w$ is private.

The prover data $w$ \emph{satisfies} the constraint system with respect to the statement $u$ if all of the following hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item the constant stretch matches $C$: $w[y] = C(y)$ for each $y \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{const} - 1}$;
\item the input--output stretch matches $u$: $w[n_\text{const} + y] = u(y)$ for each $y \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{inout} - 1}$;
\item every Zero constraint is satisfied (\S\ref{sec:zero-constraints});
\item every BitAnd constraint is satisfied (\S\ref{sec:bitand-constraints});
\item every IntMul constraint is satisfied (\S\ref{sec:intmul-constraints});
\item every BinMul constraint is satisfied (\S\ref{sec:binmul-constraints}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Shifted Value Indices}
\label{sec:shifted-indices}

The constraints in this system act not on raw prover words but on \emph{shifted} versions of them. We support eight shift operations:
\[
  \textsf{ShiftOps} \coloneqq \set{\textsf{sll}, \textsf{srl}, \textsf{sra}, \textsf{ror}, \textsf{sll32}, \textsf{srl32}, \textsf{sra32}, \textsf{ror32}}.
\]
The first four are full-width $64$-bit operations. For $v \in \BB_{64}$ and $s \in \set{0, \ldots, 63}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\textsf{sll}(v, s)$ shifts $v$ towards the more-significant end by $s$ bits, zero-filling the vacated low bits;
\item $\textsf{srl}(v, s)$ shifts $v$ towards the less-significant end by $s$ bits, zero-filling the vacated high bits;
\item $\textsf{sra}(v, s)$ shifts $v$ towards the less-significant end by $s$ bits, replicating the most-significant bit into the vacated high positions;
\item $\textsf{ror}(v, s)$ rotates $v$ right by $s$ bits, so that the $s$ least-significant bits of $v$ wrap into the most-significant positions of the result.
\end{itemize}
The remaining four operations are the \emph{$32$-bit parallel} variants $\textsf{sll32}, \textsf{srl32}, \textsf{sra32}, \textsf{ror32}$. Each treats $v$ as a pair of independent $32$-bit halves (the low and high halves of $v$) and applies the corresponding shift to both halves in parallel. The $32$-bit operations use only the five least-significant bits of the $6$-bit shift amount $s$; the most-significant bit of $s$ is ignored.

Since $|\textsf{ShiftOps}| = 8$, we index the shift operations by the hypercube $\BB_3$: we write $\text{op}(o)$ for the $o$-th operation in the order listed above, under the little-endian identification of $\BB_3$ with $\set{0, \ldots, 7}$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}). The enumeration is chosen so that the most-significant bit of $o$ selects the width---clear for the four full-width operations, set for the four $32$-bit parallel variants---while the two least-significant bits select the base operation $\textsf{sll}, \textsf{srl}, \textsf{sra}, \textsf{ror}$ in either width.

A \emph{shifted value index} names a prover word together with a pair of shift operations applied to it in sequence. Formally it is a tuple
\[
  (y, \text{op}_1, s_1, \text{op}_2, s_2) \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{words} - 1} \times \bigl( \textsf{ShiftOps} \times \set{0, \ldots, 63} \bigr)^2,
\]
naming a prover-data index $y$, an \emph{inner} shift $(\text{op}_1, s_1)$, and an \emph{outer} shift $(\text{op}_2, s_2)$. We write $\textsf{val}$ for the value it represents:
\[
  \textsf{val}(y, \text{op}_1, s_1, \text{op}_2, s_2) \coloneqq \text{op}_2\bigl( \text{op}_1(w[y], s_1), s_2 \bigr) \in \BB_{64}.
\]
Since $s = 0$ acts as the identity for every $\text{op} \in \textsf{ShiftOps}$---$\textsf{sll}(v, 0) = \textsf{srl}(v, 0) = \cdots = v$---a zero amount in either slot suppresses that shift, so single shifts and raw prover words are the special cases $s_2 = 0$ and $s_1 = s_2 = 0$ rather than a separate form. For example, $(55, \textsf{sll}, 10, \textsf{sll}, 0)$ represents \texttt{w[55] << 10}; $(55, \textsf{sll}, 40, \textsf{sra}, 40)$ represents \texttt{(int64\_t) (w[55] << 40) >> 40}, the sign-extension of the low $24$ bits of \texttt{w[55]}; and $(55, \textsf{sll}, 0, \textsf{sll}, 0)$ represents \texttt{w[55]} itself.

Composing two shifts inside a single index is strictly more expressive than composing them across two indices: the latter would need an intermediate prover word, hence a witness entry and a Zero constraint to define it, whereas the former is free at the level of the constraint system and, as \S\ref{sec:shift-reduction} shows, nearly free in the protocol. Common patterns that a single shift cannot express---sign-extension of a sub-word field, extraction of a bit field to a fixed position, masking off a byte by shifting it off one end and back---become single index entries.

\paragraph{Canonical form.}
A zero shift amount makes the accompanying operation immaterial, so a value can be named by several tuples. We fix a canonical form, which makes the representation unique: when $s_2 = 0$ we take $\text{op}_2 \coloneqq \textsf{sll}$, when $s_1 = 0$ we take $\text{op}_1 \coloneqq \textsf{sll}$, and a lone shift is placed in the inner slot, so that $s_1 = 0$ implies $s_2 = 0$. A canonical index with $s_2 \neq 0$ therefore has $s_1 \neq 0$ as well. We call such an index \emph{doubly shifted}, one with $s_2 = 0 \neq s_1$ \emph{singly shifted}, and one with $s_1 = s_2 = 0$ \emph{unshifted}. Nothing in the reduction of \S\ref{sec:shift-reduction} depends on this normalization for correctness.

\subsection{Constraint Arrays}
\label{sec:constraint-arrays}

We use a notational device to package XOR-accumulations of shifted value indices. Fix some total count $n_\text{cons}$ of constraints, and assume that for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{cons} - 1}$ we are given a list $L_x$ of shifted value indices. The \emph{constraint array} associated with $(L_x)_{x = 0}^{n_\text{cons} - 1}$ is the length-$n_\text{cons}$ array $z \in \BB_{64}^{n_\text{cons}}$ defined by
\[
  z[x] \coloneqq \bigoplus_{(y, \text{op}_1, s_1, \text{op}_2, s_2) \in L_x} \text{op}_2\bigl( \text{op}_1(w[y], s_1), s_2 \bigr).
\]
We will define Zero, BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul constraints in terms of arrays of this form.

\subsection{Zero Constraints}
\label{sec:zero-constraints}

The simplest constraint form asserts that a single constraint array vanishes. The constraint system specifies a total number $n_\text{zero}$ of Zero constraints. The $x$-th Zero constraint is a single list of shifted value indices
\[
  L^d_x, \quad x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{zero} - 1},
\]
inducing one constraint array $d \in \BB_{64}^{n_\text{zero}}$ as in \S\ref{sec:constraint-arrays}. The prover data $w$ \emph{satisfies} the Zero constraints if for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{zero} - 1}$,
\[
  d[x] = \bigoplus_{(y, \text{op}_1, s_1, \text{op}_2, s_2) \in L^d_x} \text{op}_2\bigl( \text{op}_1(w[y], s_1), s_2 \bigr) = 0.
\]

A Zero constraint is thus an arbitrary $\FF{2}$-linear relation among shifted prover words. In particular, taking $L^d_x$ to be a two-element list constrains two shifted values to be equal, and taking it to be a three-element list $\set{u, v, z}$ forces $z = u \oplus v$, so bitwise XOR is native. Such relations are already expressible with BitAnd constraints alone---mask one side with the all-ones constant (Appendix~\ref{sec:common-ops})---but a Zero constraint carries one constraint array rather than three, and \S\ref{sec:zero} discharges it with no prover message and no sumcheck round at all.

\subsection{BitAnd Constraints}
\label{sec:bitand-constraints}

The constraint system specifies a total number $n_\text{and}$ of BitAnd constraints. The $x$-th BitAnd constraint is a triple of lists of shifted value indices
\[
  (L^a_x, L^b_x, L^c_x), \quad x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{and} - 1},
\]
which together induce three constraint arrays $a, b, c \in \BB_{64}^{n_\text{and}}$ as in \S\ref{sec:constraint-arrays}: for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{and} - 1}$,
\[
  a[x] \coloneqq \bigoplus_{u \in L^a_x} \textsf{val}(u), \quad
  b[x] \coloneqq \bigoplus_{u \in L^b_x} \textsf{val}(u), \quad
  c[x] \coloneqq \bigoplus_{u \in L^c_x} \textsf{val}(u).
\]
The prover data $w$ \emph{satisfies} the BitAnd constraints if for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{and} - 1}$,
\[
  a[x] \land b[x] = c[x],
\]
where $\land$ denotes bitwise AND on $64$-bit words.

\subsection{IntMul Constraints}
\label{sec:intmul-constraints}

The constraint system specifies a total number $n_\text{mul}$ of IntMul constraints. The $x$-th IntMul constraint is a tuple of four lists of shifted value indices
\[
  (L^a_x, L^b_x, L^{c_\text{lo}}_x, L^{c_\text{hi}}_x), \quad x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{mul} - 1},
\]
inducing four length-$n_\text{mul}$ constraint arrays $a, b, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi} \in \BB_{64}^{n_\text{mul}}$ as in \S\ref{sec:constraint-arrays}. Each word $a[x] \in \BB_{64}$ has bits $a[i, x] \in \FF{2}$ for $i = 0, \ldots, 63$; for any such bit vector we write
\[
  \intval{a}[x] \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{63} 2^i \cdot a[i, x] \in \set{0, \ldots, 2^{64} - 1}
\]
for the nonnegative integer encoded by the word at $x$ (and likewise for $b, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$). The prover data $w$ \emph{satisfies} the IntMul constraints if for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{mul} - 1}$,
\[
  \intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] = 2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x],
\]
the multiplication taken over the integers. Equivalently, $\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]$ and $\intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]$ are the high and low $64$-bit halves of the $128$-bit unsigned product $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x]$.

\subsection{BinMul Constraints}
\label{sec:binmul-constraints}

The constraint system specifies a total number $n_\text{binmul}$ of BinMul constraints. A BinMul constraint multiplies two elements of the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}} \cong \FF{2}[X] / (X^{128} + X^7 + X^2 + X + 1)$ (\S\ref{sec:binary-fields}). Since $\FF{2^{128}}$ is a $128$-dimensional $\FF{2}$-space while a prover word holds only $64$ bits, each field element is carried by a pair of words---a \emph{low} word supplying the coefficients of $1, X, \ldots, X^{63}$ and a \emph{high} word supplying the coefficients of $X^{64}, \ldots, X^{127}$. Concretely, for a pair of words $(z_\text{lo}, z_\text{hi}) \in \BB_{64} \times \BB_{64}$ with bits $z_\text{lo}[i], z_\text{hi}[i] \in \FF{2}$, we write
\[
  \gval{z_\text{lo}, z_\text{hi}} \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{63} z_\text{lo}[i] \cdot X^i + \sum_{i = 0}^{63} z_\text{hi}[i] \cdot X^{64 + i} \in \FF{2^{128}}
\]
for the field element it encodes, the monomials $1, X, \ldots, X^{127}$ being the $\FF{2}$-basis of $\FF{2^{128}}$ fixed in \S\ref{sec:binary-fields}.

The $x$-th BinMul constraint is a tuple of six lists of shifted value indices
\[
  (L^{a_\text{lo}}_x, L^{a_\text{hi}}_x, L^{b_\text{lo}}_x, L^{b_\text{hi}}_x, L^{c_\text{lo}}_x, L^{c_\text{hi}}_x), \quad x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{binmul} - 1},
\]
inducing six length-$n_\text{binmul}$ constraint arrays $a_\text{lo}, a_\text{hi}, b_\text{lo}, b_\text{hi}, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi} \in \BB_{64}^{n_\text{binmul}}$ as in \S\ref{sec:constraint-arrays}. Each operand pair is read as a GHASH field element via $\gval{\cdot}$: the pair $(a_\text{lo}[x], a_\text{hi}[x])$ encodes $\gval{a_\text{lo}[x], a_\text{hi}[x]} \in \FF{2^{128}}$, and likewise for $b$ and $c$. The prover data $w$ \emph{satisfies} the BinMul constraints if for each $x \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{binmul} - 1}$,
\[
  \gval{a_\text{lo}[x], a_\text{hi}[x]} \cdot \gval{b_\text{lo}[x], b_\text{hi}[x]} = \gval{c_\text{lo}[x], c_\text{hi}[x]},
\]
the multiplication taken in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$.

\section{The Binius64 LIOP}
\label{sec:binius-liop}

This section specifies the Binius64 LIOP: an interactive oracle protocol that, given a constraint system (\S\ref{sec:constraint-system}) and a public statement $u$, verifies that the prover holds a witness $w$ satisfying the constraint system with respect to $u$. The compilation of this LIOP into a SNARK is discussed in \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}.

Throughout, we assume the constraint-system parameters are padded so that $n_\text{words} = 2^{\ell_\text{words}}$, $n_\text{zero} = 2^{\ell_\text{zero}}$, $n_\text{and} = 2^{\ell_\text{and}}$, $n_\text{mul} = 2^{\ell_\text{mul}}$, $n_\text{binmul} = 2^{\ell_\text{binmul}}$, and $n_\text{public} \coloneqq n_\text{const} + n_\text{inout} = 2^{\ell_\text{public}}$ are all powers of two. Two aggregates of the four constraint-index dimensions recur: their maximum
\[
  \ell_\text{cons} \coloneqq \max(\ell_\text{zero}, \ell_\text{and}, \ell_\text{mul}, \ell_\text{binmul}),
\]
the length of the unified constraint point of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}, and the maximum over the three that enter the batched sumcheck of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification},
\[
  \ell_\text{sc} \coloneqq \max(\ell_\text{and}, \ell_\text{mul}, \ell_\text{binmul}),
\]
the round count of the batched sumcheck that produces it. The deterministic zerocheck of \S\ref{sec:rijndael} consumes three constraint-index variables, so we additionally require $\ell_\text{and} \geq 3$. We additionally pad both the witness and the public segment so that $\lambda \mid 64 \cdot n_\text{words}$ and $\lambda \mid 64 \cdot n_\text{public}$; equivalently, $\ell_\text{words} \geq \log_2(\lambda) - 6$ and $\ell_\text{public} \geq \log_2(\lambda) - 6$. With $\lambda = 128$, this means $\ell_\text{words}, \ell_\text{public} \geq 1$. The first condition is needed for the packed-witness construction of \S\ref{sec:ring-switching} and the second for the public input check of \S\ref{sec:public-input-check}. Padding entries outside the randomizable support are taken to be zero (or, for constraint-array lists, the empty list). Where convenient we identify $\BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \cong \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{words} - 1}$ and similarly for the other domains.

\subsection{Protocol Overview}
\label{sec:protocol-overview}

The protocol commits to a single $K$-valued oracle obtained by packing the witness $w$ word-by-word, runs a sequence of sumcheck-based reductions, and finally issues a single linear query on the packed oracle. Concretely, $w$ is viewed as a function $w \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \to \FF{2}$, where $(j, y) \in \BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}$ indexes bit $j$ of word $y$; its multilinear extension is the $6 + \ell_\text{words}$-variate multilinear
\[
  \mle{w}(J_0, \ldots, J_5, Y_0, \ldots, Y_{\ell_\text{words} - 1}) \in K[J, Y]^\mlin.
\]
The prover does not commit to $w$ directly: it instead commits to the packed function $\textsf{pack}_\lambda(w) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}} \to K$, defined in \S\ref{sec:ring-switching}, which encodes $\lambda$ consecutive bits of $w$ as a single $K$-element. The packed witness is the actual LIOP oracle, in keeping with the $K$-valued-oracle convention of \S\ref{sec:liop}.

The LIOP proceeds in nine phases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Packed witness commitment.} The prover sends the packed witness oracle $\pi \coloneqq \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}} \to K$, where $\ell_\text{pack} \coloneqq \ell_\text{words} - (\log_2 \lambda - 6)$ and $\textsf{pack}_\lambda$ is the packing map defined in \S\ref{sec:ring-switching}. (Implementations commit to $\pi$ via the polynomial commitment scheme of \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}, but the LIOP itself treats $\pi$ as an oracle of length $2^{\ell_\text{pack}}$.)
\item \textbf{IntMul reduction (\S\ref{sec:intmul}).} A GKR-based reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{mul}$ IntMul constraints into per-bit evaluation claims on the four multilinear extensions $\mle{a}, \mle{b}, \mle{c}_\text{lo}, \mle{c}_\text{hi}$ of the IntMul constraint arrays, leaving the bit-index free.
\item \textbf{BinMul reduction (\S\ref{sec:binmul}).} A zerocheck-based reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{binmul}$ BinMul constraints into per-bit evaluation claims on the multilinear extensions of the six BinMul constraint arrays $a_\text{lo}, a_\text{hi}, b_\text{lo}, b_\text{hi}, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$, leaving the bit-index free.
\item \textbf{BitAnd reduction (\S\ref{sec:binand}).} A sumcheck-based reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{and}$ BitAnd constraints into evaluation claims on the three oblong-multilinearizations $\widehat{a}, \widehat{b}, \widehat{c}$ of the BitAnd constraint arrays. Its univariate skip samples the shared bit-index oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$ against which the two multiplication reductions' outputs are folded, and its sumcheck carries, batched into it, the re-randomization that moves those outputs onto the unified constraint point $r^\star_x$ (\S\ref{sec:rz-unification}).
\item \textbf{Zero reduction (\S\ref{sec:zero}).} The satisfaction of the $n_\text{zero}$ Zero constraints is transformed into a single evaluation claim on the oblong-multilinearization $\widehat{d}$ of the Zero constraint array, at the unified point the previous phase's sumcheck has just output. The phase carries no prover message and no sumcheck round.
\item \textbf{Shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}).} The evaluation claims produced by the Zero, BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul reductions---by now all stated at the shared oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$ and at the unified constraint point $r^\star_x$---are batched, and a single sumcheck reduces them to one evaluation claim on $\mle{w}$ at a point $(r_j, r_y) \in K^6 \times K^{\ell_\text{words}}$.
\item \textbf{Ring-switching (\S\ref{sec:ring-switching}).} The evaluation claim $\mle{w}(r_j, r_y) = t$ on the $\FF{2}$-valued multilinear $\mle{w}$ is converted into a linear query on the packed $K$-valued oracle $\pi$, via the protocol of Diamond and Posen~\cite{Diamond:2024ab}.
\item \textbf{Public input check (\S\ref{sec:public-input-check}).} A separate linear query on $\pi$ certifies that the packing of the public segment $C \mathbin{\Vert} u$ agrees with the prefix of $\pi$ that contains it. This query is then random-combined with the ring-switching query into a single linear query.
\item \textbf{Witness query.} The verifier issues the combined linear query on $\pi$.
\end{enumerate}

The two multiplication reductions (phases 2 and 3) must precede the BitAnd reduction (phase 4): the BitAnd univariate skip samples the shared bit-index oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$, and soundness requires that the multiplication reductions' per-bit outputs already be fixed when $r_{\widehat{i}}$ is drawn (\S\ref{sec:rz-unification}). Their outputs, once folded against $r_{\widehat{i}}$, are also the input of the re-randomization that phase 4 batches into its own sumcheck, so phase 4 is where the two axes are unified. Phases 2 and 3 are mutually independent and may run in either order. Phase 5 evaluates at the point produced by that sumcheck and so must follow phase 4; since it carries no message, this is a matter of bookkeeping rather than of interaction.

\subsection{Shift Indicator Polynomials}
\label{sec:shift-indicators}

Each of the eight shift operations in $\textsf{ShiftOps}$ (\S\ref{sec:shifted-indices}) acts on $\BB_{64}$ at the bit level. To verify claims about shifted prover words via sumcheck, we lift each shift operation to a multilinear polynomial identity. The key tool is the \emph{shift indicator polynomial}, a multilinear in $18$ variables whose values on $\BB_6^3$ describe which bit of the original word ends up in which bit of the shifted word. Following Diamond and Posen~\cite[\S 4.3]{Diamond:2025aa}, the existence of such an identity reduces shifted-witness evaluation claims to single witness evaluation claims, provided the shift indicator admits an efficient evaluation algorithm.

Recall (\S\ref{sec:protocol-overview}) that the prover data $w$ is viewed as a function $w \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \to \FF{2}$ with multilinear extension $\mle{w}(J, Y)$. For each $\text{op} \in \textsf{ShiftOps}$, the \emph{shifted witness function} is
\[
  \textsf{shift}_\text{op}(w) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \times \BB_6 \to \BB_{64}, \quad \textsf{shift}_\text{op}(w)(y, s) \coloneqq \text{op}(w[y], s).
\]
Composing with bit extraction gives a function on $\BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \times \BB_6$ sending $(i, y, s)$ to the $i$-th bit of $\text{op}(w[y], s)$; we write $\mle{\textsf{shift}}_\text{op}(\mle{w})(I, Y, S)$ for its multilinear extension.

For each $\text{op}$ we define the \emph{shift indicator} function $\textsf{shift-ind}_\text{op} \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_6 \times \BB_6 \to \FF{2}$ by
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}_\text{op}(i, j, s) \coloneqq \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if the $i$-th bit of $\text{op}(v, s)$ equals the $j$-th bit of $v$ for every $v \in \BB_{64}$,} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
\]
For example, $\textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{sll}(30, 20, 10) = 1$ because $\textsf{sll}(v, 10)_{30} = v_{20}$ for all $v$, whereas $\textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{sll}(9, j, 10) = 0$ for all $j$ because $\textsf{sll}(v, 10)_9 = 0$ for all $v$. We write $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\text{op}(I, J, S)$ for the corresponding multilinear extension, an $18$-variate multilinear with $\FF{2}$-coefficients.

The defining identity of the shift indicator polynomial is, for each $\text{op}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mle{\textsf{shift}}_\text{op}(\mle{w})(I, Y, S) = \sum_{j \in \BB_6} \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\text{op}(I, j, S) \cdot \mle{w}(j, Y).
  \label{eq:shift-indicator-identity}
\end{equation}
To verify \eqref{eq:shift-indicator-identity}, it suffices to check it on the cube: fix $i, s \in \BB_6$ and $y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}$. Either some unique $j^* \in \BB_6$ has $\textsf{shift-ind}_\text{op}(i, j^*, s) = 1$, in which case the right-hand side reduces to $\mle{w}(j^*, y) = w[y]_{j^*} = \text{op}(w[y], s)_i$; or no such $j^*$ exists, in which case $\text{op}(w[y], s)_i = 0$ by definition of $\text{op}$ on zero-fill bits, and the right-hand side is zero.

Identity \eqref{eq:shift-indicator-identity} enables the standard reduction: given an evaluation claim on $\mle{\textsf{shift}}_\text{op}(\mle{w})$ at some point $(r_i, r_y, r_s) \in K^{6 + \ell_\text{words} + 6}$, a $6$-round sumcheck binds the index $j$, leaving evaluation claims on $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\text{op}(r_i, r_j, r_s)$ (which the verifier evaluates directly) and on $\mle{w}(r_j, r_y)$ (a single query to the witness oracle).

\paragraph{Composing two shifts.}
The shift indicators compose, which is what makes the doubly shifted value indices of \S\ref{sec:shifted-indices} tractable. The key structural fact, already used above, is that for each $(i, s)$ there is \emph{at most one} $j \in \BB_6$ with $\textsf{shift-ind}_\text{op}(i, j, s) = 1$: output bit $i$ of $\text{op}(v, s)$ is either a single fixed input bit of $v$ or the constant zero. This holds for all eight operations, including $\textsf{sra}$, where the vacated high positions all take the single input bit $j = 63$, and including the $32$-bit variants, where the property holds within each half. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \text{op}_2\bigl( \text{op}_1(v, s_1), s_2 \bigr)_i = \sum_{k \in \BB_6} \sum_{j \in \BB_6} \textsf{shift-ind}_{\text{op}_2}(i, k, s_2) \cdot \textsf{shift-ind}_{\text{op}_1}(k, j, s_1) \cdot v_j
  \label{eq:composite-indicator}
\end{equation}
for every $v \in \BB_{64}$: the inner factor selects the input bit $j$ that supplies bit $k$ of the intermediate word $\text{op}_1(v, s_1)$, and the outer factor selects the bit $k$ of that intermediate word which output bit $i$ reproduces. The index $k$ ranges over the bit positions of the intermediate word, which is never materialized as a witness value; it exists only as a summation index, and the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}) carries it as one more sumcheck variable.

\paragraph{Interpolation over the operations.}
The shift reduction treats the shift operation as one more hypercube coordinate, so we assemble the eight indicators into a single polynomial. Using the indexing $o \mapsto \text{op}(o)$ of $\textsf{ShiftOps}$ by $\BB_3$ (\S\ref{sec:shifted-indices}), define the \emph{interpolated shift indicator} $\textsf{shift-ind} \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_6 \times \BB_6 \times \BB_3 \to \FF{2}$ by
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}(i, j, s, o) \coloneqq \textsf{shift-ind}_{\text{op}(o)}(i, j, s),
\]
and write $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(I, J, S, O)$ for its multilinear extension, a $21$-variate multilinear with $\FF{2}$-coefficients. Expanding the multilinear extension along its last three variables (\S\ref{sec:notation}) gives
\begin{equation}
  \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(I, J, S, O) = \sum_{o \in \BB_3} \mle{\eq}_3(O, o) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\text{op}(o)}(I, J, S),
  \label{eq:shift-indicator-interpolation}
\end{equation}
since both sides are multilinear and agree on $\BB_{21}$.

\paragraph{Succinctness.}
The shift indicator $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\text{op}$ is \emph{succinct} for each of the eight $\text{op} \in \textsf{ShiftOps}$: any point of $K^{18}$ can be evaluated in $O(1)$ field operations (concretely, at most $84$ multiplications in $K$ plus a $6$-way product). The explicit arithmetizations, with auxiliary functions $\mathsf{s}_k, \mathsf{s}'_k, \mathsf{a}_k$ for the recursive constructions of the $64$-bit and $32$-bit-parallel variants, are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:shift-arithmetizations}. The interpolated indicator $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}$ is succinct in turn: by \eqref{eq:shift-indicator-interpolation}, a point of $K^{21}$ costs the eight per-op evaluations plus the $O(1)$ weights $\textsf{TensorExpand}_3(O)$.

\subsection{The BitAnd Reduction}
\label{sec:binand}

The BitAnd reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{and}$ BitAnd constraints (\S\ref{sec:bitand-constraints}) into evaluation claims on the oblong-multilinearizations of the BitAnd constraint arrays. It is a univariate-skip variant of the sumcheck-based zerocheck \cite{Gruen:2024aa}, in which the first sumcheck round is replaced by a univariate polynomial send. To minimize prover work, we also use deterministic challenges sampled from $\FF{2^8}$ in the first three sumcheck rounds, adapting an idea of Dao and Thaler~\cite{eprint:2024/1038}.

Recall that satisfaction of the BitAnd constraints is the claim that, for each $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$, $a[x] \land b[x] = c[x]$, where the $64$-bit constraint arrays $a, b, c$ are defined in \S\ref{sec:bitand-constraints} and the bitwise AND is just per-bit field multiplication. Equivalently, the polynomial
\[
  f(I, X) \coloneqq \mle{a}(I, X) \cdot \mle{b}(I, X) - \mle{c}(I, X)
\]
on $\BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$ vanishes identically. We work with the oblong-multilinearizations of the constraint arrays: writing $\widehat{a}(\widehat{I}, X), \widehat{b}(\widehat{I}, X), \widehat{c}(\widehat{I}, X)$ for the oblong-multilinearizations (\S\ref{sec:oblong}) with $k = 6$, so each is of degree $< 64$ in $\widehat{I}$ and multilinear in $X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{and} - 1}$, the constraint reduces to the vanishing of
\[
  \widehat{f}(\widehat{I}, X) \coloneqq \widehat{a}(\widehat{I}, X) \cdot \widehat{b}(\widehat{I}, X) - \widehat{c}(\widehat{I}, X)
\]
on $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$.

\subsubsection{The Rijndael Field and Deterministic Zerocheck}
\label{sec:rijndael}

For the prover algorithm to run mostly over a small field, we fix the Rijndael field $\FF{2^8} \cong \FF{2}[X] / (X^8 + X^4 + X^3 + X + 1)$ (the AES base field) and embed it into $K$. We assume $D \subset \FF{2^8}$ (a $6$-dimensional $\FF{2}$-subspace of the $8$-dimensional $\FF{2}$-space $\FF{2^8}$); using the fixed embedding $\iota \colon \FF{2^8} \hookrightarrow K$ from \S\ref{sec:binary-fields}, this gives the chain $D \subset \FF{2^8} \subset K$.

\paragraph{Tensor basis.}
We select three elements $\rho_0, \rho_1, \rho_2 \in \FF{2^8}$ such that
\[
  \bigl( \mle{\eq}_3(\rho_0, \rho_1, \rho_2, u_0, u_1, u_2) \bigr)_{u \in \BB_3}
\]
is an $\FF{2}$-basis of $\FF{2^8}$. The choices $\rho_0 \coloneqq X$, $\rho_1 \coloneqq X^2$, and $\rho_2 \coloneqq X^4$ work: their subproducts are $1, X, X^2, \ldots, X^7$, which form an $\FF{2}$-basis of $\FF{2^8}$, so the tensor expansion $\textsf{TensorExpand}_3(\rho_0, \rho_1, \rho_2)$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}) differs from this monomial list by an invertible $\FF{2}$-linear map. We write $\sigma_j \coloneqq \iota(\rho_j) \in K$ for $j = 0, 1, 2$; since $\iota$ is an $\FF{2}$-linear injection that commutes with $\mle{\eq}$ (which has $\FF{2}$-coefficients), $\bigl( \mle{\eq}_3(\sigma, u) \bigr)_{u \in \BB_3}$ is also an $\FF{2}$-linearly independent list in $K$.

\paragraph{Deterministic zerocheck.}
The standard zerocheck on an $\FF{2}$-valued polynomial $t(X)$ on $\BB_\ell$ that is claimed to vanish samples $r_x \gets K^\ell$ and sumchecks $0 \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_\ell} t(x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_\ell(r_x, x)$, with soundness error $\ell / |K|$. The \emph{deterministic-zerocheck} variant samples only $\ell - 3$ challenges and fixes the first three to the deterministic constants $\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_2$:
\[
  r_x \coloneqq (\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \overline{r}_{x, 0}, \ldots, \overline{r}_{x, \ell - 4}), \qquad \overline{r}_x \gets K^{\ell - 3}.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
  \sum_{x \in \BB_\ell} t(x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_\ell(r_x, x) = \sum_{v \in \BB_{\ell - 3}} \overline{t}(v) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell - 3}(\overline{r}_x, v),
\]
where
\[
  \overline{t}(X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell - 4}) \coloneqq \sum_{u \in \BB_3} t(u_0, u_1, u_2, X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell - 4}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_3(\sigma, u).
\]
Since $t$ is $\FF{2}$-valued on $\BB_\ell$ and $\bigl( \mle{\eq}_3(\sigma, u) \bigr)_{u \in \BB_3}$ is $\FF{2}$-linearly independent, $t$ vanishes on $\BB_\ell$ iff $\overline{t}$ vanishes on $\BB_{\ell - 3}$, and the standard zerocheck soundness applied to $\overline{t}$ gives soundness error $(\ell - 3) / |K|$.

\subsubsection{The Univariate-Skip Protocol}
\label{sec:univariate-skip}

The BitAnd reduction runs a deterministic-zerocheck on $\widehat{f}(\widehat{I}, X)$ over $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$, but with the inner sum over $\widehat{i} \in D$ handled by a univariate polynomial send (Gruen~\cite[\S 5.1]{Gruen:2024aa}) rather than additional sumcheck rounds:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples $\overline{r}_x \gets K^{\ell_\text{and} - 3}$ and sends it to the prover. Both parties set $r_x \coloneqq (\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \overline{r}_{x, 0}, \ldots, \overline{r}_{x, \ell_\text{and} - 4})$.
\item The prover sends the univariate polynomial
  \[
    g(\widehat{I}) \coloneqq \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}} \widehat{f}(\widehat{I}, x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, x) \in K[\widehat{I}],
  \]
  by sending its evaluations on $64$ points outside $D$. The polynomial has degree at most $2 \cdot (2^6 - 1) = 126$, and these $64$ values together with the $64$ prescribed zeros on $D$ determine $g$ uniquely.
\item The verifier checks that $g$ vanishes on $D$ (using the prescribed zeros). It then samples $r_{\widehat{i}} \gets K$ and sends it to the prover. The verifier folds the multiplication reductions' per-bit outputs against $r_{\widehat{i}}$ into claims on their operand columns (\S\ref{sec:rz-unification}) and samples one further challenge $\theta \gets K$ to batch those claims into the sumcheck below.
\item The parties run a sumcheck on the claim
  \[
    g(r_{\widehat{i}}) \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}} \widehat{f}(r_{\widehat{i}}, x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, x),
  \]
  batched with the operand columns' re-randomization claims (\S\ref{sec:rz-unification}), which brings the round count to $\ell_\text{sc}$ and the round polynomials to individual degree $3$. At the end, the sumcheck produces the constraint point $r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}$---the low $\ell_\text{and}$ coordinates of the unified point $r^\star_x$ assembled in \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}---and a combined target, of which the BitAnd share is the assertion that $\widehat{f}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}})$ equals its contribution $s$.
\item The prover sends scalars $\alpha_a, \alpha_b, \alpha_c \in K$, claimed to equal $\widehat{a}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}), \widehat{b}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}), \widehat{c}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}})$ respectively. The verifier evaluates $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}})$ on its own and forms
  \[
    s \stackrel{?}{=} \bigl( \alpha_a \cdot \alpha_b - \alpha_c \bigr) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}),
  \]
  the closing check were this sumcheck run alone; in the full LIOP the right-hand side is the BitAnd term of the batched closing check of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}, scaled there by the padding constant $c_\text{and}$.
\end{enumerate}

The protocol's output is the triple of evaluation claims $\bigl( \widehat{a}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}) = \alpha_a, \widehat{b}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}) = \alpha_b, \widehat{c}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}}) = \alpha_c \bigr)$, to be discharged by the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}), together with the operand-column claims re-randomized inside its sumcheck.

\paragraph{Soundness.}
A dishonest prover violating $a[x] \land b[x] = c[x]$ on some constraint induces a nonzero $\widehat{f}$ on $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$. By the deterministic-zerocheck analysis of \S\ref{sec:rijndael}, there exists $\widehat{i}^* \in D$ such that $\overline{g}(\widehat{i}^*) \neq 0$ except with probability $(\ell_\text{and} - 3) / |K|$ over $\overline{r}_x$, where $\overline{g}(\widehat{I}) \coloneqq \sum_x \widehat{f}(\widehat{I}, x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, x)$ is the polynomial the honest prover would send. Conditioned on this, the verifier's check that $g$ vanishes on $D$ forces $g \neq \overline{g}$ as polynomials, so by univariate Schwartz--Zippel, $g(r_{\widehat{i}}) = \overline{g}(r_{\widehat{i}})$ except with probability $2 \cdot (2^6 - 1) / |K|$ over $r_{\widehat{i}}$. The sumcheck on the (now-wrong) claim adds another $3 \ell_\text{sc} / |K|$: its summand $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, X) \cdot \widehat{f}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X)$ has individual degree $3$, and the batch of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} it runs inside adds no degree and extends it to $\ell_\text{sc}$ rounds. Soundness of the final triple-claim follows by univariate Schwartz--Zippel on $\alpha_a, \alpha_b, \alpha_c$, contributing $O(1) / |K|$. Total soundness error is $O(\ell_\text{sc}) / |K|$; the batched companions are accounted for separately in \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}.

\subsubsection{Prover Algorithm}
\label{sec:rijndael-zerocheck}

The dominant cost is the univariate send: the prover must evaluate $g(\widehat{I})$ at $64$ points outside $D$. We sketch the algorithm, called the \emph{Rijndael zerocheck}, which performs most of the inner work entirely in $\FF{2^8}$ and embeds into $K$ only once every $8$ cube points. We refine the parameter $D$: we fix a $7$-dimensional $\FF{2}$-subspace $D' \supset D$ with $D' \subset \FF{2^8}$, so $|D' \setminus D| = 64$. The prover evaluates $g$ on $D' \setminus D$.

\paragraph{Lookup-based extrapolation.}
The core primitive is a precomputed map $\textsf{Extrap} \colon \FF{2}^{64} \to \FF{2^8}^{64}$ that, on input a bit-vector $p = (p_0, \ldots, p_{63})$ viewed as the values on $D$ of a univariate polynomial $P \in K[\widehat{I}]$ of degree $< 64$, returns $\bigl( \iota^{-1}(P(\widehat{i})) \bigr)_{\widehat{i} \in D' \setminus D}$ (which lies in $\FF{2^8}^{64}$ because $D' \subset \FF{2^8}$ and $P$ is $\FF{2}$-valued on $D$). Since $\textsf{Extrap}$ is $\FF{2}$-linear, it can be implemented by splitting the $64$-bit input into eight $8$-bit chunks, precomputing for each chunk a length-$256$ table of $\textsf{Extrap}$ outputs on inputs nonzero on that chunk alone, and combining via $8$ table lookups and $7$ length-$64$ byte-wise XORs per invocation, following the lookup-table extrapolation technique of Hu et al.~\cite{cryptoeprint:2025/717}. Total table size is $8 \cdot 256 \cdot 64$ bytes $= 128$\,KiB.

\paragraph{Inner-outer structure.}
The prover tensor-expands $\overline{r}_x$ once into the table $\textsf{TensorExpand}_{\ell_\text{and} - 3}(\overline{r}_x) = \bigl( \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and} - 3}(\overline{r}_x, v) \bigr)_{v \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and} - 3}}$. Then it iterates over outer index $v \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and} - 3}$, and for each $v$ accumulates a length-$64$ vector $\textsf{temp}$ in $\FF{2^8}$ by iterating $u \in \BB_3$ and computing, for $x \coloneqq u \mathbin{\Vert} v$ and each $\widehat{i} \in D' \setminus D$,
\[
  \textsf{temp}[\widehat{i}] \mathrel{+}= \bigl( \textsf{Extrap}(a[x])[\widehat{i}] \cdot \textsf{Extrap}(b[x])[\widehat{i}] - \textsf{Extrap}(c[x])[\widehat{i}] \bigr) \cdot \mle{\eq}_3(\rho, u).
\]
At the end of the inner loop, the entries of $\textsf{temp}$ are inverse-embeddings of $\sum_{u \in \BB_3} \widehat{f}(\widehat{i}, u \mathbin{\Vert} v) \cdot \mle{\eq}_3(\sigma, u)$. The prover then embeds via $\iota$, scales by $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and} - 3}(\overline{r}_x, v)$, and accumulates into a length-$64$ outer accumulator over $K$. The final accumulator contains $\bigl( g(\widehat{i}) \bigr)_{\widehat{i} \in D' \setminus D}$. The asymptotic cost is dominated by the inner $\FF{2^8}$ work, with $64$ $\FF{2^8} \to K$ embeddings and $K$-multiplications per $8$ cube points. Further speedups are available via lookup-table-based additive-NTT techniques.

\paragraph{Preparing the sumcheck.}
For the sumcheck in step 4 of \S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}, the prover needs the value tables of $\widehat{a}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X), \widehat{b}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X), \widehat{c}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X)$ on $\BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$. By the partial-specialization formula of \S\ref{sec:oblong}, each table is obtained by precomputing the $64$ Lagrange weights $\bigl( \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \bigr)_{i \in \BB_6}$ once and then, for each constraint $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{and}}$, taking the $\FF{2}$-coefficient subset sum of these weights with bits drawn from $a[x]$, $b[x]$, $c[x]$ respectively. The operand-column claims batched into this sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:rz-unification}) need one further table per multiplication reduction, the $\theta$-weighted sum of that reduction's operand columns at $r_{\widehat{i}}$, which is formed the same way---one subset sum per column, over the same weights scaled by the column's batching coefficient---together with the tensor expansions of the two reductions' constraint points. Each round then computes the three terms' factored round polynomials over their own hypercubes and combines them as in \S\ref{sec:batching-mle-checks}, so batching costs the prover only the two extra terms, not a higher-degree pass over the BitAnd tables.

\subsection{The Zero Reduction}
\label{sec:zero}

The Zero reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{zero}$ Zero constraints (\S\ref{sec:zero-constraints}) into a single evaluation claim on the oblong-multilinearization $\widehat{d}$ of the Zero constraint array. Alone among the four constraint reductions it costs nothing: it adds no prover message and no sumcheck round, and it draws no challenges of its own beyond an occasional extension of the unified constraint point. Its whole content is the observation that a Zero constraint is \emph{linear}, so that the polynomial whose vanishing is at stake is $\widehat{d}$ itself rather than a product of witness multilinears. A multilinear that vanishes on the cube is the zero polynomial; a single evaluation at a point the prover could not predict therefore certifies the constraint outright, with no sumcheck needed to get there. The reduction's only cost is one further constraint array in the batch consumed by the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}).

Write $d[i, x] \in \FF{2}$ for bit $i$ of the word $d[x]$, let $\mle{d}(I, X)$ be the multilinear extension of $d$ on $\BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{zero}}$, and let $\widehat{d}(\widehat{I}, X)$ be its oblong-multilinearization (\S\ref{sec:oblong}) with $k = 6$, of degree $< 64$ in $\widehat{I}$ and multilinear in $X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{zero} - 1}$. Satisfaction of the Zero constraints is exactly the claim that $\widehat{d}$ vanishes on $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{zero}}$, and since $\widehat{d}$ is determined by its values there, this is in turn equivalent to $\widehat{d}$ being the zero polynomial.

\paragraph{The evaluation point.}
The reduction runs directly after the BitAnd reduction (\S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}) and evaluates $\widehat{d}$ at the point that reduction has just produced: the univariate-skip challenge $r_{\widehat{i}} \in K$ of step 3, and the low $\ell_\text{zero}$ coordinates $r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}}$ of the unified constraint point $r^\star_x \in K^{\ell_\text{cons}}$ assembled in \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}. The Zero array is no summand of that sumcheck---it only reads the challenges---and those coordinates are the last $\ell_\text{zero}$ of them, except that when the Zero array is the longest of the four---so that $\ell_\text{zero}$ exceeds the sumcheck's round count $\ell_\text{sc}$---the verifier samples the shortfall $\overline{r}_d \gets K^{\ell_\text{zero} - \ell_\text{sc}}$ itself and both parties place it in the high coordinates. Every coordinate of $r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}}$ is uniform on $K$ and independent of the others: those inherited from the sumcheck are its per-round verifier challenges, and $\overline{r}_d$ is freshly sampled.

The reduction's output is the single evaluation claim
\[
  \widehat{d}\bigl( r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}} \bigr) = 0,
\]
which joins the other reductions' outputs in the batch of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}. It is already stated at the shared oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$ and at the unified constraint point, so neither the fold nor the re-randomization of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} applies to it. The prover sends nothing: the claimed value is the constant $0$, and it is the shift reduction, running against the committed witness, that certifies it.

\paragraph{Soundness.}
Suppose the prover data violates some Zero constraint, so that $d[i^*, x^*] = 1$ for some $i^* \in \BB_6$ and $x^* \in \BB_{\ell_\text{zero}}$. Then $\widehat{d}$ is not the zero polynomial: it takes the value $d[i^*, x^*] = 1$ at the cube point $(\widehat{i^*}, x^*) \in D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{zero}}$.

Moreover $\widehat{d}$ is fixed before any challenge is drawn. The array $d$ is a function of the prover data $w$, which the packed-witness oracle of phase 1 pins down before the first challenge is sampled, so a prover cannot adapt $d$ to the point at which it will be evaluated. As $\widehat{d}$ has degree $< 64$ in $\widehat{I}$ and degree $\leq 1$ in each of $X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{zero} - 1}$, and $\bigl( r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}} \bigr)$ is uniform on $K^{1 + \ell_\text{zero}}$, Schwartz--Zippel gives
\[
  \Pr\bigl[ \widehat{d}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}}) = 0 \bigr] \leq \frac{63 + \ell_\text{zero}}{|K|}.
\]
Conditioned on $\widehat{d}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}}) \neq 0$, the random batching of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} preserves a false claim except with probability $O(1) / |K|$, and the shift reduction then rejects it. Total soundness error is $O(\ell_\text{zero}) / |K|$.

\paragraph{On reusing the unified point.}
Sharing $\bigl( r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}} \bigr)$ with the other three reductions is sound despite the resulting correlation. The bound above is a statement about a bad event for the Zero constraints, and it appeals only to the marginal distribution of that point, which reuse leaves untouched; the analyses of \S\ref{sec:univariate-skip} and \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} do the same for their own bad events, and none conditions on another. Unsatisfiable prover data violates a constraint of some class, and the reduction for that class catches it with the probability computed for it in isolation. What all these analyses do require is that every challenge be drawn after $w$ is committed, which the phase ordering of \S\ref{sec:protocol-overview} guarantees.

The batched sumcheck's \emph{output} point is what makes the argument this short. The BitAnd reduction's input challenge $r_x$ would serve too, but it opens with the three deterministic coordinates $\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_2$ of \S\ref{sec:rijndael}; those axes carry no randomness, and bounding the error would first require absorbing them by the $\FF{2}$-independence argument of that section---legitimate here, since a constraint array is an array of \emph{bits} and $\widehat{d}$ is therefore $\FF{2}$-valued on $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{zero}}$, but an extra step, and one that would force $\ell_\text{zero} \geq 3$. The output point is uniform on all its coordinates, so plain Schwartz--Zippel suffices.

\subsection{The IntMul Reduction}
\label{sec:intmul}

The IntMul reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{mul}$ IntMul constraints (\S\ref{sec:intmul-constraints}) into per-bit evaluation claims on the multilinear extensions $\mle{a}, \mle{b}, \mle{c}_\text{lo}, \mle{c}_\text{hi}$ of the IntMul constraint arrays---the $64$ values $\mle{a}(r_x, i), \ldots$ at every bit-position $i \in \BB_6$ of a shared constraint point $r_x \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$. Unlike the BitAnd reduction, which exploits that bitwise AND is just per-bit field multiplication, IntMul concerns an integer-arithmetic relation that has no direct expression in $K$; we instead encode it as a multiplicative-group identity in $K^*$ and reduce that identity via a tree of small-degree sumchecks.

\subsubsection{Multiplying in the Exponent}
\label{sec:multiplying-in-exponent}

Fix a generator $g \in K^*$, so $g$ has order $2^\lambda - 1$. Recall the integer encoding $\intval{a}[x] = \sum_{i = 0}^{63} 2^i \cdot a[i, x]$ of a constraint word (\S\ref{sec:intmul-constraints}). For each $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}$, the integer relation $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] = 2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]$ can be converted into the multiplicative-group identity
\begin{equation}
  \bigl( g^{\intval{a}[x]} \bigr)^{\intval{b}[x]} \stackrel{?}{=} \bigl( g^{2^{64}} \bigr)^{\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]} \cdot g^{\intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]},
  \label{eq:exp-identity}
\end{equation}
both sides taken in $K^*$. By the basic property of cyclic groups, \eqref{eq:exp-identity} holds iff the integer relation holds modulo the order $2^\lambda - 1$.

\paragraph{Wraparound.}
The order of $K^*$ is $2^\lambda - 1$. Assuming $\lambda \geq 128$, the integer quantities in \eqref{eq:exp-identity} are bounded by
\[
  \intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] \leq (2^{64} - 1)^2 < 2^{128} - 1, \qquad 2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] \leq 2^{128} - 1,
\]
where the right-hand bound is attained exactly when $\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] = \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] = 2^{64} - 1$. Since $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] < 2^{128} - 1 \leq 2^\lambda - 1$, the left-hand side never wraps modulo the group order.

When $\lambda > 128$ strictly, the right-hand side is likewise strictly below the order, $2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] \leq 2^{128} - 1 < 2^\lambda - 1$, so neither side overflows and \eqref{eq:exp-identity} holds if and only if the integer relation does; no further condition is required.

When $\lambda = 128$, the system default, the order is exactly $2^{128} - 1$, which the right-hand side can equal. A dishonest prover could exploit this by setting $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] = 0$ and $\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] = \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] = 2^{64} - 1$, since then both sides of \eqref{eq:exp-identity} equal $1 \in K^*$ even though the integer relation fails. In this case $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x]$ is even while $2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]$ is odd, so the spurious case is ruled out by additionally requiring
\begin{equation}
  \intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] \equiv \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] \pmod 2,
  \label{eq:lsb-parity}
\end{equation}
a condition on a single bit per constraint. Together, \eqref{eq:exp-identity} and \eqref{eq:lsb-parity} are then the two conditions whose simultaneous satisfaction is equivalent to the IntMul relation $\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x] = 2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x] + \intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]$: the multiplicative-group identity captures it modulo $2^\lambda - 1$, and the parity condition pins down the one residue the cyclic group cannot see.

\subsubsection{Exponentiation Subprotocols}
\label{sec:exp-subprotocols}

Identity \eqref{eq:exp-identity} is verified by composing two generic exponentiation subprotocols. Throughout, fix the generator $g \in K^*$ and let $z \in \FF{2}^{\BB_{\ell_\text{mul}} \times \BB_k}$ be a bit vector, with $k = 6$ and entries $z[x, i] \in \FF{2}$, written with the constraint index first and the bit position last---the transpose of the bit indexing of \S\ref{sec:intmul-constraints}, to which the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}) reverts---so that the bit index occupies the high variables, matching the layer structure of the product trees below and the implementation's data layout. We index the $2^k = 64$ bit positions by $i \in \set{0, \ldots, 2^k - 1}$, identified with the hypercube $\BB_k$ via binary representation. Its multilinear extension $\mle{z}(X, I) \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{mul} - 1}, I_0, \ldots, I_{k - 1}]^\mlin$ has $\mle{z}(X, i)$ for the multilinear section at the $i$-th hypercube point, with $\mle{\eq}_k(\cdot, i)$ the corresponding Lagrange basis function. The integer encoded at each constraint index is $\intval{z}[x] = \sum_{i = 0}^{2^k - 1} 2^i \cdot z[x, i]$, with $\intval{\cdot}$ as in \S\ref{sec:intmul-constraints}.

\paragraph{The two operators.}
The \emph{fixed-base} operator raises the public generator to the encoded exponents,
\[
  \textsf{gen-exp}(z)[x] \coloneqq g^{\intval{z}[x]} \in K^*, \qquad x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}},
\]
while the \emph{variable-base} operator raises a multilinear base $\mle{f}(X) \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{mul} - 1}]^\mlin$, whose cube values $f[x] \coloneqq \mle{f}(x)$ all lie in $K^*$, to the same exponents,
\[
  \textsf{var-exp}(f, z)[x] \coloneqq f[x]^{\intval{z}[x]} \in K^*, \qquad x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}.
\]
We denote by $\mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(z)$ and $\mle{\textsf{var-exp}}(f, z)$ their multilinear extensions. The fixed-base subprotocol (\S\ref{sec:fixed-base-exp}) reduces an evaluation claim on $\mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(z)$ to the $2^k$ per-bit evaluation claims $\mle{z}(r'_x, i)$; the variable-base subprotocol (\S\ref{sec:var-base-exp}) reduces an evaluation claim on $\mle{\textsf{var-exp}}(f, z)$ to one evaluation claim on $\mle{f}$ together with the $2^k$ per-bit claims $\mle{z}(r'_x, i)$. Both run the same GKR product tree that splits the exponent into its $2^k$ bits, differing only in how the product-tree leaves are discharged into the per-bit claims.

\paragraph{The product tree.}
Define the \emph{selector} $\sel(s, v) \coloneqq 1 + (v - 1) s$, which returns $v$ when the bit $s = 1$ and $1$ when $s = 0$. Collect the $2^k$ leaves into a vector $L \in K^{\BB_{\ell_\text{mul}} \times \BB_k}$ with entries
\[
  L[x, i] \coloneqq \bigl( f[x]^{2^i} \bigr)^{z[x, i]} = \sel\bigl( z[x, i],\, f[x]^{2^i} \bigr), \qquad x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}, \quad i \in \BB_k,
\]
where the second equality uses that $z[x, i] \in \set{0, 1}$. The operator output is the product of the leaves over the bit index,
\[
  \textsf{var-exp}(f, z)[x] = \prod_{i \in \BB_k} L[x, i]
\]
(for the fixed-base operator, $f[x] \equiv g$ and the product is $\textsf{gen-exp}(z)[x]$). We evaluate this product by a balanced binary tree of depth $k$, whose layers are vectors $L^{(j)} \in K^{\BB_{\ell_\text{mul}} \times \BB_{k - j}}$, for $j = 0, \ldots, k$, with leaf layer $L^{(0)} \coloneqq L$ and each node the product of its two children,
\[
  L^{(j)}[x, i] = L^{(j - 1)}[x, (i, 0)] \cdot L^{(j - 1)}[x, (i, 1)], \qquad x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}, \quad i \in \BB_{k - j},
\]
the trailing bit selecting the child; the root is $L^{(k)} = \textsf{var-exp}(f, z)$. Write $\mle{L}^{(j)}$ for the multilinear extension of the layer vector $L^{(j)}$.

Starting from the root claim $s^{(k)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}^{(k)}(r^{(k)})$ (the root layer has no node index, so we write $r_x^{(k)} \coloneqq r^{(k)}$), the parties run the binary-tree GKR grand-product argument~\cite{Thaler:2013aa} for $j = k, k - 1, \ldots, 1$. At layer $j$ the parties hold a claim $\mle{L}^{(j)}(r_x^{(j)}, \rho^{(j)})$ at an $x$-point $r_x^{(j)} \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$ and a folded node-index point $\rho^{(j)} \in K^{k - j}$. Expanding the product relation,
\[
  \mle{L}^{(j)}(r_x^{(j)}, \rho^{(j)}) = \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \sum_{i \in \BB_{k - j}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}(r_x^{(j)}, x) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{k - j}(\rho^{(j)}, i) \cdot \mle{L}^{(j - 1)}(x, i, 0) \cdot \mle{L}^{(j - 1)}(x, i, 1),
\]
the parties run a $\bigl( \ell_\text{mul} + (k - j) \bigr)$-round MLE-check, whose composition polynomials have individual degree $\leq 2$. It produces the two child evaluations $\mle{L}^{(j - 1)}(r_x^{(j - 1)}, \rho', 0)$ and $\mle{L}^{(j - 1)}(r_x^{(j - 1)}, \rho', 1)$, which a line reduction~\cite{Goldwasser:2015aa} collapses into the single layer-$(j - 1)$ claim $\mle{L}^{(j - 1)}(r_x^{(j - 1)}, \rho^{(j - 1)})$ with $\rho^{(j - 1)} \in K^{k - j + 1}$, the line challenge appended as the new trailing node coordinate. After $k$ layers the parties hold a single leaf claim
\[
  s^{(0)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}(r_x, \rho), \qquad (r_x, \rho) \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}} \times K^k,
\]
at the final $x$-point $r_x \coloneqq r_x^{(0)}$ and fully folded node-index $\rho \coloneqq \rho^{(0)}$. Soundness error is the standard GKR sum over layers, $O\bigl( k(k + \ell_\text{mul}) \bigr) / |K|$.

\paragraph{Leaf extrapolation.}
Each subprotocol discharges the leaves one bit at a time, re-exposing them from the single folded claim $s^{(0)}$. In the general case---when the base is a witness value, as in the variable-base subprotocol---the prover sends the $2^k = 64$ leaf evaluations
\[
  s^{(0)}_i \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}(r_x, i), \qquad i \in \BB_k,
\]
and the verifier checks that they interpolate to the folded claim,
\[
  \sum_{i \in \BB_k} \mle{\eq}_k(\rho, i) \cdot s^{(0)}_i \stackrel{?}{=} s^{(0)} = \mle{L}(r_x, \rho).
\]
The $2^k$ per-bit claims, all at the common $x$-point $r_x$, are the input to the leaf discharge. The interpolation check binds them to the GKR-verified product value $s^{(0)}$; a prover that sends inconsistent values is caught downstream, where each subprotocol reduces the $s^{(0)}_i$ against the committed witness with fresh randomness. When the base is public (the fixed-base subprotocol, \S\ref{sec:fixed-base-exp}), the prover instead re-exposes the exponent bits and the verifier reconstructs the leaves itself, folding this step into the same interpolation check.

\subsubsection{Fixed-Base Exponentiation}
\label{sec:fixed-base-exp}

The fixed-base subprotocol reduces a claim $s^{(k)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(z)(r^{(k)})$ to the $2^k$ per-bit claims on $\mle{z}$. With the constant base $f[x] \equiv g$, the leaf vector is affine in the exponent bit, so its multilinear extension is already linear,
\[
  \mle{L}(X, i) = \sel\bigl( \mle{z}(X, i),\, g^{2^i} \bigr) = 1 + (g^{2^i} - 1) \cdot \mle{z}(X, i),
\]
since $g^{2^i}$ is constant and $\mle{z}(X, i)$ is multilinear in $X$.

The parties run the product-tree GKR loop (\S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}) on the root claim $s^{(k)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(z)(r^{(k)})$, folding to the single leaf claim $s^{(0)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}(r_x, \rho)$ at the common point $r'_x \coloneqq r_x$. Because the powers $g^{2^i}$ are public, the verifier can evaluate the leaf selector itself, so the prover re-exposes the exponent rather than the leaves: it sends the $2^k$ values
\[
  \alpha_{z, i} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{z}(r'_x, i), \qquad i \in \BB_k,
\]
and the verifier reconstructs each leaf evaluation locally as $\sel(\alpha_{z, i}, g^{2^i})$ and checks that the reconstructed leaves interpolate to the folded claim,
\[
  \sum_{i \in \BB_k} \mle{\eq}_k(\rho, i) \cdot \sel\bigl( \alpha_{z, i},\, g^{2^i} \bigr) \stackrel{?}{=} s^{(0)} = \mle{L}(r_x, \rho).
\]
The $2^k$ per-bit claims $\alpha_{z, i}$, all at the common point $r'_x$, are the subprotocol output; a prover that sends inconsistent values is caught downstream, where they are reduced against the committed witness with fresh randomness. The subprotocol is therefore the depth-$k$ GKR tree and one interpolation check, with total soundness error $O\bigl(k(k + \ell_\text{mul})\bigr) / |K|$.

The subprotocol never uses that the base is the generator: for any public base $h \in K^*$ it applies verbatim to the vector with entries $h^{\intval{z}[x]}$, reducing an evaluation claim on it to the per-bit claims on $\mle{z}$, with the leaf constants $g^{2^i}$ replaced by $h^{2^i}$. The combined protocol invokes this with $h = g^{2^{64}}$ for the high-half factor (\S\ref{sec:intmul-combined}).

\subsubsection{Variable-Base Exponentiation}
\label{sec:var-base-exp}

The variable-base subprotocol reduces a claim $s^{(k)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{var-exp}}(f, z)(r^{(k)})$ to one claim on $\mle{f}$ and the $2^k$ per-bit claims on $\mle{z}$. The parties first run the product-tree GKR loop and leaf extrapolation (\S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}), obtaining $2^k$ leaf claims $s^{(0)}_i \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}(r_x, i)$ at the common point $r_x$. Unlike the fixed-base case, the leaves couple the base and the exponent: the entry $L[x, i] = \sel(z[x, i], f[x]^{2^i}) = 1 + z[x, i] \cdot (f[x]^{2^i} - 1)$. Naively reducing the leaf claim to a claim on $\mle{f}$ would involve the polynomial $\mle{f}(X)^{2^i}$, whose degree blows up; we sidestep this using the Frobenius endomorphism $\varphi(\alpha) = \alpha^2$ (\S\ref{sec:binary-fields}). Applying $\varphi^{-i}$ (well-defined because $\varphi$ is invertible on $K$, with inverse $\varphi^{\lambda - 1}$) to the leaf claim:
\[
  \varphi^{-i}(s^{(0)}_i) \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}\bigl( \varphi^{-i}(r_x), x \bigr) \cdot \bigl( 1 + \mle{z}(x, i) \cdot (\mle{f}(x) - 1) \bigr),
\]
using that $\varphi^{-i}$ is a ring automorphism that fixes the $\FF{2}$-coefficients of $\mle{\eq}$ and of $\mle{z}(\cdot, i)$, and that $\varphi^{-i}(\mle{f}(x)^{2^i}) = \mle{f}(x)$.

The right-hand side is now of degree $\leq 2$ in $\mle{f}$ and $\mle{z}(\cdot, i)$. The verifier samples a single vector $\gamma \gets K^k$ and the parties run a batched $\ell_\text{mul}$-round sumcheck, using the multilinear Lagrange weights $\mle{\eq}_k(\gamma, i)$ as batching coefficients in place of $2^k$ independent ones:
\[
  \sum_{i = 0}^{2^k - 1} \mle{\eq}_k(\gamma, i) \cdot \varphi^{-i}(s^{(0)}_i) \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \sum_{i = 0}^{2^k - 1} \mle{\eq}_k(\gamma, i) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}\bigl( \varphi^{-i}(r_x), x \bigr) \cdot \bigl( 1 + \mle{z}(x, i) \cdot (\mle{f}(x) - 1) \bigr).
\]
A single $\gamma$ suffices because the weights $\mle{\eq}_k(\gamma, i)$ are the values at $\gamma$ of the $k$-variate multilinear Lagrange basis, so a false claim survives with probability at most $k / |K|$ by Schwartz--Zippel. At the end, the parties hold a final sumcheck challenge $r'_x \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$ and claimed evaluations $\alpha_f \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{f}(r'_x)$ and $\alpha_{z, i} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{z}(r'_x, i)$ for $i \in \set{0, \ldots, 2^k - 1}$. The verifier evaluates each $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}(\varphi^{-i}(r_x), r'_x)$ on its own, costing at most $\lambda$ squarings per $i$ to compute $\varphi^{-i}(r_x)$, and closes out the batched sumcheck. The claim $\alpha_f \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{f}(r'_x)$ is the output base claim, and the $2^k$ per-bit claims $\alpha_{z, i} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{z}(r'_x, i)$ are the output exponent claims.

\subsubsection{Combined Protocol}
\label{sec:intmul-combined}

The IntMul reduction verifies the multiplicative-group identity \eqref{eq:exp-identity} and the wraparound parity \eqref{eq:lsb-parity} for all $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}$ simultaneously, producing, for each of $\mle{a}, \mle{b}, \mle{c}_\text{lo}, \mle{c}_\text{hi}$, its $64$ per-bit evaluations at a shared constraint point. Write
\[
  \mle{A} \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(a), \qquad \mle{C}_\text{lo} \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{gen-exp}}(c_\text{lo}),
\]
the multilinear extensions of the base-$g$ fixed-base exponentiation vectors $A, C_\text{lo}$, whose entries $A[x] = g^{\intval{a}[x]}$ and $C_\text{lo}[x] = g^{\intval{c_\text{lo}}[x]}$ lie in $K^*$. The high half enters the integer relation weighted by $2^{64}$, so its factor is a fixed-base exponentiation with base $g^{2^{64}}$: write $C_\text{hi}$ for the vector with entries
\[
  C_\text{hi}[x] \coloneqq \bigl( g^{2^{64}} \bigr)^{\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]} = g^{2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]} \in K^*,
\]
and $\mle{C}_\text{hi}$ for its multilinear extension. The fixed-base subprotocol (\S\ref{sec:fixed-base-exp}) applies to $C_\text{hi}$ verbatim, with the public leaf constants $g^{2^i}$ replaced by $\bigl( g^{2^{64}} \bigr)^{2^i} = g^{2^{64 + i}}$; folding the high half straight into a base-$g^{2^{64}}$ exponentiation avoids ever forming the base-$g$ vector $g^{\intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]}$ and its $2^{64}$-th power. In this notation identity \eqref{eq:exp-identity} becomes the pointwise relation
\[
  \textsf{var-exp}(A, b)[x] = C_\text{lo}[x] \cdot C_\text{hi}[x], \qquad x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}},
\]
since $\textsf{var-exp}(A, b)[x] = A[x]^{\intval{b}[x]} = g^{\intval{a}[x] \cdot \intval{b}[x]}$ while $C_\text{lo}[x] \cdot C_\text{hi}[x] = g^{\intval{c_\text{lo}}[x] + 2^{64} \cdot \intval{c_\text{hi}}[x]}$. The base $A$ of the variable-base exponentiation is itself the output of a fixed-base exponentiation, so verifying the relation chains the two subprotocols of \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}.

We organize the reduction into six phases (0 through 5), writing $r_k$ for the principal verifier challenge of phase $k$ where unambiguous.

\paragraph{Phase 0 (setup).}
The verifier samples $r_0 \gets K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$ and sends it to the prover, who returns a single value $y_0 \in K$. This $y_0$ is claimed to equal both readings of the relation at $r_0$:
\[
  y_0 \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{var-exp}}(A, b)(r_0), \qquad\qquad y_0 \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}(r_0, x) \cdot C_\text{lo}[x] \cdot C_\text{hi}[x],
\]
the right-hand sum being the multilinear extension of the relation's right-hand side evaluated at $r_0$. The first reading is discharged by phases~1--4, the second by the \emph{deferred right-hand-side sumcheck} opened in phase~2. A single $y_0$ satisfies both readings iff the two multilinear sides agree at $r_0$, which by Schwartz--Zippel certifies the pointwise relation except with probability $\ell_\text{mul} / |K|$.

\paragraph{Phase 1 (variable-base tree and Frobenius twist).}
The parties run the product-tree GKR loop and leaf extrapolation (\S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}) of the variable-base subprotocol on the root claim $y_0 \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{var-exp}}(A, b)(r_0)$, with base $\mle{A}$ and exponent $b$. This reduces it to $64$ leaf claims
\[
  s^{(0)}_i \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}(r_1, i), \qquad L[x, i] = 1 + \bigl( A[x]^{2^i} - 1 \bigr) \cdot b[x, i], \qquad i \in \BB_6,
\]
all at the common leaf $x$-point $r_1 \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$, identifying the product tree's leaf $x$-point $r_x$ with the phase-$1$ challenge $r_1$. Because the leaves couple the base and the exponent, the parties then apply the Frobenius twist $\varphi^{-i}$ to each leaf claim and batch across $i$ with weights $\mle{\eq}_6(\gamma, i)$ for a verifier challenge $\gamma \gets K^6$ (\S\ref{sec:var-base-exp}). Phase~1 closes with the resulting variable-base sumcheck claim
\[
  \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \mle{\eq}_6(\gamma, i) \cdot \varphi^{-i}\bigl( s^{(0)}_i \bigr) \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \mle{\eq}_6(\gamma, i) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}\bigl( \varphi^{-i}(r_1), x \bigr) \cdot \bigl( 1 + (\mle{A}(x) - 1) \cdot \mle{b}(x, i) \bigr),
\]
a degree-$2$ sumcheck in $\mle{A}$ and $\mle{b}$.

\paragraph{Phase 2 (batched sumcheck).}
The parties run the variable-base sumcheck claim that phase~1 set up, batching into the same $\ell_\text{mul}$-round sumcheck the \emph{deferred right-hand-side reading} of phase~0,
\[
  y_0 \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}(r_0, x) \cdot \mle{C}_\text{lo}(x) \cdot \mle{C}_\text{hi}(x),
\]
a degree-$2$ sumcheck in $\mle{C}_\text{lo}$ and $\mle{C}_\text{hi}$. Sharing the common challenge $r_2 \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$, the sumchecks output the three root claims $\mle{A}(r_2)$, $\mle{C}_\text{lo}(r_2)$, and $\mle{C}_\text{hi}(r_2)$, the last a base-$g^{2^{64}}$ exponentiation claim. The variable-base subprotocol also outputs the $64$ per-bit exponent claims $\mle{b}(r_2, i)$ at the constraint point $r_2$; the verifier recombines them into the single claim $\mle{b}(r_2, r_I^b) = \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \mle{\eq}_6(r_I^b, i) \, \mle{b}(r_2, i)$ by sampling $r_I^b \gets K^6$, carrying one exponent claim rather than $64$ into the later phases. The phase hands the three root claims to phase~3 and the single recombined exponent claim $\mle{b}(r_2, r_I^b)$ to phase~4.

\paragraph{Phase 3 (fixed-base trees).}
The parties run the three fixed-base product trees on $\mle{A}(r_2)$, $\mle{C}_\text{lo}(r_2)$, $\mle{C}_\text{hi}(r_2)$, all rooted at the common point $r_2$ (the $\mle{A}$ and $\mle{C}_\text{lo}$ trees with base $g$, the $\mle{C}_\text{hi}$ tree with base $g^{2^{64}}$). The binary-tree grand-product loop (\S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}) is run down through \emph{all but the last layer}, folding both the bit-index and the constraint variable and batching the three trees to a common point $r_3$. Phase~3 ends holding, for each of $a, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$, an MLE-check at $r_3$ on the product of the two layer-$0$ child \emph{selectors}: writing $\mle{L}_{c_\text{lo}}$ for the gen-exp leaf with boolean values $\mle{L}_{c_\text{lo}}[x, i] = \sel(c_\text{lo}[x, i], g^{2^i})$, the claim is on $\mle{L}_{c_\text{lo}}(r_3, \rho, 0) \cdot \mle{L}_{c_\text{lo}}(r_3, \rho, 1)$ with $\rho \in K^5$ the folded lower bit-index, and analogously for $a$ and for $c_\text{hi}$ (whose leaf uses the high-half constants, $\mle{L}_{c_\text{hi}}[x, i] = \sel(c_\text{hi}[x, i], g^{2^{64 + i}})$). No local inversion is performed: with the bit-index folded, the selectors are kept symbolic and the conversion to $\mle{c}_\text{lo}$ is deferred to phase~5.

\paragraph{Phase 4 (last layer, re-randomization, and wraparound).}
A single batched sumcheck over the constraint variable runs three checks at once:
\begin{itemize}
\item the last product-tree layer of each of $a, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$---the MLE-check of the layer-$0$ selector products at $r_3$;
\item the re-randomization of the exponent: an MLE-check point-switching the single claim $\mle{b}(r_2, r_I^b)$ from the phase-$2$ constraint point $r_2$ to the shared point $r_x$;
\item the \emph{wraparound parity} zerocheck \eqref{eq:lsb-parity}, $\sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{mul}}(r_2, x) \bigl[ \mle{a}(x, 0) \, \mle{b}(x, 0) - \mle{c}_\text{lo}(x, 0) \bigr] \stackrel{?}{=} 0$, reusing the phase-$2$ challenge $r_2$---available for free because the $\mle{b}$ re-randomization already evaluates at constraint $r_2$.
\end{itemize}
The batch folds the constraint variable to the shared point $r_x \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$ and the tree bit-index to $r_I \in K^6$. Its outputs are the three leaf-selector claims $\mle{L}_a(r_x, r_I)$, $\mle{L}_{c_\text{lo}}(r_x, r_I)$, $\mle{L}_{c_\text{hi}}(r_x, r_I)$, the exponent claim $\mle{b}(r_x, r_I^b)$, and the bit-$0$ wraparound claims $\mle{a}(r_x, 0)$, $\mle{b}(r_x, 0)$, $\mle{c}_\text{lo}(r_x, 0)$.

\paragraph{Phase 5 (leaf reconstruction).}
The prover sends the $4 \times 64$ per-bit values $\mle{a}(r_x, i), \mle{b}(r_x, i), \mle{c}_\text{lo}(r_x, i), \mle{c}_\text{hi}(r_x, i)$ for $i \in \BB_6$; these are the IntMul output. The verifier binds them to the phase-$4$ claims rather than collapsing them: for each of $a, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$ it forms the selector recombination
\[
  \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \mle{\eq}_6(r_I, i) \, \sel\bigl( \mle{a}(r_x, i), g^{2^i} \bigr) \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}_a(r_x, r_I),
\]
using the leaf constant $g^{2^i}$ for $a$ and $c_\text{lo}$ and the high-half constant $g^{2^{64 + i}}$ for $c_\text{hi}$; for $b$ it tests $\sum_{i \in \BB_6} \mle{\eq}_6(r_I^b, i) \, \mle{b}(r_x, i)$ against $\mle{b}(r_x, r_I^b)$, and it tests the $i = 0$ values against the wraparound claims. The sent values are thereby bound to the GKR-verified products, and to the committed witness by the shift reduction downstream.

The $4 \times 64$ output claims, all at the shared constraint point $r_x \in K^{\ell_\text{mul}}$, are the IntMul operand columns' per-bit claims of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}, which folds them over the bit-index and re-randomizes them onto the unified constraint point before the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}) consumes them. Total soundness error is $O(\ell_\text{mul}) / |K|$, dominated by the depth-$6$ GKR trees and the batched sumchecks of phases~1--4, the last also carrying the wraparound parity zerocheck and the leaf-reconstruction binding.

\subsubsection{Prover Algorithm}
\label{sec:intmul-prover}

The dominant cost is preparing and maintaining tables of values for the various exponentiated multilinears at the relevant cube points; this involves substantial work in $K$. We highlight a few implementation considerations.

\paragraph{Computing the leaf tables.}
For each of the four exponentiations, the prover initializes the leaf tables $\bigl( L[x, i] \bigr)_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{mul}}}$ for $i = 0, \ldots, 63$ from the base values $f[x]$ and the bits $z[x, i]$. Since each $z[x, i] \in \FF{2}$, the entry $L[x, i]$ is either $1$ or $f[x]^{2^i} = \varphi^i(f[x])$. The $A$ and $C_\text{lo}$ exponentiations use base $g$, so the powers $g^{2^i}$ are precomputed once into a table indexed by $i$; the $C_\text{hi}$ exponentiation uses base $g^{2^{64}}$, whose powers $g^{2^{64 + i}}$ form a second such table. In each case the leaves are obtained by a bit-controlled select, so no per-entry exponentiation or Frobenius application is needed. For the variable-base exponentiation (base $\mle{A}$, exponent $b$), the prover applies $\varphi^i$ to each entry of the $\mle{A}$-table, which costs $i$ squarings per entry.

\paragraph{Sumcheck round computation.}
Each layer of the GKR loop runs a batched sumcheck with composing polynomials of individual degree $2$ (plus the equality indicator), over $\ell_\text{mul}$ rounds. Standard sumcheck-prover algorithms (e.g.\ Thaler~\cite[\S 4.1]{Thaler:2022aa}) apply directly. The Frobenius step's sumcheck has composing polynomials of degree $\leq 2$ in $\mle{f}$ and $\mle{z}(\cdot, i)$ (plus the equality indicator), so the per-round prover work is also linear in the table size.

\paragraph{Frobenius application.}
Computing $\varphi^{-i}(r_x)$ for $i = 0, \ldots, 63$, needed by the verifier in the variable-base Frobenius step, costs at most $\lambda \cdot 64$ field squarings in total. The prover precomputes these quantities once and reuses them across the batched sumcheck.

\subsection{The BinMul Reduction}
\label{sec:binmul}

The BinMul reduction transforms the satisfaction of the $n_\text{binmul}$ BinMul constraints (\S\ref{sec:binmul-constraints}) into evaluation claims on the multilinear extensions of the six BinMul constraint arrays. The BitAnd and IntMul constraints act on $\FF{2}$-valued arrays that carry one relation per bit; a BinMul constraint instead asserts a single multiplicative relation in the GHASH field $\FF{2^{128}}$ per constraint. Packing each operand's two words into one field element (\S\ref{sec:binmul-constraints}) turns the constraint into a product relation between $\FF{2^{128}}$-valued multilinears, which an ordinary zerocheck discharges.

\subsubsection{Packing the Operands}
\label{sec:binmul-packing}

Recall that $\FF{2^{128}} \cong \FF{2}[X] / (X^{128} + X^7 + X^2 + X + 1)$ is the GHASH field (\S\ref{sec:binary-fields}) and write $\gamma \in \FF{2^{128}}$ for the class of $X$, so that $(1, \gamma, \ldots, \gamma^{127})$ is the monomial $\FF{2}$-basis in which $\gval{\cdot}$ encodes operands. In the system default $\lambda = 128$ we have $\FF{2^{128}} = K$; when $128 \mid \lambda$ strictly, the reduction runs over $K$ with the operand values confined to the subfield $\FF{2^{128}} \subseteq K$ and $\gamma$ taken as the image of $X$ under the fixed embedding.

For each of the six constraint arrays write $\mle{a}_\text{lo}(I, X), \mle{a}_\text{hi}(I, X), \ldots, \mle{c}_\text{hi}(I, X)$ for the multilinear extensions, with bit-index $I \in \BB_6$ and constraint-index $X \in \BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}$. Define the packed operand multilinears $\mle{A}, \mle{B}, \mle{C} \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{\ell_\text{binmul} - 1}]^\mlin$ by
\[
  \mle{A}(X) \coloneqq \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \gamma^i \cdot \mle{a}_\text{lo}(i, X) + \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \gamma^{64 + i} \cdot \mle{a}_\text{hi}(i, X),
\]
and analogously $\mle{B}$ from $(b_\text{lo}, b_\text{hi})$ and $\mle{C}$ from $(c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi})$, where in each case $i \in \BB_6$ is identified with $\set{0, \ldots, 63}$ via binary representation. Each is multilinear in $X$ and $\FF{2^{128}}$-valued, and at a hypercube point $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}$ it evaluates to the encoded field element
\[
  \mle{A}(x) = \gval{a_\text{lo}[x], a_\text{hi}[x]} = A[x] \in \FF{2^{128}},
\]
since $\mle{a}_\text{lo}(i, x) = a_\text{lo}[i, x]$ is the $i$-th bit of the word $a_\text{lo}[x]$, and likewise for $B$ and $C$.

\subsubsection{The Zerocheck}
\label{sec:binmul-zerocheck}

Satisfaction of the BinMul constraints is the claim that $A[x] \cdot B[x] = C[x]$ for every $x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}$, i.e., that
\[
  f(X) \coloneqq \mle{A}(X) \cdot \mle{B}(X) - \mle{C}(X)
\]
vanishes on $\BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}$. The operands are already $K$-valued, so, unlike the BitAnd reduction (\S\ref{sec:binand}), no univariate skip or Rijndael-field specialization applies; the reduction is an ordinary product-check zerocheck.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples $r_z \gets K^{\ell_\text{binmul}}$ and sends it to the prover.
\item The parties run an $\ell_\text{binmul}$-round sumcheck on
  \[
    0 \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{binmul}}(r_z, x) \cdot \bigl( \mle{A}(x) \, \mle{B}(x) - \mle{C}(x) \bigr).
  \]
  The composing polynomial $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{binmul}}(r_z, X) \cdot \bigl( \mle{A}(X) \mle{B}(X) - \mle{C}(X) \bigr)$ has individual degree $\leq 3$, so the prover sends a degree-$3$ round polynomial each round. The sumcheck produces a challenge $r_x \in K^{\ell_\text{binmul}}$ and a target $s$, with the assertion that $s \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{binmul}}(r_z, r_x) \cdot \bigl( \mle{A}(r_x) \mle{B}(r_x) - \mle{C}(r_x) \bigr)$.
\item The prover sends the $6 \times 64$ per-bit values $\mle{a}_\text{lo}(r_x, i), \mle{a}_\text{hi}(r_x, i), \mle{b}_\text{lo}(r_x, i), \mle{b}_\text{hi}(r_x, i), \mle{c}_\text{lo}(r_x, i), \mle{c}_\text{hi}(r_x, i)$ for $i \in \BB_6$. The verifier recombines them into the packed evaluations
  \[
    \alpha_A \coloneqq \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \gamma^i \cdot \mle{a}_\text{lo}(r_x, i) + \gamma^{64 + i} \cdot \mle{a}_\text{hi}(r_x, i),
  \]
  and $\alpha_B, \alpha_C$ likewise, evaluates $\mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{binmul}}(r_z, r_x)$ on its own, and checks
  \[
    s \stackrel{?}{=} \bigl( \alpha_A \cdot \alpha_B - \alpha_C \bigr) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{binmul}}(r_z, r_x).
  \]
\end{enumerate}

The reduction's output is the $6 \times 64$ evaluation claims $\mle{a}_\text{lo}(r_x, i), \ldots, \mle{c}_\text{hi}(r_x, i)$ at the shared constraint point $r_x \in K^{\ell_\text{binmul}}$. These are the BinMul operand columns' per-bit claims of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}, in the same form as the IntMul output (\S\ref{sec:intmul}), and they take the same route to the shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}): a fold over the bit-index against $r_{\widehat{i}}$, then a re-randomization onto the unified constraint point. Soundness is that of the standard zerocheck, with error $O(\ell_\text{binmul}) / |K|$.

\subsubsection{Prover Algorithm}
\label{sec:binmul-prover}

The prover forms the value tables of $\mle{A}, \mle{B}, \mle{C}$ on $\BB_{\ell_\text{binmul}}$ by packing the bit-columns of the constraint arrays: each entry $\mle{A}(x) = A[x]$ is assembled from the $128$ bits of $(a_\text{lo}[x], a_\text{hi}[x])$ by a bit-controlled XOR of the precomputed basis constants $\gamma^0, \ldots, \gamma^{127}$, exactly as the IntMul leaf tables are formed by bit-controlled selects (\S\ref{sec:intmul-prover}). With the three tables in hand, the zerocheck sumcheck runs by standard algorithms (Thaler~\cite[\S 4.1]{Thaler:2022aa}), with per-round work linear in $2^{\ell_\text{binmul}}$ and a composing polynomial of individual degree $\leq 3$. The constraint arrays are themselves computed from the witness via the shift structure shared with the other reductions.

\subsection{The Shift Reduction}
\label{sec:shift-reduction}

The shift reduction consumes the evaluation claims produced by the Zero, BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul reductions and transforms all of them into a single multilinear-evaluation claim on the witness $\mle{w}$. It is the largest single component of the LIOP, with a sumcheck whose underlying domain contains the witness; the prover algorithm therefore has to exploit the sparsity of the constraint system to run in time close to the witness size.

For exposition we describe the reduction for a single oblong-multilinearized constraint array $\widehat{z}(\widehat{I}, X)$ over $D \times \BB_{\ell_\text{cons}}$, with a single input evaluation claim $\alpha_z \stackrel{?}{=} \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x)$. The protocol extends to multiple constraint arrays by random batching, with the verifier sampling fresh batching coefficients before the sumcheck begins. In the full Binius64 LIOP, the outputs of all four constraint reductions are first brought to a common oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$ over the bit-index and to a common constraint point $r^\star_x \in K^{\ell_\text{cons}}$, as described in \S\ref{sec:rz-unification}; a single shift reduction then processes the batched claim.

Both points must be shared, and for different reasons. The oblong point is shared out of necessity: it is carried by the $\mle{\delta}_D$ and shift-indicator factors below, which the sumcheck binds jointly for all arrays. The constraint point is shared for cost. It enters the derivation solely through the constraint matrix $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ of \S\ref{sec:mathematizing}, which is a per-array object to begin with, so arrays at distinct points $\rho_z$ batch perfectly well with coefficients $\eta_z$---the single term $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, y, S_1, O_1, S_2, O_2)$ becoming $\sum_z \eta_z \cdot \mle{Z}^z_\text{cons}(\rho_z, y, S_1, O_1, S_2, O_2)$, with the factorization of \S\ref{sec:shift-reduction-prover} untouched---but each distinct point costs the prover its own tensor expansion in the index build and the verifier its own pass over the constraint lists. Unification collapses these to one apiece.

An array $z$ shorter than $\ell_\text{cons}$ keeps its own $\ell_z$ constraint variables here and is claimed at $r^\star_x|_{\ell_z}$, the low $\ell_z$ coordinates of $r^\star_x$: the variable padding of \S\ref{sec:variable-padding}, used by the sumcheck that produces $r^\star_x$, aligns the arrays on their low variables. The points of any two arrays are therefore nested, and a single tensor expansion of $r^\star_x$, built one variable at a time from the lowest up, passes through $\textsf{TensorExpand}_{\ell_z}(r^\star_x|_{\ell_z})$ for every array in the batch as an intermediate stage.

\subsubsection{Unifying the Output Claims}
\label{sec:rz-unification}

The BitAnd and Zero constraints are the fundamental ones: every constraint system has them, and their reductions are built around the BitAnd sumcheck, which produces the point at which the shift reduction will work. The IntMul and BinMul constraints are auxiliary. Their reductions leave behind evaluation claims on \emph{operand columns}---the four arrays $a, b, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$ of IntMul (\S\ref{sec:intmul}, phase~5) and the six arrays $a_\text{lo}, a_\text{hi}, b_\text{lo}, b_\text{hi}, c_\text{lo}, c_\text{hi}$ of BinMul (\S\ref{sec:binmul-zerocheck})---and those claims are at the wrong points on both axes: the bit-index is left free rather than folded to the oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$, and the constraint index sits at the reduction's own final challenge rather than at the BitAnd point. This subsection moves them. Write $\textsf{Ops}$ for the set of ten operand columns; a column $z \in \textsf{Ops}$ has constraint dimension $\ell_z \in \set{\ell_\text{mul}, \ell_\text{binmul}}$ and comes with a constraint point $\rho_z \in K^{\ell_z}$, the final challenge of the reduction that produced it (so all columns of one reduction share a point). Fix an enumeration $m \colon \textsf{Ops} \to \set{1, \ldots, 10}$.

\paragraph{Folding the bit-index.}
For each $z \in \textsf{Ops}$ the prover has sent the $64$ per-bit evaluations $\mle{z}(\rho_z, i)$, $i \in \BB_6$. Once the BitAnd univariate skip has drawn $r_{\widehat{i}}$ (\S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}, step~3), the verifier folds them on its own into the oblong evaluation
\[
  \alpha_z \coloneqq \sum_{i \in \BB_6} \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \cdot \mle{z}(\rho_z, i) \stackrel{?}{=} \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, \rho_z),
\]
using the $64$ oblong weights of \S\ref{sec:oblong}. Every column is now a claim on an oblong-multilinearization at the shared $r_{\widehat{i}}$, like the BitAnd and Zero outputs. Drawing $r_{\widehat{i}}$ only after the per-bit values are in the transcript is what makes the fold binding; this is why the multiplication reductions run before BitAnd (phases 2--4 of \S\ref{sec:protocol-overview}).

\paragraph{Re-randomizing the constraint index.}
An evaluation claim on a multilinear is a sum over the cube,
\[
  \alpha_z \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_z}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_z}(\rho_z, x) \cdot \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, x),
\]
so a sumcheck moves it to whatever point the sumcheck's challenges land on. We batch all ten such claims into the BitAnd sumcheck of \S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}, step~4, so that they land on the BitAnd point. The verifier samples $\theta \gets K$ together with $r_{\widehat{i}}$; column $z$ enters with coefficient $\theta^{m(z)}$ and the BitAnd claim with $\theta^0 = 1$.

The summands have different variable counts, $\ell_\text{and}$ for BitAnd and $\ell_z$ for column $z$. Padding each on its high variables to the common count $\ell_\text{sc}$ (\S\ref{sec:variable-padding})---with $\nu_z \coloneqq \ell_\text{sc} - \ell_z$ and $\nu_\text{and} \coloneqq \ell_\text{sc} - \ell_\text{and}$, the padded summands are
\begin{align*}
  F_\text{and}(X) &\coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}(r_x, X_{< \ell_\text{and}}) \cdot \widehat{f}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X_{< \ell_\text{and}}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\nu_\text{and}}(0^{\nu_\text{and}}, X_{\geq \ell_\text{and}}), \\
  F_z(X) &\coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{\ell_z}(\rho_z, X_{< \ell_z}) \cdot \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, X_{< \ell_z}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\nu_z}(0^{\nu_z}, X_{\geq \ell_z}),
\end{align*}
where $X_{< \ell}$ and $X_{\geq \ell}$ denote the low $\ell$ and the remaining variables of $X \in K^{\ell_\text{sc}}$---the batched sumcheck is
\[
  g(r_{\widehat{i}}) + \sum_{z \in \textsf{Ops}} \theta^{m(z)} \alpha_z \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_\text{sc}}} \Bigl( F_\text{and}(x) + \sum_{z \in \textsf{Ops}} \theta^{m(z)} F_z(x) \Bigr).
\]
The BitAnd summand has individual degree $3$ and the column summands degree $2$, so the prover sends three coefficients per round, one more than the BitAnd sumcheck alone; the equality indicators sit at different points and none factors out of the combined round polynomial, but the prover still computes each summand's round polynomial in factored form and multiplies in the linear indicator afterwards (\S\ref{sec:batching-mle-checks}). Since the columns of one reduction share $\rho_z$ and $\ell_z$, the prover needs only one table per reduction for the round polynomials, $\sum_z \theta^{m(z)} \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, \cdot)$ over that reduction's columns: two extra multilinears in the sumcheck, each formed by the same subset-sum fold the BitAnd prover applies to $a, b, c$ (\S\ref{sec:rijndael-zerocheck}).

\paragraph{The unified constraint point.}
Let $r^\text{sc} \in K^{\ell_\text{sc}}$ be the sumcheck's challenge vector, coordinate $m$ being the challenge bound to $X_m$, and set
\[
  r^\star_x \coloneqq \bigl( r^\text{sc}, \overline{r}_d \bigr) \in K^{\ell_\text{cons}},
\]
where the high $\ell_\text{cons} - \ell_\text{sc}$ coordinates $\overline{r}_d$ are the fresh challenges of \S\ref{sec:zero}, present only when the Zero array is the longest of the four. For $\ell \leq \ell_\text{cons}$ write $r^\star_x|_\ell \coloneqq (r^\star_{x, 0}, \ldots, r^\star_{x, \ell - 1})$ for the low $\ell$ coordinates. Because the padding occupies the high variables, which are bound first, a summand on $\ell$ variables closes at $r^\star_x|_\ell$: every constraint array's point is a prefix of $r^\star_x$, and any two are nested.

\paragraph{Closing the sumcheck.}
The prover sends $\alpha_a, \alpha_b, \alpha_c$ (step~5 of \S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}) and, for each $z \in \textsf{Ops}$, a scalar $\sigma_z \stackrel{?}{=} \widehat{z}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_z})$. With $c_z \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{\nu_z}(0^{\nu_z}, r^\text{sc}_{\geq \ell_z})$ the value of summand $z$'s padding factor (and $c_\text{and}$ likewise), the verifier checks the final target against
\[
  s \stackrel{?}{=} c_\text{and} \, \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{and}}\bigl( r_x, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{and}} \bigr) \bigl( \alpha_a \alpha_b - \alpha_c \bigr)
  + \sum_{z \in \textsf{Ops}} \theta^{m(z)} \, c_z \, \mle{\eq}_{\ell_z}\bigl( \rho_z, r^\star_x|_{\ell_z} \bigr) \, \sigma_z,
\]
every factor of which it evaluates on its own. This leaves fourteen evaluation claims---$\widehat{a}, \widehat{b}, \widehat{c}$ from BitAnd, $\widehat{d}(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x|_{\ell_\text{zero}}) = 0$ from Zero (\S\ref{sec:zero}), and the ten $\widehat{z}$---all at the oblong point $r_{\widehat{i}}$ and at prefixes of $r^\star_x$. The verifier samples fresh batching coefficients $\eta_z$ and combines them into the single claim the shift reduction consumes. The $\eta_z$ must be drawn only now: the thirteen sent scalars are bound by one closing identity, and a prover holding $\eta$ in advance could perturb them by an $\eta$-orthogonal error that still satisfies it.

\paragraph{Cost and soundness.}
Unification costs the prover the fold of ten more arrays against the oblong weights, two more multilinears and two tensor expansions in the sumcheck, and one field element per round of transcript. It buys a single constraint point downstream: the shift reduction's constraint-column index and the verifier's evaluation of $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ (\S\ref{sec:mathematizing}) are served by one tensor expansion of $r^\star_x$, and the batched constraint matrix $\sum_z \eta_z \mle{Z}^z_\text{cons}(r^\star_x|_{\ell_z}, \cdot)$ lives at one point. The sumcheck error, $3 \ell_\text{sc} / |K|$, is charged to the BitAnd reduction in \S\ref{sec:univariate-skip}; the batching and padding add $O(1) / |K|$ and nothing, respectively.

\subsubsection{Mathematizing Constraint Arrays}
\label{sec:mathematizing}

We first re-express the oblong-multilinearization $\widehat{z}(\widehat{I}, X)$ in terms of $\mle{w}$ and the shift indicators.

\paragraph{Constraint matrix.}
Define the six-dimensional $\FF{2}$-valued matrix $Z_\text{cons}$ of shape $n_\text{cons} \times n_\text{words} \times 64 \times 8 \times 64 \times 8$ by
\[
  Z_\text{cons}[x, y, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2] \coloneqq \mathbb{1}\bigl[ (y, \text{op}(o_1), s_1, \text{op}(o_2), s_2) \in L^z_x \bigr],
\]
where $L^z_x$ is the list of shifted value indices generating $z[x]$ and $o_1, o_2 \in \BB_3$ index $\textsf{ShiftOps}$ as in \S\ref{sec:shifted-indices}. The constraint array can be re-expressed as
\[
  z[x] = \sum_{o_1, o_2 \in \BB_3} \sum_{s_1, s_2 \in \BB_6} \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}} Z_\text{cons}[x, y, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2] \cdot \text{op}(o_2)\bigl( \text{op}(o_1)(w[y], s_1), s_2 \bigr).
\]
Writing $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ for the multilinear extension of $Z_\text{cons}$, taking multilinear extensions and unrolling the composition identity \eqref{eq:composite-indicator} gives
\begin{align*}
  \mle{z}(I, X) = \sum_{o_1, o_2 \in \BB_3} \sum_{s_1, s_2 \in \BB_6} \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}} \sum_{k, j \in \BB_6} &\mle{Z}_\text{cons}(X, y, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2) \\
  &{} \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(I, k, s_2, o_2) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, j, s_1, o_1) \cdot \mle{w}(j, y),
\end{align*}
where the shift indicators have been assembled into the interpolated indicator of \eqref{eq:shift-indicator-interpolation}, legitimately so because $o_1$ and $o_2$ are summed over the cube. Applying the oblong-multilinearization construction of \S\ref{sec:oblong} (introducing $\delta_D(\widehat{I}, \widehat{i})$ and summing $i \in \BB_6$) yields the analogous expression for $\widehat{z}(\widehat{I}, X)$. Specializing at $(r_{\widehat{i}}, r^\star_x)$ produces the explicit form
\begin{align}
  \alpha_z \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{o_1, o_2 \in \BB_3} \sum_{s_1, s_2 \in \BB_6} \sum_{j, k, i \in \BB_6} \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}} &\delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \cdot \mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, y, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2) \notag \\
  &{} \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(i, k, s_2, o_2) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, j, s_1, o_1) \cdot \mle{w}(j, y).
  \label{eq:shift-reduction-claim}
\end{align}
This nested sum is over $\ell_\text{words} + 36$ variables.

\paragraph{The sumcheck.}
The parties discharge \eqref{eq:shift-reduction-claim} by a single sumcheck over its variables $(O_1, S_1, O_2, S_2, J, K, I, Y)$. Write $\mle{\delta}_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, I)$ for the multilinear extension in $I$ of the $64$ oblong weights $i \mapsto \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i})$; it agrees with them on $\BB_6$, so putting it in place of the first factor leaves \eqref{eq:shift-reduction-claim} unchanged. Each of the five factors is then multilinear in the summation variables, and each variable occurs in exactly two of them---$I$ in $\mle{\delta}_D$ and the outer indicator; $K$ in the outer and the inner indicator; $J$ in the inner indicator and $\mle{w}$; $S_1$ and $O_1$ in $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ and the inner indicator; $S_2$ and $O_2$ in $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ and the outer indicator; $Y$ in $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ and $\mle{w}$---so the summand has degree $2$ in every variable, and the prover sends a degree-$2$ round polynomial in each of the $36 + \ell_\text{words}$ rounds.

The variables are bound in the order
\[
  O_2, \quad S_2, \quad O_1, \quad S_1, \quad J, \quad K, \quad I, \quad Y,
\]
the outer shift ahead of the inner one, producing challenges $r_{o_2}, r_{o_1} \in K^3$, $r_{s_2}, r_{s_1} \in K^6$, $r_j, r_k, r_i \in K^6$, and $r_y \in K^{\ell_\text{words}}$. This order is what admits the efficient multi-phase prover algorithm of \S\ref{sec:shift-reduction-prover}, which peels the shifts from the output end inward. The sumcheck closes with the claim
\begin{align*}
  \gamma \stackrel{?}{=} \; &\mle{\delta}_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, r_i) \cdot \mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, r_y, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2}) \\
  &{} \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(r_i, r_k, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2}) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(r_k, r_j, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}) \cdot \mle{w}(r_j, r_y).
\end{align*}
The verifier evaluates the first four factors on its own and outputs the single witness-evaluation claim $\mle{w}(r_j, r_y)$. This bit-level claim on the $\FF{2}$-valued witness is reduced to a linear query on the packed $K$-valued oracle $\pi$ in the ring-switching step of \S\ref{sec:ring-switching}.

\paragraph{Verifier evaluations.}
The factor $\mle{\delta}_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, r_i)$ is the inner product of the $64$ oblong weights $\bigl( \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \bigr)_{i \in \BB_6}$ (\S\ref{sec:oblong}) with $\textsf{TensorExpand}_6(r_i)$. The two shift-indicator factors are evaluations of the interpolated indicator, which \eqref{eq:shift-indicator-interpolation} expands into the eight succinct arithmetizations of \S\ref{sec:shift-indicators}, weighted by $\textsf{TensorExpand}_3$ of the operation challenge in the corresponding slot. For $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, r_y, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2})$ the straightforward sparse-evaluation algorithm runs in $O(n_\text{cons}) + O(\text{circuit size})$ time by iterating over the constraint lists; succinct verification is left for future work, following e.g.\ the Spark compiler of Setty~\cite{Setty:2020aa}. Because all four reductions have been brought to the unified point, one tensor expansion of $r^\star_x$ serves every constraint class: an array of $\ell_z$ constraint variables reads the intermediate stage $\textsf{TensorExpand}_{\ell_z}(r^\star_x|_{\ell_z})$ and contributes with its batching coefficient $\eta_z$.

\subsubsection{Prover Algorithm}
\label{sec:shift-reduction-prover}

We sketch the prover-side data structures and the main loops. The binding order of \S\ref{sec:mathematizing} cuts the sumcheck into six phases---nine rounds on $(O_2, S_2)$, nine on $(O_1, S_1)$, then six each on $J$, $K$, and $I$, and finally $\ell_\text{words}$ on $Y$---of which only the last touches a table indexed by the witness. The goal is to make the prover's work close to linear in the witness size despite the formal $\ell_\text{words} + 36$-dimensional sum.

\paragraph{Factorization.}
In \eqref{eq:shift-reduction-claim}, no single factor depends on both $i$ and $y$: the oblong weights and the two shift indicators reach from $i$ down to $j$, whereas $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}$ and $\mle{w}$ reach from $y$ up to $j$. The two sides meet only at $J$. Through the first three phases, while the shift variables and $J$ are being bound, $i$, $k$, and $y$ all still range over the cube, so the sums over $(i, k)$ and over $y$ factor and the running claim reads
\[
  \alpha_z \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{o_1, o_2 \in \BB_3} \sum_{s_1, s_2 \in \BB_6} \sum_{j \in \BB_6} h(j, s_2, o_2, s_1, o_1) \cdot g(j, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2),
\]
where
\begin{align*}
  h(J, S_2, O_2, S_1, O_1) &\coloneqq \sum_{i, k \in \BB_6} \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i}) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(i, k, S_2, O_2) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, J, S_1, O_1), \\
  g(J, S_1, O_1, S_2, O_2) &\coloneqq \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{words}}} \mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, y, S_1, O_1, S_2, O_2) \cdot \mle{w}(J, y).
\end{align*}
Both are multilinear in their $24$ variables: in each summand the factors depend multilinearly on disjoint blocks of them, the shared indices $i, k, y$ being summed over the cube. A value table of $h$ would have $2^{24}$ entries, too large to form. The prover never materializes it, working instead from two successive $2^{15}$ tables built by the operator below.

\paragraph{The shift operator.}
For a vector $\psi \in K^{\BB_6}$ write $T[\psi]$ for the $2^{15}$-entry table
\[
  T[\psi](J, S, O) \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \BB_6} \psi(k) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, J, S, O).
\]
Each $(s, o)$ slice of $T[\psi]$ is a shift of $\psi$. For $\textsf{srl}$ the indicator $\textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{srl}(k, j, s)$ is $\mathbb{1}[j = k + s]$ on the cube, so the slice holds $\psi(j - s)$ where that is valid and $0$ elsewhere; similar shape patterns apply to $\textsf{sll}$, $\textsf{sra}$, $\textsf{ror}$, and their $32$-bit variants. For $\textsf{sra}$ the entry at $j = 63$ collects several values of $k$, and these are accumulated in a running prefix sum over $s$. The whole table costs $O(2^{15})$ field operations, a single slice $O(2^6)$.

Writing $d \in K^{\BB_6}$ for the oblong weights $d(i) \coloneqq \delta_D(r_{\widehat{i}}, \widehat{i})$, precomputed once (\S\ref{sec:oblong}), both contractions in $h$ are applications of $T$. Setting $\eta \coloneqq T[d]$ and naming its first index $K$,
\[
  \eta(K, S_2, O_2) = \sum_{i \in \BB_6} d(i) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(i, K, S_2, O_2), \qquad h(J, s_2, o_2, s_1, o_1) = T\bigl[ \eta(\cdot, s_2, o_2) \bigr](J, s_1, o_1).
\]
So $h$ is reached by applying $T$ to the oblong weights, taking the outer slice, and applying $T$ again.

\paragraph{Active shift quadruples.}
A constraint system draws on only a few of the $2^{18}$ quadruples $(s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2)$. Hash and cipher circuits fix a handful of shift and rotation amounts and reuse them throughout, so the count
\[
  n_\text{shift} \coloneqq \bigl| \set{(s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2) : (y, \text{op}(o_1), s_1, \text{op}(o_2), s_2) \in L^z_x \text{ for some } x \text{ and some array } z \text{ of the batch}} \bigr|
\]
of \emph{active} quadruples---taken across the Zero, BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul arrays that the batched reduction consumes---is typically in the tens. For an inactive quadruple the entire slice $Z_\text{cons}[\cdot, \cdot, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2]$ vanishes, hence so does $g(J, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2)$. The prover accordingly carries $g$ on the active quadruples alone.

\paragraph{Constraint-column index.}
Most shifted value indices in practice are sparse: each constraint list $L^z_x$ has $O(1)$ entries on average. The prover builds, in a single linear pass over the constraint system, a sparse index data structure $\textsf{index} \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{words}} \to (\BB_6 \times \BB_3 \times \BB_6 \times \BB_3 \to K)$ such that
\[
  \textsf{index}[y][(s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2)] = \mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, y, s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2)
\]
exactly for those keys at which the value is nonzero (otherwise the key is absent). Writing $E_\ell \coloneqq \textsf{TensorExpand}_\ell(r^\star_x|_\ell)$, the build is
\begin{quote}
  expand $r^\star_x$ one variable at a time from the lowest up, retaining the stages $E_\ell$ for the lengths $\ell$ that occur; then for each array $z$ of the batch, each $x \in \BB_{\ell_z}$ and each $(y, \text{op}(o_1), s_1, \text{op}(o_2), s_2) \in L^z_x$, add $\eta_z \cdot E_{\ell_z}[x]$ to $\textsf{index}[y][(s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2)]$.
\end{quote}
Total work and space are $O(\text{circuit size}) + O(n_\text{words}) + O(n_\text{cons})$ field elements. The single expansion serves every array because the unification of \S\ref{sec:rz-unification} makes the arrays' constraint points nested prefixes of $r^\star_x$; the index it fills is the batched matrix $\sum_z \eta_z \mle{Z}^z_\text{cons}$, and the phases below never see the individual arrays again.

\paragraph{Computing $g$.}
Using $\textsf{index}$, the prover forms the $2^6 \cdot n_\text{shift}$-entry table $g[j][q] = \sum_y \mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, y, q) \cdot \mle{w}(j, y)$, indexed by the bit position and by an active quadruple $q$, by iterating over $y$ and, for each populated key $q$ in $\textsf{index}[y]$, adding $\textsf{index}[y][q]$ into $g[j][q]$ for each bit $j$ where $w[y]_j = 1$. Total work is proportional to the (sparse) circuit size. This loop is the reduction's one pass over the whole circuit, and the accumulator it scatters into stays cache-resident throughout.

\paragraph{Phase 1: binding the outer shift.}
The prover materializes $\eta = T[d]$ and folds it over $(O_2, S_2)$ as the nine rounds proceed, at $2^{15}$ multiplications in total. In round $t$ it needs, for each active quadruple whose outer pair is still live, the $2^6$-vector
\[
  h_t(\cdot) = T\bigl[ \eta_t(\cdot, u) \bigr](\cdot, s_1, o_1),
\]
where $u$ is the partially bound outer index and $\eta_t$ the folded table: one slice of $T$ applied to one stride of $\eta_t$, hence $O(2^6)$ apiece. Pairing these against the sparse $g$ costs another $O(2^6)$ per active combination, so a round costs $O(2^6 \cdot n_\text{shift})$.

The inner pair $(s_1, o_1)$ is still a cube index throughout this phase, and that is what keeps the slice of $T$ a shift. Under the opposite order the outer indicator would be folded to a dense $2^6 \times 2^6$ matrix by the time the inner pair was reached, and each active combination would cost a full matrix--vector product in place of a shift.

\paragraph{Phase 2: binding the inner shift.}
Phase 1 leaves $\eta$ folded to the single $2^6$-vector $\psi \coloneqq \eta(\cdot, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2})$. The prover forms $T[\psi]$, whose slices are the concatenated shifts of $\psi$, and runs the nine rounds binding $(O_1, S_1)$ against it. This is an ordinary sparse--dense sumcheck. Let $L_t$ denote the image of the active inner pairs under the map forgetting the $t$ coordinates bound so far; since binding a variable merges two indices into one, $|L_t| \leq \min(n_\text{shift}, 2^{9 - t})$. The folded $g$ is supported on $L_t$, so the prover folds and forms round polynomials over those entries alone, at $O(2^6 \cdot |L_t|)$ field operations in round $t$ and $O\bigl( 2^6 \cdot n_\text{shift} \cdot \log_2 (2^9 / n_\text{shift}) \bigr)$ across the phase. The table $T[\psi]$ is folded in full: its folded value at an active pair is a weighted sum over that index's whole subtree, inactive pairs included, so the dense table is what supplies it. This costs at most $2^{15}$ multiplications, a fixed charge independent of the circuit.

\paragraph{Phase 3: binding $J$.}
Both tables now have length $2^6$ in $J$ alone, and the six rounds binding $J$ are a dense sumcheck on their product, at $O(2^6)$ field operations. The phase closes with
\[
  \beta \stackrel{?}{=} h(r_j, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2}, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}) \cdot G, \qquad G \coloneqq g(r_j, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2}),
\]
both factors being the terminal entries of the two folds. In particular $G$ carries the summation over $y$ of $\mle{Z}_\text{cons} \cdot \mle{w}$, which is what built the table of $g$ in the first place.

\paragraph{Phase 4: binding $K$.}
Unrolling $h$ at the bound shift point exposes the six rounds binding $K$ as a sumcheck on
\[
  \beta \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{k \in \BB_6} \psi(k) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, r_j, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}) \cdot G.
\]
The first factor is the vector $\psi$ that phase 1 left behind: the terminal fold of $\eta$ is exactly the partial evaluation $\sum_{i \in \BB_6} d(i) \cdot \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(i, K, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2})$, the multilinear extension commuting with the finite sum over $i$. The second the prover tabulates as $\chi(k) \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(k, r_j, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1})$, from $2^6$ evaluations of the succinct arithmetizations of \S\ref{sec:shift-indicators}. Keeping $\psi$ and $\chi$ apart is what makes the round polynomials degree $2$; the phase costs $O(2^6)$ field operations.

\paragraph{Phase 5: binding $I$.}
The same step once more, one link further up the chain. The prover holds $d$ from the outset and tabulates $v(i) \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(i, r_k, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2})$ from another $2^6$ succinct evaluations, then runs six rounds on
\[
  \sum_{i \in \BB_6} d(i) \cdot v(i) \cdot \Lambda, \qquad \Lambda \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}(r_k, r_j, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}) \cdot G,
\]
where $\Lambda$ collects the scalars already fixed, the first being the terminal entry of the $\chi$ fold. Neither of the last two phases touches a table indexed by $y$.

\paragraph{Phase 6: binding $Y$.}
For the $\ell_\text{words}$ rounds binding $Y$ the prover needs the value tables of the multilinears $\mle{w}(r_j, Y)$ (a standard partial specialization of $\mle{w}$, computed via $\textsf{TensorExpand}_6(r_j)$) and $\mle{Z}_\text{cons}(r^\star_x, Y, r_{s_1}, r_{o_1}, r_{s_2}, r_{o_2})$. The second again uses $\textsf{index}$: precompute the four tensor expansions $\textsf{TensorExpand}_6(r_{s_1})$, $\textsf{TensorExpand}_3(r_{o_1})$, $\textsf{TensorExpand}_6(r_{s_2})$, $\textsf{TensorExpand}_3(r_{o_2})$, then accumulate, for each $y$ and each populated $q = (s_1, o_1, s_2, o_2) \in \textsf{index}[y]$, the product of $\textsf{index}[y][q]$ with $\mle{\eq}_6(r_{s_1}, s_1) \cdot \mle{\eq}_3(r_{o_1}, o_1) \cdot \mle{\eq}_6(r_{s_2}, s_2) \cdot \mle{\eq}_3(r_{o_2}, o_2)$ into a length-$n_\text{words}$ vector. The scalars fixed by the earlier phases ride along as a constant factor.

\subsection{Ring-Switching to the Packed Witness}
\label{sec:ring-switching}

The shift reduction (\S\ref{sec:shift-reduction}) closes by establishing a single evaluation claim
\[
  \mle{w}(r_j, r_y) = t, \qquad r_j \in K^6, \quad r_y \in K^{\ell_\text{words}}, \quad t \in K,
\]
on the multilinear extension of the $\FF{2}$-valued witness $w$. Our LIOP framework (\S\ref{sec:liop}) requires the prover oracle to be $K$-valued and the final witness-side check to be a single linear query against that oracle. Ring-switching, due to Diamond and Posen~\cite{Diamond:2024ab}, bridges the two by packing the $\FF{2}$-valued witness into a $K$-valued oracle and reducing the bit-level evaluation claim to a linear query on the packed oracle. We summarize the construction at a level sufficient for the LIOP specification and defer protocol details and proofs to \cite{Diamond:2024ab}.

\paragraph{The packed witness.}
We view the witness as a length-$64 \cdot n_\text{words}$ bit-string indexed by $\BB_{6 + \ell_\text{words}}$. Set the packing parameter
\[
  \kappa \coloneqq \log_2 \lambda,
\]
the $\FF{2}$-dimension of $K$. Our padding assumption $\lambda \mid 64 \cdot n_\text{words}$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}) ensures $6 + \ell_\text{words} \geq \kappa$, so we can group the $6 + \ell_\text{words}$ Boolean dimensions of $w$ into the first $\kappa$ (``packed'' bit dimensions, indexed by $i \in \BB_\kappa$) and the remaining
\[
  \ell_\text{pack} \coloneqq 6 + \ell_\text{words} - \kappa
\]
(``unpacked'' dimensions, indexed by $y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}}$). Each ``cell'' indexed by $y$ contains $\lambda$ witness bits; with $\lambda = 128$ this packs two consecutive $64$-bit witness words into a single $K$-element.

Fix once and for all an $\FF{2}$-basis $(\beta_i)_{i \in \BB_\kappa}$ of $K$, indexed by $\BB_\kappa$ for notational convenience. The \emph{packing map} $\textsf{pack}_\lambda$ takes the witness $w \colon \BB_{6 + \ell_\text{words}} \to \FF{2}$ to the function
\[
  \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}} \to K, \qquad \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w)(y) \coloneqq \sum_{i \in \BB_\kappa} w(i, y) \cdot \beta_i,
\]
i.e., each entry of $\textsf{pack}_\lambda(w)$ is the inner product of $\lambda$ witness bits with the chosen basis. The LIOP commits to the oracle $\pi \coloneqq \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w)$, of length $2^{\ell_\text{pack}}$ over $K$.

\paragraph{Ring-switching reduction.}
The reduction follows Diamond and Posen \cite[Construction~3.1]{Diamond:2024ab}. We split the shift reduction's evaluation point $(r_j, r_y) \in K^{6 + \ell_\text{words}}$ into a packed part $r_\text{pack} \in K^\kappa$ and an unpacked part $r_\text{rest} \in K^{\ell_\text{pack}}$, reinterpreting $(r_j, r_y)$ under the packing layout above.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The prover sends the vector of partial evaluations
  \[
    \hat{s} \coloneqq \bigl( \hat{s}_i \bigr)_{i \in \BB_\kappa}, \qquad \hat{s}_i \coloneqq \mle{w}(i, r_\text{rest}),
  \]
  one $K$-element per index $i \in \BB_\kappa$.
\item The verifier checks consistency with the input claim $t$ by verifying the inner product
  \[
    t \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{i \in \BB_\kappa} \mle{\eq}_\kappa(i, r_\text{pack}) \cdot \hat{s}_i.
  \]
\item The verifier samples $r' \gets K^{\ell_\text{pack}}$.
\item The verifier specializes the \emph{ring-switching indicator multilinear} of \cite[Construction~3.1]{Diamond:2024ab} at $(r_\text{rest}, r')$ to obtain a multilinear $\textsf{ring-switch-ind}_{r_\text{rest}, r'}(Y) \in K[Y_0, \ldots, Y_{\ell_\text{pack} - 1}]^\mlin$, and derives a target value
  \[
    s' \in K
  \]
  as a fixed $K$-linear combination of the entries of $\hat{s}$ whose coefficients depend on $r_\text{rest}$, $r'$, and the basis $(\beta_i)$. The exact functional form of both $\textsf{ring-switch-ind}_{r_\text{rest}, r'}$ and $s'$ is given by \cite[Construction~3.1]{Diamond:2024ab}; the verifier evaluates both in $O(\ell_\text{pack} \cdot \lambda)$ operations.
\end{enumerate}

The output of ring-switching is the pair $(\textsf{ring-switch-ind}_{r_\text{rest}, r'}, s')$, which defines the linear oracle query on $\pi$:
\[
  \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}}} \textsf{ring-switch-ind}_{r_\text{rest}, r'}(y) \cdot \pi(y) \stackrel{?}{=} s'.
\]
Soundness of this reduction is established in \cite[\S 3]{Diamond:2024ab}; the error is $O(\ell_\text{pack})/|K|$ over $r'$.

For notational uniformity with the rest of the document, we abbreviate this output query as $(t_\text{rs}, s_\text{rs}) \coloneqq (\textsf{ring-switch-ind}_{r_\text{rest}, r'}, s')$. It is one of the two linear queries on $\pi$ produced by the LIOP; it is random-combined with the public-input-check query of \S\ref{sec:public-input-check} to yield the single final query of \S\ref{sec:liop}.

\subsection{Public Input Check}
\label{sec:public-input-check}

The public input check verifies that the first $n_\text{public} \coloneqq n_\text{const} + n_\text{inout}$ words of $w$ equal the verifier-held public data: $w[y] = C(y)$ for $y \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{const} - 1}$ and $w[n_\text{const} + y] = u(y)$ for $y \in \set{0, \ldots, n_\text{inout} - 1}$. We concatenate the constants and the statement into a single vector
\[
  \textsf{public} \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_{\ell_\text{public}} \to \FF{2}, \qquad \textsf{public} \coloneqq C \mathbin{\Vert} u,
\]
under the identification of $\BB_{\ell_\text{public}}$ with $\set{0, \ldots, n_\text{public} - 1}$. The padding requirement $\lambda \mid 64 \cdot n_\text{public}$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}) lets us apply the packing map $\textsf{pack}_\lambda$ (\S\ref{sec:ring-switching}) to $\textsf{public}$ in the same way as to $w$, producing
\[
  \textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public}) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{public-pack}} \to K, \qquad \ell_\text{public-pack} \coloneqq \ell_\text{public} - (\log_2 \lambda - 6).
\]
The check the verifier wants to enforce is that $\pi$ agrees with $\textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public})$ on the first $2^{\ell_\text{public-pack}}$ entries: for all $y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{public-pack}}$,
\[
  \pi(y, 0, \ldots, 0) \stackrel{?}{=} \textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public})(y),
\]
where the index $(y, 0, \ldots, 0) \in \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}}$ pads $y$ with $\ell_\text{pack} - \ell_\text{public-pack}$ zeros on the high coordinates. Equivalently, the polynomial
\[
  \mle{\pi}(Y_0, \ldots, Y_{\ell_\text{public-pack} - 1}, 0, \ldots, 0) - \mle{\textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public})}(Y_0, \ldots, Y_{\ell_\text{public-pack} - 1})
\]
vanishes on $\BB_{\ell_\text{public-pack}}$.

\paragraph{Reduction to a linear query.}
The verifier samples $r_p \gets K^{\ell_\text{public-pack}}$ and checks
\[
  \mle{\pi}(r_p, 0, \ldots, 0) \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public})}(r_p).
\]
The right-hand side is computable by the verifier on its own in $O(n_\text{public} / \lambda)$ time via the standard $\textsf{TensorExpand}$-based multilinear-evaluation algorithm. The left-hand side is an evaluation of the oracle's multilinear extension, which is exactly the multilinear-evaluation linear query on $\pi$ at the point $(r_p, 0, \ldots, 0) \in K^{\ell_\text{pack}}$:
\[
  t_\text{pub}(Y) \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{pack}}\bigl( (r_p, 0, \ldots, 0), Y \bigr) = \mle{\eq}_{\ell_\text{public-pack}}(r_p, Y_{<\ell_\text{public-pack}}) \cdot \prod_{i = \ell_\text{public-pack}}^{\ell_\text{pack} - 1} (1 - Y_i),
\]
with target $s_\text{pub} \coloneqq \mle{\textsf{pack}_\lambda(\textsf{public})}(r_p)$. The check passes iff $\sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}}} t_\text{pub}(y) \cdot \pi(y) = s_\text{pub}$.

By the standard zerocheck soundness, the reduction has error $\ell_\text{public-pack} / |K|$ over the choice of $r_p$.

\paragraph{Batching with the ring-switching query.}
The LIOP could output the two queries $(t_\text{rs}, s_\text{rs})$ from ring-switching and $(t_\text{pub}, s_\text{pub})$ from the public input check independently. We instead random-combine them into a single linear query on $\pi$. The verifier samples a batching coefficient $\xi \gets K$ and outputs
\[
  t(Y) \coloneqq t_\text{rs}(Y) + \xi \cdot t_\text{pub}(Y), \qquad s \coloneqq s_\text{rs} + \xi \cdot s_\text{pub},
\]
so that the LIOP's final linear query is $\sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}}} t(y) \cdot \pi(y) = s$. Both $t_\text{rs}$ and $t_\text{pub}$ are succinct (evaluable in $O(\ell_\text{pack})$ field operations), so $t$ is too. The random batching contributes an additional $1/|K|$ to the soundness error.

\section{Basic SNARK Compiler}
\label{sec:basic-snark-compiler}

This section presents a generic compiler from any LIOP (\S\ref{sec:liop}) to a SNARK. The compiler is a composition of three standard transformations,
\[
  \text{LIOP} \xrightarrow{\text{BaseFold}} \text{IOP} \xrightarrow{\text{BCS}} \text{IP} \xrightarrow{\text{Fiat--Shamir}} \text{SNARK},
\]
each of which is independent of the LIOP and applies uniformly to LIOPs with any number of oracles and any number of linear queries. Applying the compiler to the Binius64 LIOP of \S\ref{sec:binius-liop} yields the Binius64 SNARK (\S\ref{sec:binius64-snark}).

\subsection{Compiling LIOPs to SNARKs}
\label{sec:liop-to-snark}

\paragraph{Stage 1: LIOP \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} IOP via BaseFold.}
An LIOP's verifier closes by issuing linear queries, each specified by a multilinear polynomial $t_j$ and target $s_j$ against an oracle $\pi_{i_j}$ (\S\ref{sec:liop}). To turn this into a standard IOP, in which the verifier issues point queries, we encode each LIOP oracle $\pi_i \in K^{2^{n_i}}$ as a codeword of a Reed--Solomon code over $K$. Each linear query then becomes an additional linear constraint on the codeword: the codeword is required not only to be a valid Reed--Solomon codeword but also to satisfy $\sum_{w \in \BB_{n_i}} t_j(w) \cdot \pi_{i_j}(w) = s_j$. The pair (Reed--Solomon code, linear constraint) is a \emph{constrained Reed--Solomon code} in the sense of Arnon, Chiesa, Fenzi, and Yogev~\cite[\S 4.1]{Arnon:2025aa}. BaseFold (\S\ref{sec:basefold}) is an \emph{interactive oracle proof of proximity} (IOPP) for constrained Reed--Solomon codes: given oracle access to a candidate codeword, BaseFold accepts iff the codeword is close to a true codeword of the constrained code, using a polylogarithmic number of point queries. Composing one BaseFold instance per LIOP linear query yields a public-coin IOP whose oracles are the original $\pi_i$ together with the folded codewords introduced by BaseFold.

\paragraph{Stage 2: IOP \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} IP via BCS.}
The BCS transform of Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, and Spooner~\cite{Ben-Sasson:2016aa} replaces each oracle message of an IOP with the Merkle-tree root of its content; subsequent point queries are answered by the prover with the corresponding leaf and Merkle authentication path. In the random oracle model, this transformation preserves soundness up to a tight multiplicative loss in the adversary's query budget, and the result is a public-coin interactive proof (no oracles) of the same statement.

\paragraph{Stage 3: IP \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{to} SNARK via Fiat--Shamir.}
The Fiat--Shamir transform makes the public-coin IP non-interactive by deriving each verifier challenge as a hash of the running transcript. The resulting non-interactive argument is a SNARK in the random oracle model.

\paragraph{Succinctness.}
The compiled SNARK is succinct (proof size and verification time polylogarithmic in the witness size) provided the source LIOP is succinct in the sense of \S\ref{sec:liop}: when each linear query operand $t_{j, i}$ is evaluable in $\text{poly}(n_i)$ time, BaseFold's verifier uses polylogarithmically many point queries, the Merkle openings are polylog-sized, and the LIOP's own non-oracle messages are short.

\subsection{BaseFold}
\label{sec:basefold}

BaseFold is an IOPP for constrained Reed--Solomon codes (Arnon, Chiesa, Fenzi, and Yogev~\cite[\S 4.1]{Arnon:2025aa}). The original construction is due to Zeilberger, Chen, and Fisch~\cite{Zeilberger:2024aa}; we use the binary-field adaptation of Diamond and Posen~\cite{Diamond:2024ab}, with encoding via the additive number-theoretic transform (NTT) of Lin, Chung, and Han~\cite{Lin:2014aa}. Tightened soundness analyses are given by Haböck~\cite{Habock:2024ab}.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Fix a power-of-two rate parameter $\mathcal{R} \geq 2$ and a nested chain of additive $\FF{2}$-linear subspaces $\set{0} = E_0 \subset E_1 \subset \cdots \subset E_n \subset K$, where each $E_k$ has dimension $k + \log_2 \mathcal{R}$.

The encoding map $\textsf{Enc}_k \colon K^{2^k} \to K^{|E_k|}$ takes an oracle $\pi \colon \BB_k \to K$ to its evaluation on $E_k$ of the polynomial
\[
  f_\pi(X) \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{2^k - 1} \pi_{\textsf{rev}(i)} \cdot X_i(X),
\]
where $X_0, \ldots, X_{2^k - 1}$ is the novel polynomial basis underlying the Lin--Chung--Han additive NTT~\cite{Lin:2014aa} (the basis of monic polynomials with $\deg X_i = i$ that diagonalizes the additive-NTT butterfly tree), and $\textsf{rev}(i)$ is the $k$-bit reversal of $i \in \set{0, \ldots, 2^k - 1}$. The bit-reversed indexing is a deliberate choice with two consequences. First, it aligns hypercube zero-padding on the message side with codeword duplication on the encoding side, which we use in \S\ref{sec:lifted-basefold} to handle oracles of unequal sizes. Second, it causes BaseFold's sumcheck to specialize high-indexed variables of $\pi$'s multilinear extension first, which yields a more SIMD-friendly prover algorithm than specializing low-indexed variables first.

The constrained Reed--Solomon code at level $n$ is parameterized by a multilinear \emph{multiplicand} $t \colon \BB_n \to K$ and a target $s \in K$; it is the set of codewords $c = \textsf{Enc}_n(\pi)$ for $\pi \colon \BB_n \to K$ satisfying
\[
  \pi \cdot t = s \qquad \text{i.e.,} \qquad \sum_{w \in \BB_n} \pi(w) \cdot t(w) = s.
\]

\paragraph{Protocol.}
On input an oracle $c_n \in K^{|E_n|}$ and a constraint $(t, s)$, BaseFold proceeds in two phases:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Folding phase.} For each round $k = n - 1, \ldots, 0$, the parties interleave one round of a sumcheck on the constraint $\sum_{w \in \BB_n} \pi(w) \cdot t(w) = s$ with a single fold of the codeword. The prover sends a sumcheck round polynomial; the verifier samples $\rho_k \gets K$ and updates the running sumcheck claim; both parties fold the codeword from $c_{k + 1} \in K^{|E_{k + 1}|}$ to $c_k \in K^{|E_k|}$ using $\rho_k$. The prover provides $c_k$ as a new oracle each round.
\item \emph{Query phase.} After $n$ rounds, the codeword $c_0 \in K^{|E_0|}$ is short (of length $\mathcal{R}$) and the sumcheck claim reduces to a single multilinear-extension evaluation that the verifier can check against the prover's claimed reduced codeword. The verifier additionally samples $\mu$ random query positions in $E_n$, and for each position requests the corresponding entries of $c_n, c_{n - 1}, \ldots, c_0$ from the prover oracles. The verifier checks two things at each query position: that the entries are consistent with each successive fold given the challenges $\rho_k$, and that the chain bottoms out in the sent $c_0$.
\end{enumerate}

The verifier accepts iff all sumcheck round-polynomial checks pass, the final reduced claim matches, and all $\mu$ fold-consistency checks pass.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
For target soundness $2^{-\lambda}$, the query count $\mu$ is chosen logarithmic in $\lambda$ and in the inverse proximity gap of the underlying code; concrete settings are given in \cite{Habock:2024ab}. The prover work is $O(2^n \cdot \mathcal{R})$ in $K$, dominated by the initial encoding; the verifier work is $\text{poly}(n) + O(\mu \cdot n)$.

\subsection{Lifted BaseFold}
\label{sec:lifted-basefold}

The Batched BaseFold protocol of \S\ref{sec:batched-basefold} requires the oracles to share a common variable count $\mathbf{n}$. When the LIOP commits oracles of unequal sizes, we lift each smaller oracle's codeword to the level-$\mathbf{n}$ encoding via the \emph{Lifted BaseFold} (a.k.a.~Lifted FRI, after the Fast Reed--Solomon IOPP) construction~\cite{youtube:LiftedFRI}.

We use three operators on $K$-vectors. For $v \in K^N$ with $N = 2^\mu$, and $\eta \geq 0$, define
\begin{align*}
  \textsf{ZeroPadLSB}_\eta(v) &\in K^{N \cdot 2^\eta}, & \textsf{ZeroPadLSB}_\eta(v)_i &= \begin{cases} v_{i / 2^\eta} & \text{if } 2^\eta \mid i, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \\
  \textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_\eta(v) &\in K^{N \cdot 2^\eta}, & \textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_\eta(v)_i &= \begin{cases} v_i & \text{if } i < N, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \\
  \textsf{Duplicate}_\eta(v) &\in K^{N \cdot 2^\eta}, & \textsf{Duplicate}_\eta(v)_i &= v_{\lfloor i / 2^\eta \rfloor}.
\end{align*}
The two zero-padding operators differ in which index bits they zero: $\textsf{ZeroPadLSB}_\eta$ keeps entries only at positions whose low $\eta$ bits vanish, while $\textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_\eta$ keeps entries only at the bottom $N$ indices (positions whose high $\eta$ bits vanish).

The Lin--Chung--Han additive NTT exhibits a clean zero-pad/duplicate correspondence on the LSB side:

\begin{theorem}[LCH NTT zero-pad correspondence]
\label{thm:lch-ntt-zeropad}
For all $\mu, \eta \geq 0$ and any coefficient vector $a \in K^{2^\mu}$ with respect to the novel polynomial basis of \S\ref{sec:basefold},
\[
  \textsf{NTT}_{\mu + \eta}\bigl( \textsf{ZeroPadLSB}_\eta(a) \bigr) = \textsf{Duplicate}_\eta\bigl( \textsf{NTT}_\mu(a) \bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

Now fix an oracle $\pi \colon \BB_n \to K$ with $n \leq \mathbf{n}$, identified with its value vector in $K^{2^n}$ when applying the operators. Its lift $\textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_{\mathbf{n} - n}(\pi) \colon \BB_{\mathbf{n}} \to K$ has multilinear extension
\begin{equation}
  \mle{\textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_{\mathbf{n} - n}(\pi)}(X_0, \ldots, X_{\mathbf{n} - 1}) = \mle{\pi}(X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\mathbf{n} - n}\bigl( 0^{\mathbf{n} - n}, X_n, \ldots, X_{\mathbf{n} - 1} \bigr),
  \label{eq:zeropad-msb-mle}
\end{equation}
where $\mle{\eq}_{\mathbf{n} - n}(0^{\mathbf{n} - n}, Y_0, \ldots, Y_{\mathbf{n} - n - 1}) = \prod_j (1 - Y_j)$ vanishes off the all-zeros high block. The right-hand side is the variable padding of \S\ref{sec:variable-padding}, which is what lets the batched sumcheck of \S\ref{sec:batched-basefold} treat the lifted oracles as claims on a common variable count.

The bit-reversed indexing of \S\ref{sec:basefold} turns end-padding on the hypercube into LSB zero-padding on the coefficient side: the coefficient vector of $\textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_{\mathbf{n} - n}(\pi)$ in the novel basis is exactly $\textsf{ZeroPadLSB}_{\mathbf{n} - n}$ applied to the coefficient vector of $\pi$. Combining with Theorem~\ref{thm:lch-ntt-zeropad}, we obtain the central identity of Lifted BaseFold:
\begin{equation}
  \textsf{Enc}_{\mathbf{n}}\bigl( \textsf{ZeroPadMSB}_{\mathbf{n} - n}(\pi) \bigr) = \textsf{Duplicate}_{\mathbf{n} - n}\bigl( \textsf{Enc}_n(\pi) \bigr).
  \label{eq:lifted-basefold-identity}
\end{equation}
The lifted codeword is therefore the original codeword with each entry duplicated $2^{\mathbf{n} - n}$ times. Given Merkle access to the original codeword $c = \textsf{Enc}_n(\pi)$, the verifier emulates oracle access to the lifted codeword by translating a query at position $e \in E_{\mathbf{n}}$ into a query at $\lfloor e \cdot |E_n| / |E_{\mathbf{n}}| \rfloor \in E_n$.

Using this lift, every LIOP oracle is virtually placed at a common level $\mathbf{n} = \max_i n_i$ (where $n_i$ is the variable count of the $i$-th oracle) before the parties run Batched BaseFold (\S\ref{sec:batched-basefold}). Soundness of the lift requires a refined proximity-gap analysis (the lifted codeword's proximity to the level-$\mathbf{n}$ code is constrained by the original's proximity to the level-$n$ code, with a scaling factor of $2^{\mathbf{n} - n}$); see \cite{youtube:LiftedFRI} for details.

\subsection{Batched BaseFold}
\label{sec:batched-basefold}

The compiler of \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark} runs one BaseFold instance per LIOP linear query. When the LIOP commits multiple oracles with exactly one opening claim per oracle, the openings can be discharged with a single BaseFold instance via two preliminary reductions. We present this batched variant, which is the setting needed for the IronSpartan LIOP of \S\ref{sec:ironspartan}. (The Binius64 LIOP itself uses only one oracle and one opening, so it does not need this machinery.)

Suppose the LIOP holds opening claims
\[
  \langle \pi_i, t_i \rangle = s_i, \qquad i = 0, \ldots, k - 1,
\]
where $\pi_i \colon \BB_{n_i} \to K$ is the $i$-th oracle, $t_i \colon \BB_{n_i} \to K$ is the \emph{transparent} operand---a multilinear the verifier can evaluate on its own, with no help from the prover---and $s_i \in K$ is the target.

\paragraph{Step 1: Batched sumcheck.}
The verifier samples a single batching challenge $\alpha \gets K$. Let $\pi_i^{(\uparrow)}, t_i^{(\uparrow)} \colon \BB_{\mathbf{n}} \to K$ denote the ZeroPadMSB lifts (\S\ref{sec:lifted-basefold}) of $\pi_i, t_i$ to the common variable count $\mathbf{n} \coloneqq \max_i n_i$; the lifts vanish off the original domains, so $\sum_{x \in \BB_{\mathbf{n}}} \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(x)\, \mle{t}_i^{(\uparrow)}(x) = s_i$. The parties run a sumcheck on
\[
  \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha^i s_i \stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{x \in \BB_{\mathbf{n}}} \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha^i \cdot \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(x) \cdot \mle{t}_i^{(\uparrow)}(x).
\]
After $\mathbf{n}$ rounds with sumcheck challenges $r \in K^{\mathbf{n}}$, the batched claim reduces to $\sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha^i \cdot \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(r) \cdot \mle{t}_i^{(\uparrow)}(r)$. The verifier evaluates each $\mle{t}_i^{(\uparrow)}(r)$ on its own (via the factorization \eqref{eq:zeropad-msb-mle}). The remaining unknowns are the prover-claimed evaluations
\[
  \alpha_i \coloneqq \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(r), \qquad i = 0, \ldots, k - 1.
\]

\paragraph{Step 2: Random reduction to a piecewise-concatenated oracle.}
To verify the $k$ evaluation claims $\alpha_i$ via a single BaseFold instance, we treat the $\pi_i^{(\uparrow)}$ as restrictions of a single \emph{piecewise concatenated} multilinear
\[
  \boldsymbol{\pi}(X, Y) \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(X) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\log_2 k}(i, Y),
\]
in $X \in K^{\mathbf{n}}$ and $\log_2 k$ fresh oracle-index variables $Y \in K^{\log_2 k}$, so that $\boldsymbol{\pi}(X, i) = \mle{\pi}_i^{(\uparrow)}(X)$ for each $i \in \BB_{\log_2 k}$.

The verifier samples $r' \gets K^{\log_2 k}$ and computes the target
\[
  s' \coloneqq \sum_{i = 0}^{k - 1} \alpha_i \cdot \mle{\eq}_{\log_2 k}(i, r') = \boldsymbol{\pi}(r, r').
\]
The parties then run a single BaseFold instance on $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ at the point $(r, r')$ with target $s'$. The $k$ separately-committed codewords of $\pi_0, \ldots, \pi_{k - 1}$ from the LIOP together form the virtual oracle for $\boldsymbol{\pi}$: a BaseFold query at position $(e, y) \in E_\mathbf{n} \times \BB_{\log_2 k}$ is answered by querying the codeword of $\pi_y$ at position $e$, with the Merkle authentication path drawn from the original $\pi_y$ commitment.

\subsection{The Binius64 SNARK}
\label{sec:binius64-snark}

The Binius64 SNARK is the result of applying the compiler of \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark} to the Binius64 LIOP of \S\ref{sec:binius-liop}. The LIOP has a single oracle $\pi = \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w) \colon \BB_{\ell_\text{pack}} \to K$ and ends with a single succinct linear query $(t, s)$, namely the random combination of the ring-switching and public-input-check queries (\S\ref{sec:public-input-check}). The compiler instantiates this LIOP query with a single BaseFold IOPP instance on the constrained Reed--Solomon code (RS code over $K$ with the constraint $\sum_{y} t(y) \cdot \pi(y) = s$), and applies BCS and Fiat--Shamir on top.

Concretely, the non-interactive proof consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Merkle root of $\textsf{Enc}_{\ell_\text{pack}}(\pi)$, sent at the start (commitment to the oracle);
\item the LIOP's non-oracle messages (the univariate polynomial $g(\widehat{I})$ of the BitAnd reduction, GKR-step claimed evaluations and Frobenius-step values of the IntMul reduction, the per-bit column values of the BinMul reduction, the round polynomials of the various sumchecks, the vector $\hat{s}$ of the ring-switching step), with verifier challenges Fiat--Shamir-derived from the running transcript;
\item the BaseFold transcript verifying the LIOP's final linear query: Merkle roots of the folded codewords, the sumcheck round polynomials of BaseFold's folding phase, and the Merkle openings answering the $\mu$ query positions.
\end{itemize}

The verifier reconstructs each LIOP and BaseFold challenge by hashing the transcript prefix in the canonical Fiat--Shamir order, runs each LIOP verifier-side check, and runs the BaseFold verifier on the IOPP transcript.

\paragraph{Soundness.}
Combining the soundness errors of the LIOP, BaseFold, and BCS gives an overall soundness error of $O\!\left( \ell_\text{words} + \ell_\text{cons} \right) / |K| + 2^{-\lambda}$ in the random oracle model, where the first term collects the various sumcheck and Schwartz--Zippel errors of \S\ref{sec:binand}--\S\ref{sec:public-input-check} and the second term is the BaseFold soundness floor. Knowledge soundness follows from the same compilation chain with knowledge extraction at each stage.

\section{The IronSpartan LIOP}
\label{sec:ironspartan}

The Binius64 LIOP of \S\ref{sec:binius-liop} is specialized to the BitAnd, IntMul, and BinMul constraint system. We now specify a second LIOP, \emph{IronSpartan}, for the simple language of rank-one constraint systems (R1CS). IronSpartan adapts the Spartan PIOP of Setty~\cite{Setty:2020aa} to the LIOP framework of \S\ref{sec:liop}, and presents it as a \emph{commit-and-prove} argument: the assignment is split into a public, a precommit, and a witness segment, and the prover commits the precommit segment as an oracle \emph{before} the public instance is fixed. Where Spartan answers its single witness-evaluation query with a polynomial commitment scheme, IronSpartan phrases the analogous claim as linear queries on the committed segment oracles, to be discharged by the batched compiler of \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}. The construction is moreover honest-verifier zero-knowledge (HVZK), so composed with the Batched ZK BaseFold compiler of \S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold} it yields a zero-knowledge commit-and-prove SNARK for R1CS.

\subsection{R1CS and the Spartan PIOP}
\label{sec:r1cs-spartan}

IronSpartan proves satisfaction of a rank-one constraint system in \emph{commit-and-prove} form, over an assignment partitioned into three \emph{data segments}
\[
  z = (z_{\textsf{pub}}, z_{\textsf{pre}}, z_{\textsf{wit}}),
\]
indexed by $\sigma \in \set{\textsf{pub}, \textsf{pre}, \textsf{wit}}$ -- the \emph{public}, \emph{precommit}, and \emph{witness} segments, of sizes $n_{\textsf{pub}}, n_{\textsf{pre}}, n_{\textsf{wit}}$. The public segment $z_{\textsf{pub}}$ carries the constant $1$ together with the public inputs and is held by the verifier; the precommit and witness segments are private and are committed as oracles. The defining feature of the commit-and-prove setting is that the prover sends the precommit oracle $z_{\textsf{pre}}$ \emph{before} the public instance $z_{\textsf{pub}}$ is fixed: the precommitted data cannot depend on the statement, and one precommitment may be reused across many statements.

The constraint system is specified by \emph{nine wiring matrices} $W_{\textsf{op}, \sigma} \in K^{n_M \times n_\sigma}$, one per operator $\textsf{op} \in \set{A, B, C}$ and segment $\sigma$. Concatenating the segments columnwise recovers the usual R1CS matrices, $A = [\,W_{A, \textsf{pub}} \mid W_{A, \textsf{pre}} \mid W_{A, \textsf{wit}}\,]$ and likewise for $B$ and $C$, so that each matrix--vector product splits across segments as
\[
  \textsf{op} \cdot z = \sum_{\sigma} W_{\textsf{op}, \sigma}\, z_\sigma, \qquad \textsf{op} \in \set{A, B, C}.
\]
The assignment \emph{satisfies} the instance if
\[
  (Az) \circ (Bz) = Cz,
\]
where $\circ$ denotes the entry-wise product. We pad $n_M$ and each segment size $n_\sigma$ to powers of two, $n_M = 2^{\ell_M}$ and $n_\sigma = 2^{\ell_\sigma}$, indexing constraints (matrix rows) by $\BB_{\ell_M}$ and the entries of segment $\sigma$ (matrix columns) by $\BB_{\ell_\sigma}$.

\paragraph{Multilinear encoding.}
Each wiring matrix $W_{\textsf{op}, \sigma} \colon \BB_{\ell_M} \times \BB_{\ell_\sigma} \to K$ and each segment $z_\sigma \colon \BB_{\ell_\sigma} \to K$ has a multilinear extension, written $\mle{W}_{\textsf{op}, \sigma}(X, Y)$ and $\mle{z}_\sigma(Y)$. For $M \in \set{A, B, C}$, write
\[
  \mle{(Mz)}(X) \coloneqq \sum_{\sigma} \sum_{y \in \BB_{\ell_\sigma}} \mle{W}_{M, \sigma}(X, y)\, \mle{z}_\sigma(y)
\]
for the multilinear extension of the product vector $Mz$. The relation holds iff $\mle{(Az)}(x) \cdot \mle{(Bz)}(x) = \mle{(Cz)}(x)$ for every constraint $x \in \BB_{\ell_M}$.

\paragraph{The Spartan PIOP.}
After committing the assignment, Spartan~\cite{Setty:2020aa} verifies this relation in two sumcheck stages.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Constraint check.} The verifier samples $\tau \gets K^{\ell_M}$, and the parties run an MLE-check (\S\ref{sec:notation}) on
\[
  \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_M}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_M}(\tau, x) \cdot \bigl[ \mle{(Az)}(x)\, \mle{(Bz)}(x) - \mle{(Cz)}(x) \bigr] = 0.
\]
This reduces to a random point $r_x \in K^{\ell_M}$ at which the prover sends the three claimed products $a = \mle{(Az)}(r_x)$, $b = \mle{(Bz)}(r_x)$, $c = \mle{(Cz)}(r_x)$, for which the verifier checks $a \cdot b = c$.
\item \textbf{Wiring check.} The verifier samples $\gamma \gets K$ and a second MLE-check batches the three products into a single evaluation query on the committed assignment.
\end{enumerate}
Spartan answers the resulting evaluation query with a polynomial commitment scheme; IronSpartan replaces this stage, as we now describe.

\subsection{IronSpartan Overview}
\label{sec:ironspartan-overview}

IronSpartan departs from Spartan in how the wiring check is discharged, exploiting the segment structure to keep the public data out of the committed oracles. For the batching challenge $\gamma$, define for each segment the \emph{batched wiring operand}
\[
  t_\sigma(Y) \coloneqq \mle{W}_{A, \sigma}(r_x, Y) + \gamma\, \mle{W}_{B, \sigma}(r_x, Y) + \gamma^2\, \mle{W}_{C, \sigma}(r_x, Y), \qquad \sigma \in \set{\textsf{pub}, \textsf{pre}, \textsf{wit}},
\]
a multilinear on $\BB_{\ell_\sigma}$ that the verifier evaluates from the public wiring matrices and the challenges $r_x, \gamma$. Batching the products $a, b, c$ with $\gamma$ and splitting across segments,
\[
  a + \gamma b + \gamma^2 c = \sum_{\sigma} \langle z_\sigma, t_\sigma \rangle = \langle z_{\textsf{pub}}, t_{\textsf{pub}} \rangle + \langle z_{\textsf{pre}}, t_{\textsf{pre}} \rangle + \langle z_{\textsf{wit}}, t_{\textsf{wit}} \rangle.
\]
The public summand the verifier computes on its own; the precommit and witness summands are \emph{linear queries} (\S\ref{sec:liop}) on the committed oracles $z_{\textsf{pre}}$ and $z_{\textsf{wit}}$, with operands $t_{\textsf{pre}}$ and $t_{\textsf{wit}}$. As in the Binius64 case these operands are \emph{transparent} -- the verifier evaluates each $t_\sigma$ from the public wiring matrices -- but, unlike there, they are not succinct: evaluating $t_\sigma$ reads the wiring matrices. IronSpartan does not require succinctness. It therefore omits Spartan's second sumcheck entirely, emitting the wiring claim as linear queries that the compiler (\S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}) verifies against the Reed--Solomon encodings of the segment oracles. The constraint check is retained as in Spartan.

\paragraph{Splitting the target.}
The constraint check fixes only the total $s \coloneqq a + \gamma b + \gamma^2 c$, whereas the compiler needs a separate target for each oracle query. Once $\gamma$ is sampled, the prover sends a single field element, the precommit contribution
\[
  s_{\textsf{pre}} \coloneqq \langle z_{\textsf{pre}}, t_{\textsf{pre}} \rangle.
\]
The verifier computes the public contribution $s_{\textsf{pub}} \coloneqq \langle z_{\textsf{pub}}, t_{\textsf{pub}} \rangle$ on its own and recovers the witness target as the residual
\[
  s_{\textsf{wit}} \coloneqq s - s_{\textsf{pub}} - s_{\textsf{pre}}.
\]
IronSpartan then emits two linear queries, $(t_{\textsf{pre}}, s_{\textsf{pre}})$ on $z_{\textsf{pre}}$ and $(t_{\textsf{wit}}, s_{\textsf{wit}})$ on $z_{\textsf{wit}}$. Together with the verifier's public computation these pin the full inner product: a prover satisfying both queries satisfies $\sum_\sigma \langle z_\sigma, t_\sigma \rangle = s$. The extra element $s_{\textsf{pre}}$ costs no soundness, as it is itself certified by the precommit query.

\paragraph{Toward zero knowledge.}
Three leakage sources must be closed to make IronSpartan honest-verifier zero-knowledge:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{constraint-check sumcheck}, whose round polynomials depend on the committed segments; we mask it with a Libra-style masking polynomial committed as an auxiliary oracle $\pi_g$ (\S\ref{sec:zk-sumcheck-machinery});
\item the \emph{oracle openings} of $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, $z_{\textsf{wit}}$, and $\pi_g$ performed by the compiler's BaseFold instance; these are blinded by the padding and mask of the Batched ZK BaseFold compiler (\S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold});
\item the revealed products $(a, b, c)$, witness-dependent linear images of the assignment; we mask these with dummy constraints (\S\ref{sec:dummy-masking}).
\end{itemize}
The first and third are intrinsic to the LIOP and are treated below; the second is supplied by the compiler. Instantiated over binary fields via the binary BaseFold of \S\ref{sec:basefold} (FRI-Binius~\cite{Diamond:2025ab}), the result runs over the same fields as the rest of this document.

\subsection{Zero-Knowledge Sumcheck Machinery}
\label{sec:zk-sumcheck-machinery}

IronSpartan's constraint check (\S\ref{sec:constraint-check-reduction}) is a sumcheck, and the standard sumcheck protocol is not zero-knowledge: each round polynomial is a partial sum of the witness-dependent summand and leaks information about it. We recall the masking technique of Libra~\cite{Xie:2019aa}, which restores zero knowledge in odd characteristic but \emph{fails} in characteristic $2$ -- the case of interest, since $K$ has characteristic $2$ -- and then show that the \emph{multilinear-extension-check} form of IronSpartan's constraint check repairs the masking in every characteristic. The honest-verifier zero-knowledge statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:zk-mle-check}, proved in Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck}.

Throughout, $f \in K[X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}]$ is an $n$-variate polynomial of individual degree at most $d$, and we treat the sumcheck claim $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} f(x) = s_f$. In round $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$ the prover binds $X_{n - i - 1}$ and sends the univariate \emph{round polynomial}
\[
  R_f^{(i)}(X) \coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_{n - i - 1}} f(v, X, r_{n - i}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}), \qquad \deg R_f^{(i)} \le d,
\]
where $r_{n - i}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}$ are the prior challenges; the verifier checks $R_f^{(i)}(0) + R_f^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$ against the running claim (with $s^{(0)} = s_f$), samples $r_{n - i - 1} \gets K$, and sets $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R_f^{(i)}(r_{n - i - 1})$. Transmitting $d$ of the $d + 1$ coefficients of each $R_f^{(i)}$ suffices, the last being fixed by the consistency check; these coefficients, with $s_f$, are the messages a zero-knowledge variant must hide.

\subsubsection{Libra and Its Failure in Characteristic 2}
\label{sec:libra}

Libra masks the sumcheck by additively blending in a random polynomial. The prover samples a \emph{masking polynomial}
\[
  g(X_0, \ldots, X_{n - 1}) = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} g_j(X_j) \cdot X_j, \qquad g_j(X) = \sum_{k = 0}^{d - 1} g_{j, k} X^k,
\]
with constant $g_c \in K$ and random univariates $g_j$ of degree $\le d - 1$, so $g$ has individual degree $\le d$ and $1 + nd$ coefficients. It announces $s_g \coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_n} g(v)$; the verifier samples a blending challenge $\alpha \gets K$; and the parties sumcheck the combined claim
\[
  s_f + \alpha\, s_g = \sum_{x \in \BB_n} (f + \alpha\, g)(x),
\]
with round polynomials $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha\, R_g^{(i)}$. After the final challenge the prover reveals $g^* \coloneqq g(r)$ at $r = (r_0, \ldots, r_{n - 1})$, and the verifier recovers the unmasked value $f^* \coloneqq s^{(n)} - \alpha\, g^*$.

\paragraph{Committing the mask.}
Since $g$ has individual degree $d > 1$ it is not multilinear and cannot be an LIOP oracle directly; the prover commits a multilinear proxy for its coefficients. Put
\[
  \nu \coloneqq \lceil \log_2 n \rceil, \qquad \delta \coloneqq \lceil \log_2 (d + 1) \rceil,
\]
and let $g' \colon \BB_\nu \times \BB_\delta \to K$ be a uniformly random multilinear, committed as the oracle $\pi_g \coloneqq g'$. Identifying $\BB_\nu \cong \set{0, \ldots, 2^\nu - 1}$ and $\BB_\delta \cong \set{0, \ldots, 2^\delta - 1}$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}), the prover reads the mask coefficients off $g'$,
\[
  g_c \coloneqq \sum_{j \in \BB_\nu} g'(j, 0), \qquad g_{j, k - 1} \coloneqq g'(j, k) \quad (j < n,\ 1 \le k \le d),
\]
the other entries of $g'$ remaining slack. To evaluate $g$ at the sumcheck point $r$, define the multilinear $\textsf{libra-eval}_r \colon \BB_\nu \times \BB_\delta \to K$ by
\[
  \textsf{libra-eval}_r(j, k) \coloneqq \begin{cases} r_j^{\,k} & \text{if } j < n \text{ and } k \le d, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}
\]
with $r_j$ the $j$-th coordinate of $r$. Expanding,
\[
  \langle g', \textsf{libra-eval}_r \rangle = \sum_{(j, k) \in \BB_{\nu + \delta}} g'(j, k)\, \textsf{libra-eval}_r(j, k) = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} g_j(r_j)\, r_j = g(r),
\]
so the claim $g^* = g(r)$ is a linear query on $\pi_g$ with operand $\textsf{libra-eval}_r$ and target $g^*$, which the verifier evaluates in $O(nd)$ operations.

\begin{protocol}[Zero-knowledge sumcheck via Libra masking]
\label{prot:zk-sumcheck-libra}
\emph{Claim:} $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} f(x) = s_f$, for $f$ of individual degree $\le d$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Masking.} The prover samples $g' \gets K^{\BB_{\nu + \delta}}$, commits $\pi_g \coloneqq g'$, derives $g$ as above, and sends $s_g = \sum_{v \in \BB_n} g(v)$. The verifier samples $\alpha \gets K$ and sets $s^{(0)} \coloneqq s_f + \alpha\, s_g$.
\item \emph{Rounds.} For $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$: the prover sends all but one coefficient of $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha\, R_g^{(i)}$; the verifier recovers the last from $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$, samples $r_{n - i - 1} \gets K$, and sets $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_{n - i - 1})$.
\item \emph{Reduction.} The prover reveals $g^* = g(r)$. The verifier forms $f^* \coloneqq s^{(n)} - \alpha\, g^*$ and outputs the evaluation claim $f^* = f(r)$ on $f$ and the linear query $g^* = \langle g', \textsf{libra-eval}_r \rangle$ on $\pi_g$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\paragraph{Failure in characteristic 2.}
The masking messages -- $s_g$ and the transmitted coefficients of the $R_g^{(i)}$ -- form a fixed $K$-linear image $L_r$ of the $1 + nd$ coefficients of $g$. Expanding over the hypercube,
\[
  s_g = 2^n g_c + 2^{n - 1} \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} g_j(1), \qquad R_g^{(i)}(X) = 2^{n - i - 1}\, g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X + (\text{terms in } g_c \text{ and the } g_j,\ j \neq n - i - 1),
\]
so the message block carrying the fresh univariate $g_{n - i - 1}$ is scaled by $2^{n - i - 1}$, and $L_r$ is block lower-triangular with these powers of two on its diagonal. In odd characteristic the diagonal entries are units, $L_r$ is invertible, and a uniform mask yields uniform messages (Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck}). In characteristic $2$, however, $2^k = 0$ for $k > 0$: the diagonal collapses for every round $i < n - 1$, the rank of $L_r$ falls to $1 + d$, and the non-constant coefficients of the first $n - 1$ round polynomials are left unmasked. Libra masking thus gives no zero knowledge over $K$ when the sum runs over the Boolean hypercube.

\paragraph{Repair over the infinity hypercube.}
The vanishing factors are the vertex counts $2^{n - i - 1}$ of the unbound low coordinates. Over the infinity hypercube $\set{0, \infty}^n$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}) each unbound coordinate contributes $\sum_{v \in \set{0, \infty}} 1 = 1$ instead of $2$, and a low term $g_j(v_j)\, v_j$ contributes only its top coefficient $g_{j, d - 1}$, so
\[
  s_g = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} g_{j, d - 1}, \qquad R_g^{(i)}(X) = g_c + \sum_{j < n - i - 1} g_{j, d - 1} + g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X + \sum_{j > n - i - 1} g_j(r_j)\, r_j,
\]
with the fresh univariate $g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X$ carried with \emph{unit} coefficient in every round. Its degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients are exactly those of $g_{n - i - 1}$, so $L_r$ is a bijection in every characteristic (Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform}) and Protocol~\ref{prot:zk-sumcheck-libra}, run over $\set{0, \infty}^n$ with the consistency check $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(\infty) = s^{(i)}$, is hiding over $K$. This is the form the zero-knowledge compiler uses for its degree-$2$ batch sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}). IronSpartan's own constraint check instead stays on the Boolean hypercube and repairs the masking differently, as we now describe.

\subsubsection{Zero-Knowledge Multilinear-Extension Check}
\label{sec:zk-mle-check}

IronSpartan's constraint check is not a bare sumcheck but a \emph{multilinear-extension check} (\S\ref{sec:notation}): the summand carries an explicit equality-indicator factor, the claim being $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} \mle{\eq}_n(\tau, x)\, f(x) = s_f$ for a verifier challenge $\tau \in K^n$. Masking it with the same $g, g', \alpha$ -- now summing the $\mle{\eq}_n(\tau, \cdot)$-weighted combination -- repairs the masking in every characteristic, following Gruen~\cite{Gruen:2024aa}. The equality-indicator factor is pulled out of the round polynomials, so each $R^{(i)}$ stays of degree $\le d$ and the round relation becomes the \emph{$\mle{\eq}$-weighted} consistency
\[
  s^{(i)} = (1 - \tau_{n - i - 1})\, R^{(i)}(0) + \tau_{n - i - 1}\, R^{(i)}(1), \qquad s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_{n - i - 1}),
\]
in place of the unweighted $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$ of the bare sumcheck.

\begin{protocol}[Zero-knowledge multilinear-extension check]
\label{prot:zk-mle-check}
\emph{Claim:} $\sum_{x \in \BB_n} \mle{\eq}_n(\tau, x)\, f(x) = s_f$, for a challenge $\tau \in K^n$ and $f$ of individual degree $\le d$.

The protocol is Protocol~\ref{prot:zk-sumcheck-libra} with every hypercube sum weighted by $\mle{\eq}_n(\tau, \cdot)$: the prover sends $s_g = \sum_{v \in \BB_n} \mle{\eq}_n(\tau, v)\, g(v)$, and $R_f^{(i)}, R_g^{(i)}$ are the equality-indicator-factored round polynomials of the weighted summand, each of degree $\le d$, computed by the MLE-check prover of \cite{Gruen:2024aa}. In round $i$ the prover sends the degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients of $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha\, R_g^{(i)}$; the verifier recovers the constant coefficient from $s^{(i)} = (1 - \tau_{n - i - 1})\, R^{(i)}(0) + \tau_{n - i - 1}\, R^{(i)}(1)$, samples $r_{n - i - 1} \gets K$, and sets $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_{n - i - 1})$. The reduction is unchanged: it outputs the evaluation claim $f^* = f(r)$ on $f$ and the linear query $g^* = \langle g', \textsf{libra-eval}_r \rangle$ on $\pi_g$.
\end{protocol}

\paragraph{Why it works.}
With the equality-indicator factor pulled out, the as-yet-unbound low variables are summed against $\mle{\eq}_{n - i - 1}(\tau_{< n - i - 1}, \cdot)$, contributing the factor $\sum_{x_{< n - i - 1}} \mle{\eq}_{n - i - 1}(\tau_{< n - i - 1}, x_{< n - i - 1}) = 1$ -- in place of the $\sum_{x_{< n - i - 1}} 1 = 2^{n - i - 1}$ of the bare sumcheck. The masking contributions are therefore
\[
  s_g = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} \tau_j\, g_j(1), \qquad R_g^{(i)}(X) = g_c + \sum_{j > n - i - 1} g_j(r_j)\, r_j + \sum_{j < n - i - 1} \tau_j\, g_j(1) + g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X.
\]
The fresh univariate appears as $g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X$ with \emph{unit} coefficient, rather than scaled by the vanishing power of two $2^{n - i - 1}$. Its degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients are exactly those of $g_{n - i - 1}$, so the round message hides the corresponding coefficients of $f$ in every characteristic; the masking is in fact perfect once the blending challenge $\alpha$ is nonzero (Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck}).

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:zk-mle-check}
The zero-knowledge multilinear-extension check (Protocol~\ref{prot:zk-mle-check}) is honest-verifier zero-knowledge: there is an efficient simulator whose transcript, given the public claim $(\tau, s_f)$ and the reduced value $f^*$, is statistically $O(1)/|K|$-close to an honest execution. The same holds for the bare-sumcheck masking of Protocol~\ref{prot:zk-sumcheck-libra} run over the infinity hypercube $\set{0, \infty}^n$; over the Boolean hypercube the bare-sumcheck masking is honest-verifier zero-knowledge only when $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq 2$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck}. This masked multilinear-extension check is what IronSpartan uses for its constraint check (\S\ref{sec:constraint-check-reduction}).

\subsection{Dummy-Constraint Masking}
\label{sec:dummy-masking}

Masking the sumcheck and the oracle openings still leaves one leak: the constraint check reveals the products $(a, b, c)$, which are witness-dependent linear images of the assignment evaluated at the random point $r_x$. We neutralize this by enlarging the instance with \emph{dummy constraints} carrying fresh random values, in the manner of Diamond~\cite{Diamond:2025ac}. Concretely, we append two multiplication constraints whose operands are fresh witness-segment variables assigned uniformly random field elements consistent with the multiplicative relation, occupying two otherwise-unused matrix rows and six fresh columns of the witness segment (two operands and one product per dummy constraint). The wiring extensions $\mle{W}_{A, \textsf{wit}}(r_x, \cdot), \mle{W}_{B, \textsf{wit}}(r_x, \cdot), \mle{W}_{C, \textsf{wit}}(r_x, \cdot)$ then carry random components supported on the dummy columns, rendering the revealed $(a, b, c)$ uniform subject to $a \cdot b = c$ and independent of the real witness.

\subsection{The IronSpartan LIOP}
\label{sec:ironspartan-liop}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The setup pads the constraint count and each segment to power-of-two dimensions, reserving room for the dummy constraints. It chooses $\ell_M$ and the $\ell_\sigma$ with
\[
  2^{\ell_M} \ge n_M + 2, \qquad 2^{\ell_{\textsf{pub}}} \ge n_{\textsf{pub}}, \qquad 2^{\ell_{\textsf{pre}}} \ge n_{\textsf{pre}}, \qquad 2^{\ell_{\textsf{wit}}} \ge n_{\textsf{wit}} + 6.
\]
The two extra constraint rows hold the dummy constraints of \S\ref{sec:dummy-masking}, and the six extra witness-segment columns hold their fresh variables. The segments reserve no randomizable support: the randomness that hides the codeword openings is appended by the Batched ZK BaseFold compiler (\S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}) outside the segments. Remaining padding entries are zero.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
In keeping with the commit-and-prove setting, the prover first commits the precommit oracle $z_{\textsf{pre}}$. Once the public instance $z_{\textsf{pub}}$ is fixed, the prover commits the witness oracle $z_{\textsf{wit}}$ and the Libra oracle $\pi_g$ (the masking coefficients $g'$ of \S\ref{sec:zk-mle-check}). The parties then run the constraint-check reduction (\S\ref{sec:constraint-check-reduction}) followed by the reduction to inner-product claims (\S\ref{sec:ip-reduction}), terminating in linear queries on $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, $z_{\textsf{wit}}$, and $\pi_g$ -- three oracles with one query each, exactly the setting of Batched BaseFold (\S\ref{sec:batched-basefold}).

\subsubsection{Constraint-Check Reduction}
\label{sec:constraint-check-reduction}

The constraint-check reduction is the zero-knowledge MLE-check of \S\ref{sec:zk-mle-check} applied to the R1CS constraint polynomial.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples $\tau \gets K^{\ell_M}$.
\item The parties run a zero-knowledge MLE-check, masked by the Libra polynomial committed in $\pi_g$, on
\[
  \sum_{x \in \BB_{\ell_M}} \mle{\eq}_{\ell_M}(\tau, x) \cdot \bigl[ \mle{(Az)}(x)\, \mle{(Bz)}(x) - \mle{(Cz)}(x) \bigr] = 0.
\]
\item The check reduces to a random point $r_x \in K^{\ell_M}$, at which the prover sends the claimed products $a = \mle{(Az)}(r_x)$, $b = \mle{(Bz)}(r_x)$, $c = \mle{(Cz)}(r_x)$ of \S\ref{sec:r1cs-spartan}; the verifier checks $a \cdot b = c$ and samples a batching challenge $\gamma \gets K$.
\end{enumerate}
The reduction outputs the claimed products $(a, b, c)$, the point $r_x$, the challenge $\gamma$, and a Libra evaluation claim -- a linear query on $\pi_g$ (operand $\textsf{libra-eval}_r$, target $g^*$).

\subsubsection{Reduction to Inner-Product Claims on Matrix Extensions}
\label{sec:ip-reduction}

It remains to certify the three products $(a, b, c)$. As in \S\ref{sec:ironspartan-overview}, IronSpartan batches them with $\gamma$ and splits the inner product across segments using the operands $t_\sigma$. The prover sends the precommit contribution $s_{\textsf{pre}} = \langle z_{\textsf{pre}}, t_{\textsf{pre}} \rangle$; the verifier computes the public contribution $s_{\textsf{pub}} = \langle z_{\textsf{pub}}, t_{\textsf{pub}} \rangle$ and forms the witness target $s_{\textsf{wit}} = (a + \gamma b + \gamma^2 c) - s_{\textsf{pub}} - s_{\textsf{pre}}$. The LIOP thus terminates with three linear queries -- $(t_{\textsf{pre}}, s_{\textsf{pre}})$ on $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, $(t_{\textsf{wit}}, s_{\textsf{wit}})$ on $z_{\textsf{wit}}$, and the Libra query on $\pi_g$ -- whose transparent operands the verifier evaluates from the public matrices. The compiler of \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler} discharges all three with a single Batched BaseFold instance. Replacing it with the Batched ZK BaseFold compiler of \S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}, together with the dummy-constraint masking established above, makes the compiled argument honest-verifier zero-knowledge; the full simulator is given in \S\ref{sec:zk-compiler}.

\section{Zero-Knowledge Compiler}
\label{sec:zk-compiler}

\subsection{Proof Composition for ZK-SNARKs}
\label{sec:proof-composition}

Our zero-knowledge SNARK is obtained by \emph{proof composition}: an arbitrary, not-necessarily-zero-knowledge LIOP for a relation $R$ is wrapped inside the zero-knowledge commit-and-prove IronSpartan LIOP of \S\ref{sec:ironspartan}, yielding a zero-knowledge LIOP for the same relation $R$. The strategy instantiates the classical paradigm of Ben-Or et al.~\cite{10.5555/88314.88333} -- every interactive proof can be made zero-knowledge by having the prover commit (encrypt) its messages and then prove, in zero knowledge, that the verifier's acceptance predicate holds on the committed messages. We follow the concrete realizations of Frigo and shelat~\cite{Frigo:2024aa} and of VEIL~\cite{cryptoeprint:2026/683}, which specialize this paradigm to the hash-based multilinear proof systems compiled in \S\ref{sec:basic-snark-compiler}. IronSpartan is the outer, zero-knowledge proof: its R1CS commit-and-prove interface is exactly what is needed to certify the inner verifier's predicate without revealing the inner prover's messages.

We call the wrapped protocol the \emph{inner} LIOP, and its prover and verifier the inner prover and inner verifier. The composition encrypts every value the inner prover would reveal under a one-time pad, so it requires the number of such values to be \emph{predetermined} -- a fixed function of the relation $R$ rather than of the witness. There are two kinds, each needing its own OTP key: the non-oracle field elements the inner prover sends during the protocol, and the targets of the linear queries its verifier reduces to. Both counts are predetermined for the LIOPs of this document. The two protocols are then interleaved as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Precommit.} Before the public instance is fixed, the composed prover commits IronSpartan's precommit segment $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, which holds one independent uniform \emph{one-time-pad (OTP) key} for each non-oracle field element the inner prover will send, and one for each linear-query target the inner verifier will produce. Because both counts are predetermined and the precommit segment may be fixed ahead of the statement (\S\ref{sec:ironspartan}), this commitment is well defined.

\item \textbf{Run the inner LIOP.} The composed prover runs the inner prover against the public-coin verifier. Whenever the inner prover would send a non-oracle field element $m$, the composed prover instead sends the ciphertext $c \coloneqq m + k$, where $k$ is the OTP key precommitted for that element. Since each $k$ is uniform and used once, every $c$ is uniform and independent of $m$, so the inner prover's non-oracle messages leak nothing. The inner oracles, meanwhile, are committed as oracles of the composed LIOP, to be discharged by the compiler of \S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}.

\item \textbf{Run IronSpartan.} Finally, the composed prover invokes the IronSpartan LIOP to prove that the plaintexts $m = c - k$ -- with the ciphertexts $c$ public and the keys $k$ read from the precommit segment -- satisfy exactly the algebraic relations that the inner verifier would check on the inner prover's messages. These checks are low-degree in the transmitted elements and the (public) challenges, so they form an R1CS instance over $(z_{\textsf{pre}}, z_{\textsf{wit}})$ that IronSpartan discharges in zero knowledge.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Hiding the query targets.}
The inner verifier's predicate reduces to linear queries $\langle \pi, t \rangle = s$ on the inner oracles. The operand $t$ depends only on public challenges, but the target $s$ is a witness-dependent linear image of $\pi$, so the composed prover never reveals it. It sends the ciphertext $c \coloneqq s + k$ under the key $k$ precommitted for this target, the R1CS of Step~3 asserts $s = c - k$, and the compiler receives the query
\begin{equation}
  \langle \pi, t \rangle + \langle z_{\textsf{pre}}, e_k \rangle = c,
  \label{eq:target-relation}
\end{equation}
with $e_k$ the indicator of the key's index in $z_{\textsf{pre}}$ and the public target $c$. IronSpartan binds $s = c - k$ to the committed $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, and the compiler binds $\langle \pi, t \rangle + k = c$ to the committed $\pi$ and $z_{\textsf{pre}}$; together they pin $\langle \pi, t \rangle = s$. The key must live in $z_{\textsf{pre}}$: one drawn from $\pi$ itself would not bind, since IronSpartan cannot read entries of $\pi$.

\subsection{Batched ZK BaseFold}
\label{sec:batched-zk-basefold}

The compiler of \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark} is not zero-knowledge: BaseFold's query phase reveals codeword symbols of the committed oracles, and its folding phase reveals sumcheck round polynomials and folded codewords that depend on those oracles. We now give an \emph{alternate} compiler, \emph{Batched ZK BaseFold}, with the stronger guarantee that whenever the source LIOP is honest-verifier zero-knowledge (HVZK), the compiled interactive proof is HVZK as well -- and hence, after Fiat--Shamir, a non-interactive zero-knowledge argument in the random oracle model. It follows the construction of Diamond~\cite{Diamond:2025ac}, itself inspired by the low-degree-test masking technique of Aurora~\cite{Ben-Sasson:2019ab}, adapted to the batched setting of \S\ref{sec:batched-basefold}, and it replaces the Batched BaseFold instantiation of Stage~1 in the compiler of \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark}, leaving the BCS and Fiat--Shamir stages untouched. It also merges the oracles of each interaction round into one commitment and accepts any number of queries, each spanning any number of oracles.

\paragraph{Input.}
The source LIOP's prover sends oracles $\pi_i \colon \BB_{n_i} \to K$, each flagged \emph{witness-dependent} or not (an oracle fixed by the public instance, such as a precomputed table, is not), and its verifier closes with linear queries (\S\ref{sec:liop})
\[
  \sum_i \langle \pi_i, t_{j, i} \rangle = s_j, \qquad j = 0, \ldots, J - 1,
\]
each involving any number of oracles. The oracles carry no randomizable support of their own; the compiler appends the padding it needs.

\paragraph{Leakage channels.}
Four kinds of message in the compiled protocol depend on the oracles, and we hide each with its own source of randomness.
\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{query phase} opens each committed codeword at $q$ verifier-chosen positions (the parameter $\mu$ of \S\ref{sec:basefold}), exposing symbols of $\textsf{Enc}(\pi_i)$. These are hidden by a \emph{ZK pad}: uniform coordinates the compiler appends to each committed message.
\item The \emph{batch sumcheck} that reduces the queries to evaluation claims reveals round polynomials. These are hidden by a Libra mask (\S\ref{sec:libra}), which the compiler commits as one further oracle.
\item The \emph{evaluation claims} passed between the phases are linear images of the oracles. These are hidden by the ZK pad as well.
\item The \emph{folding phase} of BaseFold reveals round polynomials and folded codewords. These are hidden by a \emph{ZK mask}: an independent uniform codeword committed alongside each oracle, in the manner of Aurora.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Round merging.}
A \emph{round} of the LIOP is the set of oracles the prover sends between two consecutive verifier challenges. The compiler commits each round as one \emph{merged oracle}. Index the rounds by $r$, and let the round's constituents be $\pi_i$ for $i \in r$, sorted by nonincreasing $n_i$ and laid end to end: the constituent $\pi_i$ occupies the block of $2^{n_i}$ coordinates beginning at an offset that, by the sorting, is a multiple of $2^{n_i}$, so the block is addressed by a \emph{block index} $b_i \in \BB_{N_r - n_i}$ on the high coordinates. The merged oracle $M_r \colon \BB_{N_r} \to K$ is the function with
\[
  M_r(v, b_i) = \pi_i(v) \quad \text{for } v \in \BB_{n_i},\ i \in r,
\]
on the constituent blocks, zero on the remaining coordinates except for the pad below, with $N_r$ the least length accommodating the constituents and the pad. A merged oracle is witness-dependent iff some constituent is; a public oracle sharing a round with a witness-dependent one is padded and masked along with it, at a cost in randomness but not in correctness. A query operand on a constituent becomes an operand on its merged oracle, extended by the block indicator,
\[
  \langle \pi_i, t \rangle = \langle M_r, t^{(b_i)} \rangle, \qquad t^{(b_i)}(v, u) \coloneqq t(v) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{N_r - n_i}(b_i, u), \quad (v, u) \in \BB_{n_i} \times \BB_{N_r - n_i},
\]
the table that agrees with $t$ on block $b_i$ and vanishes elsewhere, so the inner product sees only the constituent's own data. Operands of one query on two constituents of the same merged oracle add. Henceforth every query is a query on the merged oracles $M_r$, with the same targets.

\paragraph{ZK pad.}
Let $m$ denote the variable count of the merged oracle holding the compiler's Libra oracle (defined in the opening phase below; concretely $m = O(\log \mathbf{n} + \log q)$). For each witness-dependent merged oracle the compiler reserves a block $P_r \subseteq \BB_{N_r}$ of
\[
  |P_r| \ge q + 2m + 2
\]
coordinates above the constituent blocks, the \emph{ZK pad}, enlarging $N_r$ by one when the slack above the constituents is too small, and the prover fills $P_r$ with independent uniform field elements. Every extended operand $t^{(b_i)}$ vanishes on $P_r$, so the pad enters no query: it is a randomizable support (\S\ref{sec:liop}) of the merged oracle, filled uniformly. Its budget is spent on the four groups of linear functionals of $M_r$ that the opening phase reveals, one entry of randomness per revealed functional:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
revealed functional of $M_r$ & count \\
\hline
codeword symbols opened in the query phase & $q$ \\
the evaluation $\alpha_r$ output by Phase~A & $1$ \\
transmitted round-polynomial coefficients of Phase~A$'$ & $2m$ \\
the evaluation $\alpha'_r$ output by Phase~A$'$ & $1$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No further coordinate is needed to make the Merkle commitment hiding: every opened leaf of a masked oracle also carries a symbol of the uniform mask $\omega_r$ introduced next, which already salts it.

\paragraph{ZK mask and commit phase.}
When the LIOP prover completes a round, the compiled prover forms $M_r$ as above. If the round is not witness-dependent it commits $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r)$ as in \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark}. Otherwise it samples a uniform \emph{mask} $\omega_r \colon \BB_{N_r} \to K$ and commits the \emph{interleaved} Reed--Solomon codeword whose two rows are $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r)$ and $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(\omega_r)$: the Merkle commitment is taken over the columns of this $2 \times |E_{N_r}|$ array, so that opening a single position $e$ exposes both $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r)[e]$ and $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(\omega_r)[e]$ under one authentication path. We regard the pair as the single multilinear
\[
  \mle{G}_r(X, Y) \coloneqq (1 - Y) \cdot \mle{M}_r(X) + Y \cdot \mle{\omega}_r(X), \qquad X \in K^{N_r},\ Y \in K,
\]
whose restriction to $Y \in \BB_1$ is the committed pair and whose value at $Y = 0$ is the message. The interleave coordinate $Y$ is absent from an unmasked oracle, which is constant in $Y$.

\paragraph{Monomial form.}
The opening phase runs its batch sumchecks over the infinity hypercube $\set{0, \infty}^n$ of \S\ref{sec:notation}. For a value list $u \in K^{\BB_n}$ write
\[
  u^\infty(X) \coloneqq \sum_{v \in \BB_n} u(v) \cdot X^v, \qquad X^v \coloneqq \prod_{j = 0}^{n - 1} X_j^{v_j},
\]
for the multilinear whose \emph{monomial coefficients} are $u$. Then for all $u, w \in K^{\BB_n}$ and every $\nu \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{x \in \set{0, \infty}^{n + \nu}} u^\infty(x_{< n}) \cdot w^\infty(x_{< n}) = \langle u, w \rangle,
  \label{eq:infty-inner-product}
\end{equation}
since evaluation on $\set{0, \infty}^n$ reads off monomial coefficients and the $\nu$ unused coordinates pass through the sum unchanged. Queries of unequal length therefore batch on a common variable count with no padding factor, and a claim on $n$ variables closes at the low $n$ coordinates of the common challenge vector. A sumcheck over the infinity hypercube ends with the ordinary evaluation $u^\infty(\rho)$ at its challenge $\rho \in K^n$, which is a linear query on $u$ with the tensor operand $(\rho^v)_{v \in \BB_n}$. It is also a multilinear-extension evaluation at a projectively transformed point: in characteristic $2$, whenever $\rho_j \ne 1$ for all $j$,
\begin{equation}
  u^\infty(\rho) = \Bigl( \prod_{j = 0}^{n - 1} (1 + \rho_j) \Bigr) \cdot \mle{u}(\sigma), \qquad \sigma_j \coloneqq \frac{\rho_j}{1 + \rho_j},
  \label{eq:monomial-to-mle}
\end{equation}
because $\mle{\eq}(v_j, \sigma_j) = \rho_j^{v_j} / (1 + \rho_j)$ for $v_j \in \BB_1$. The excluded event $\rho_j = 1$ has probability $n / |K|$ over a uniform $\rho$, and the verifier rejects if it occurs. This translation is what hands the output of the batch sumchecks to BaseFold, which runs over the Boolean hypercube.

\paragraph{Opening phase.}
After the LIOP's final challenge the parties hold the queries on the merged oracles. Write $\mathbf{n} \coloneqq \max_r N_r$. The opening proceeds in three phases.

\begin{protocol}[Batched ZK BaseFold opening]
\label{prot:batched-zk-basefold}
\emph{Claims:} $\sum_r \langle M_r, t_{j, r} \rangle = s_j$ for $j < J$, on committed merged oracles $M_r \colon \BB_{N_r} \to K$.

\emph{Phase~A (batch sumcheck).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples a batching challenge $\mu \gets K$. Set $s_f \coloneqq \sum_j \mu^j s_j$ and, for each merged oracle, the combined operand $T_r \coloneqq \sum_j \mu^j t_{j, r} \in K^{\BB_{N_r}}$. By \eqref{eq:infty-inner-product} the batched claim reads
\[
  s_f = \sum_{x \in \set{0, \infty}^{\mathbf{n}}} f(x), \qquad f(X) \coloneqq \sum_r M_r^\infty(X_{< N_r}) \cdot T_r^\infty(X_{< N_r}),
\]
a bare sumcheck claim on a summand of individual degree $2$.
\item The prover samples a Libra mask $h$ on $\mathbf{n}$ variables with $d = 2$ -- each univariate $h_j$ of degree $\le 1$, so $h$ has $1 + 2\mathbf{n}$ coefficients -- and commits its coefficient table $h'$ as the oracle $\pi_h$ exactly as in \S\ref{sec:libra}, as a witness-dependent oracle in a round of its own; let $m$ be the variable count of the merged oracle holding $\pi_h$ (so $2^m \ge 2^{\nu + \delta} + q + 2m + 2$). The parties run the Libra-masked sumcheck of Protocol~\ref{prot:zk-sumcheck-libra} on $s_f = \sum_{x \in \set{0, \infty}^{\mathbf{n}}} f(x)$, over the infinity hypercube, where it is hiding in every characteristic (Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform}).
\item At the sumcheck point $\rho \in K^{\mathbf{n}}$, the reduction outputs $f^* = f(\rho)$ and the query $\beta = \langle h', \textsf{libra-eval}_\rho \rangle$ on $\pi_h$. The prover sends $\alpha_r \coloneqq M_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r})$ for every $r$; the verifier evaluates each $T_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r})$ on its own and checks $\sum_r \alpha_r \cdot T_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r}) = f^*$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Phase~A$'$ (rerandomization).} The claims now in hand are the evaluations $M_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r}) = \alpha_r$ and the query $\langle h', \textsf{libra-eval}_\rho \rangle = \beta$ on $\pi_h$, whose operand is not of evaluation form. This phase reduces all of them to evaluations at one point.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier samples $\mu' \gets K$. Writing $\rho = (\rho_{< m}, \rho_{\ge m})$ and $e_\rho(Y) \coloneqq \prod_{j < m} (1 + \rho_j Y_j)$, the multilinear whose coefficient table is $(\rho_{< m}^y)_{y \in \BB_m}$, each evaluation claim is an $m$-variate sum,
\[
  \alpha_r = \sum_{y \in \set{0, \infty}^m} M_r^\infty(y, \rho_{m \le \cdot < N_r}) \cdot e_\rho(y),
\]
by \eqref{eq:infty-inner-product} applied to the coefficient table of $M_r^\infty(\cdot, \rho_{m \le \cdot < N_r})$ (for $N_r < m$ the second argument is absent), and the query on $\pi_h$ is $\beta = \sum_{y \in \set{0, \infty}^m} h'^\infty(y) \cdot \textsf{libra-eval}_\rho^\infty(y)$. The parties run a bare sumcheck over $\set{0, \infty}^m$ on the $\mu'$-batch of these $m$-variate degree-$2$ claims, without a Libra mask.
\item At its point $\rho' \in K^m$ the prover sends $\alpha'_r \coloneqq M_r^\infty(\rho', \rho_{m \le \cdot < N_r})$ for every $r$ and $\beta' \coloneqq h'^\infty(\rho')$; the verifier evaluates $e_\rho(\rho')$ and $\textsf{libra-eval}_\rho^\infty(\rho')$ and checks the batched reduced claim. Set $\rho'' \coloneqq (\rho', \rho_{\ge m}) \in K^{\mathbf{n}}$: every merged oracle now carries the evaluation claim $M_r^\infty(\rho''_{< N_r}) = \alpha'_r$, and $\pi_h$ (within its merged oracle) the claim $h'^\infty(\rho''_{< m}) = \beta'$, all at prefixes of the one point $\rho''$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Phase~B (Batched BaseFold).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The verifier rejects if some $\rho''_j = 1$; otherwise both parties translate each claim by \eqref{eq:monomial-to-mle} into the multilinear-extension claim $\mle{M}_r(\sigma_{< N_r}) = a_r$ at $\sigma_j \coloneqq \rho''_j / (1 + \rho''_j)$, with $a_r \coloneqq \alpha'_r / \prod_{j < N_r} (1 + \rho''_j)$, and likewise for $\pi_h$'s merged oracle. In terms of the committed pairs this is $\mle{G}_r(\sigma_{< N_r}, 0) = a_r$: an evaluation claim with target coordinate $0$ in the interleave variable $Y$.
\item The verifier samples fresh oracle-index challenges $r' \gets K^{\lceil \log_2 \#\text{oracles} \rceil}$, \emph{after} the $a_r$ are fixed, and the parties run Batched BaseFold Step~2 (\S\ref{sec:batched-basefold}) on the piecewise-concatenated oracle built from the lifted $\mle{G}_r$ at the point $(\sigma, 0, r')$ with target $s' \coloneqq \sum_r a_r \cdot \mle{\eq}(r, r')$. The single BaseFold instance (\S\ref{sec:basefold}) opens with one MLE-check round for the interleave variable: the prover sends the linear round polynomial, the verifier checks it against $s'$ at $Y = 0$, samples $\gamma \gets K$, and both parties fold the interleaved pairs to the codewords $(1 - \gamma) \cdot \textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r) + \gamma \cdot \textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(\omega_r)$, which the verifier emulates at a queried column from the two rows of the commitment. Unmasked oracles are unaffected by this fold. The remaining rounds and the query phase are those of \S\ref{sec:basefold} and \S\ref{sec:lifted-basefold}, over the Boolean hypercube.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

Phase~A is the one sumcheck of the document run over the infinity hypercube: that is where Libra masking of a bare sumcheck is hiding in characteristic $2$ (\S\ref{sec:libra}), and its summand has degree $2$ throughout. The document's other sumchecks stay on $\BB_n$, which the infinity hypercube cannot replace in batched sumchecks of mixed degree, where the leading coefficient read at $\infty$ depends on the degree bound. Phase~A$'$ costs the prover one pass over each merged oracle, to form the coefficient tables $M_r^\infty(\cdot, \rho_{m \le \cdot < N_r})$, and $m$ rounds; it is cheap because the mask oracle is small. Phase~B is unchanged from \S\ref{sec:batched-basefold} except for the extra interleave round, which costs one linear round polynomial.

\paragraph{Soundness.}
Fix the committed $M_r$, $\omega_r$, and $h'$. If some query is unsatisfied then $s_f \ne \sum_x f(x)$ except with probability $J / |K|$ over $\mu$ (Schwartz--Zippel on the batching polynomial); the Libra blending adds $1 / |K|$ (the masked claim is affine in $\alpha$); and the sumcheck over $\set{0, \infty}^{\mathbf{n}}$ has the soundness error $2 \mathbf{n} / |K|$ of its Boolean counterpart~\cite[\S 3]{cryptoeprint:2026/762}. A prover that reaches the end of Phase~A with a false running claim must therefore send some $\alpha_r \ne M_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r})$ or a false $\beta$. Phase~A$'$ is a sumcheck on the $\mu'$-batch of exactly these evaluation claims, so with error $O(J + m) / |K|$ it forces a false $\alpha'_r$ or $\beta'$, and the fresh $r'$ of Phase~B makes $s'$ a random linear test of all of them at once, so some translated claim $a_r$ is false, which BaseFold rejects with its own error (\S\ref{sec:basefold}, \S\ref{sec:lifted-basefold}). Sampling $r'$ after the $a_r$ is essential: a prover who knew $r'$ in advance could satisfy both the Phase~A$'$ closing check and $s' = \sum_r a_r\, \mle{\eq}(r, r')$ with individually false $a_r$. The interleave round is an ordinary MLE-check round, and $(1 - \gamma) \textsf{Enc}(M_r) + \gamma \textsf{Enc}(\omega_r)$ is a codeword of the same code, so Phase~B inherits the analysis of \S\ref{sec:batched-basefold} verbatim. Knowledge soundness is preserved: the extractor recovers each $M_r$ from the first row of its interleaved commitment, and each constituent is a block of it.

\paragraph{Zero knowledge.}
Each leakage channel is covered by one source of randomness, and we take them in protocol order.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Phase~A} reveals $s_h$, the round polynomials $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha R_h^{(i)}$, and $\beta = h(\rho)$. The combined operands $T_r$ vanish on the pads, so the $R_f^{(i)}$ are functions of the constituents alone and the pad cannot help here; the Libra mask does. By Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform} the masking messages are uniform over the infinity hypercube in every characteristic, so for $\alpha \ne 0$ the transcript of Phase~A is uniform and the simulator of Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck} applies, with $f^*$ supplied by the next item. The evaluations $\alpha_r = M_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r})$ are revealed; each is a linear functional of $M_r$ that touches the pad with the monomial weights $\rho^v$, $v \in P_r$.
\item \emph{Phase~A$'$} reveals $2m$ round coefficients, the $\alpha'_r$, and $\beta'$. The Libra mask does not cover this phase: the masking map is a bijection, so the coefficients of $h'$ are determined by the Phase~A transcript up to the unknown $R_f^{(i)}$, and every further linear functional of $h'$ -- which the Phase~A$'$ round polynomials contain, through the partial sums of $h'^\infty \cdot \textsf{libra-eval}_\rho^\infty$ -- discloses a linear functional of the unmasked $R_f^{(i)}$. Each transmitted coefficient is, however, such a functional \emph{added to} a partial sum of $M_r^\infty(\cdot, \rho_{m \le \cdot < N_r}) \cdot e_\rho$, which touches the pads: the pad entries are monomial coefficients of $M_r^\infty$ and enter with the nonzero weights $\rho_{\ge m}^{u}$. The sum is uniform provided the pad functionals are independent, which is Lemma~\ref{lem:pad-independence}. The claim $\beta' = h'^\infty(\rho')$ touches the pad of $\pi_h$'s merged oracle in the same way.
\item \emph{Phase~B} and the query phase. For $\gamma \ne 0$ the folded codeword $(1 - \gamma) \textsf{Enc}(M_r) + \gamma \textsf{Enc}(\omega_r)$ is a uniform codeword, so the folding-phase transcript -- the interleave round polynomial, which reveals $\sum_r \mle{\eq}(r, r')\, \mle{\omega}_r(\sigma)$, the later round polynomials, the intermediate codewords, and the final codeword -- is independent of the messages. The query phase reveals $q$ symbols of each $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r)$ outright, which the pad covers, and $q$ symbols of $\textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(\omega_r)$, which are uniform.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Pad independence; proof deferred]
\label{lem:pad-independence}
Fix a witness-dependent merged oracle $M_r$ with pad $P_r$, $|P_r| \ge q + 2m + 2$. Consider the $q + 2m + 2$ linear functionals of $M_r \in K^{\BB_{N_r}}$ revealed by Protocol~\ref{prot:batched-zk-basefold}: the $q$ codeword symbols $M_r \mapsto \textsf{Enc}_{N_r}(M_r)[e]$ at the queried positions $e$; the evaluation $M_r \mapsto M_r^\infty(\rho_{< N_r})$; the $2m$ transmitted coefficients of the Phase~A$'$ round polynomials, as functionals of $M_r$; and the evaluation $M_r \mapsto M_r^\infty(\rho''_{< N_r})$. Over uniform challenges $\rho, \rho'$ and uniform query positions, the restrictions of these functionals to the coordinates $P_r$ are linearly independent except with probability $\text{poly}(q, m, N_r) / |K|$.
\end{lemma}

The $q$ codeword functionals are those the randomizable-support analysis of Diamond~\cite{Diamond:2025ac} treats; the new content is that the $2m + 2$ evaluation-type functionals, each a sum of monomial weights at a random point over a sub-cube, are independent of them and of each other on the pad. We state the lemma as the hypothesis the simulator needs and defer its proof. Granting it, the pad entries are uniform and appear in no other message, so the revealed values are jointly uniform, and a simulator reproduces the compiled transcript from the targets $s_j$ alone: uniform values for every message above, with the evaluation claims and the interleave round programmed to satisfy the verifier's checks, and the HVZK simulator of the source LIOP supplying the rest. The three sources of randomness cover disjoint messages -- the mask $h$ the Phase~A round polynomials, the pads the evaluation claims, the Phase~A$'$ round polynomials, and the opened symbols, and the masks $\omega_r$ the folding phase -- and each is sampled fresh per proof.

\subsection{Compiling Binius64 to a ZK-SNARK}
\label{sec:binius-zk-snark}

We assemble the zero-knowledge SNARK for the Binius64 constraint system from three components: the Binius64 LIOP of \S\ref{sec:binius-liop}, the IronSpartan LIOP of \S\ref{sec:ironspartan}, and the Batched ZK BaseFold compiler of \S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}. The Binius64 LIOP is complete and knowledge-sound but not zero-knowledge: its sumcheck round polynomials and its terminal evaluation claim are witness-dependent. We take it as the \emph{inner} LIOP of the proof composition of \S\ref{sec:proof-composition} and discharge its verifier's predicate with IronSpartan as the outer zero-knowledge proof.

Recall (\S\ref{sec:protocol-overview}) that the Binius64 LIOP commits the packed witness $\pi = \textsf{pack}_\lambda(w)$, runs the IntMul, BinMul, BitAnd, shift, ring-switching, and public-input reductions, and terminates in a single linear query on $\pi$. Apart from this committed oracle, the prover transmits a predetermined number $N$ of non-oracle field elements: the sumcheck round-polynomial coefficients, the claimed evaluations passed between reductions, and the GKR layer claims, all fixed in count by the padded constraint-system parameters. The number of terminal queries is likewise predetermined. (An implementation may let a reduction commit auxiliary oracles of its own, with further queries on them; the composition treats such an oracle exactly like $\pi$.) This is the predetermined-count condition the composition of \S\ref{sec:proof-composition} requires.

Following that composition, the composed prover first precommits, in IronSpartan's precommit segment $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, one uniform OTP key per non-oracle element and one per terminal query. It then runs the Binius64 prover, sending each of the $N$ elements masked by its key, so the transcript reveals only uniform ciphertexts. The Binius64 verifier's acceptance is a conjunction of low-degree checks -- the per-round sumcheck consistency relations $R^{(i)}(0) + R^{(i)}(1) = s^{(i)}$, the products checked between the reductions, and the derivation of the final query target -- each a low-degree function of the transmitted elements and the public Fiat--Shamir challenges. The composed prover encodes this conjunction as an R1CS instance whose public segment $z_{\textsf{pub}}$ holds the ciphertexts, the challenges, and the Binius64 public statement $u$; whose precommit segment holds the OTP keys; and whose witness segment $z_{\textsf{wit}}$ holds the plaintext elements and the intermediate values of the checks. Since the Binius64 verifier is succinct, this R1CS is small -- its size is that of the verifier's computation, polylogarithmic in the witness -- so IronSpartan proves it cheaply, in zero knowledge.

The one place the inner oracle survives into the compiled argument is the terminal linear query $\langle \pi, t \rangle = s$ -- the combined ring-switching and public-input query of \S\ref{sec:protocol-overview}. Following \S\ref{sec:proof-composition}, the prover sends the ciphertext $c = s + k$ of the target under its precommitted key, the R1CS asserts $s = c - k$, and the compiler receives the query \eqref{eq:target-relation}.

\paragraph{Oracles and rounds.}
The composed LIOP commits, in order: $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, before the statement is fixed; the packed witness $\pi$, which the inner prover sends before any challenge, so that $z_{\textsf{pre}}$ and $\pi$ fall in the same round and are merged into one committed oracle; then, once the inner LIOP has run and the R1CS instance is formed, IronSpartan's $z_{\textsf{wit}}$ and Libra oracle $\pi_g$, which share a round. The compiler adds its own Libra oracle $\pi_h$ in the opening phase. All of these are witness-dependent, so every merged oracle carries a ZK pad of $q + 2m + 2$ coordinates and a mask. The queries are \eqref{eq:target-relation}, whose two operands land on the one merged oracle and add; IronSpartan's queries $(t_{\textsf{pre}}, s_{\textsf{pre}})$ on $z_{\textsf{pre}}$, $(t_{\textsf{wit}}, s_{\textsf{wit}})$ on $z_{\textsf{wit}}$, and its Libra query on $\pi_g$ (\S\ref{sec:ip-reduction}); and the compiler's Libra query on $\pi_h$. IronSpartan's segments need no randomizable support of their own (\S\ref{sec:ironspartan-liop}); only its dummy constraints (\S\ref{sec:dummy-masking}) remain, since the products $(a, b, c)$ it reveals are its own messages. Compiling Stage~1 with Batched ZK BaseFold and leaving the BCS and Fiat--Shamir stages of \S\ref{sec:liop-to-snark} untouched yields a non-interactive zero-knowledge argument for the Binius64 relation in the random oracle model.

\paragraph{Security.}
The argument is honest-verifier zero-knowledge: a simulator with no access to the witness produces the transcript in three layers. The $N$ non-oracle messages and the target ciphertext $c$ are reproduced as uniform values, matching the honest distribution because each OTP key is uniform and used once. IronSpartan's portion is produced by its HVZK simulator (\S\ref{sec:zk-sumcheck-machinery}, Appendix~\ref{app:zk-sumcheck}), which simulates the masked constraint check, the dummy-masked products (\S\ref{sec:dummy-masking}), and the segment-query targets. Finally, the Batched ZK BaseFold simulator (\S\ref{sec:batched-zk-basefold}) reproduces the commit phase, the three opening phases, and the query phase of all merged oracles from the query targets alone, using the Libra mask for Phase~A, the pads for the evaluation claims, Phase~A$'$, and the opened symbols (under Lemma~\ref{lem:pad-independence}), and the masks $\omega_r$ for the folding phase. After Fiat--Shamir this is a non-interactive zero-knowledge argument in the random oracle model. Knowledge soundness and the soundness error are inherited from the inner LIOP, IronSpartan, and the compiler, increased only by the $O(J + \mathbf{n}) / |K|$ terms of the batching, masking, and composition steps.

\section{Expressing Common Operations}
\label{sec:common-ops}

The constraint system is far more expressive than its primitive forms suggest; bitwise NOT, OR, integer addition, and integer subtraction are all expressible using Zero and BitAnd constraints with appropriate shifted value indices. We sketch the encodings below, motivated by the RV64I instruction set.

\paragraph{Bitwise AND, XOR, NOT, OR.}
Bitwise AND and bitwise XOR are both native: AND is a BitAnd constraint, and $z = x \oplus y$ is the Zero constraint $x \oplus y \oplus z = 0$. To constrain that an unshifted value equals $w[y]$, one uses the shifted index $(y, \textsf{sll}, 0, \textsf{sll}, 0)$, which acts as the identity. Bitwise NOT is a Zero constraint naming the constant $\texttt{0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF}$ (available because that constant is placed in the constant stretch): the constraint $x \oplus y \oplus \texttt{0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF} = 0$ forces $y = \overline{x}$. Bitwise OR follows from the identity $x \lor y = (x \land y) \oplus x \oplus y$: the BitAnd constraint $x \land y = x \oplus y \oplus z$ forces $z = x \lor y$.

\paragraph{Integer addition.}
To constrain $z = (x + y) \bmod 2^{64}$, the prover supplies an auxiliary witness word $c_\text{out}$ whose bits are the per-position carries. Writing $c_\text{in} \coloneqq \textsf{sll}(c_\text{out}, 1)$, the BitAnd constraint
\[
  (x \oplus c_\text{in}) \land (y \oplus c_\text{in}) = c_\text{in} \oplus c_\text{out}
\]
enforces correct carry propagation, and the Zero constraint $z \oplus x \oplus y \oplus c_\text{in} = 0$ forces $z = x \oplus y \oplus c_\text{in}$.

\paragraph{Integer subtraction.}
To constrain $z = (x - y) \bmod 2^{64}$, the prover supplies an auxiliary witness word $b_\text{out}$ whose bits are the per-position borrows. Writing $b_\text{in} \coloneqq \textsf{sll}(b_\text{out}, 1)$, the BitAnd constraint
\[
  (\overline{x} \oplus b_\text{in}) \land (y \oplus b_\text{in}) = b_\text{in} \oplus b_\text{out}
\]
enforces correct borrow propagation, and the Zero constraint $z \oplus x \oplus y \oplus b_\text{in} = 0$ forces $z = x \oplus y \oplus b_\text{in}$.

\section{Arithmetization of Shift Indicators}
\label{app:shift-arithmetizations}

This appendix gives the explicit arithmetizations of the eight shift indicators $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\text{op}$ defined in \S\ref{sec:shift-indicators}, establishing the succinctness claim stated there. The constructions generalize the shifted virtual polynomial construction of Diamond and Posen~\cite[\S 4.3]{Diamond:2025aa}.

\subsection{Logical Shifts and Rotation}
\label{sec:logical-shifts}

We arithmetize $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\textsf{srl}$ first. The case $\textsf{sll}$ follows by transposing the first two arguments, since on the cube
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{sll}(i, j, s) = \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{srl}(j, i, s),
\]
and the same identity extends to the multilinear extensions.

The shift indicators of $\textsf{srl}$, $\textsf{sll}$, and $\textsf{ror}$ all reduce to two auxiliary functions $\mathsf{s}_k, \mathsf{s}'_k$ on $\BB_k^3$, defined for $k \in \set{0, \ldots, 6}$ by
\[
  \mathsf{s}_k(i, j, s) \coloneqq \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } j = i + s \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}, \qquad \mathsf{s}'_k(i, j, s) \coloneqq \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } j = i + s - 2^k \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases},
\]
where $i, j, s \in \BB_k$ are read as integers (\S\ref{sec:notation}) and the equalities $j = i + s$ and $j = i + s - 2^k$ are taken over the integers. So $\mathsf{s}_k$ indicates that $i + s$ does not overflow modulo $2^k$ and equals $j$, while $\mathsf{s}'_k$ indicates that $i + s$ overflows by exactly $2^k$ and equals $j + 2^k$.

By definition,
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{srl} = \mathsf{s}_6.
\]
The function $\textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{ror}(i, j, s)$ holds iff $j \equiv i + s \pmod{64}$; since $0 \leq i, s < 64$, this means $j = i + s$ or $j = i + s - 64$, and so
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{ror} = \mathsf{s}_6 + \mathsf{s}'_6.
\]

\paragraph{Recursive arithmetization.}
The functions $\mathsf{s}_k, \mathsf{s}'_k$ satisfy a recursive identity. The base case is $\mathsf{s}_0 = 1$ and $\mathsf{s}'_0 = 0$. For the inductive case, write $i = (i_{<k-1}, i_{k-1})$ for the splitting of $i \in \BB_k$ into its low $k - 1$ bits and its top bit, similarly for $j$ and $s$; then on $\BB_k^3$,
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{s}_k(i, j, s) &= \begin{cases} \mathsf{s}_{k - 1}(i_{<k-1}, j_{<k-1}, s_{<k-1}) & \text{if } i_{k - 1} + s_{k - 1} = j_{k - 1}, \\ \mathsf{s}'_{k - 1}(i_{<k-1}, j_{<k-1}, s_{<k-1}) & \text{if } i_{k - 1} + s_{k - 1} + 1 = j_{k - 1}, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \\
  \mathsf{s}'_k(i, j, s) &= \begin{cases} \mathsf{s}_{k - 1}(i_{<k-1}, j_{<k-1}, s_{<k-1}) & \text{if } i_{k - 1} + s_{k - 1} = j_{k - 1} + 2, \\ \mathsf{s}'_{k - 1}(i_{<k-1}, j_{<k-1}, s_{<k-1}) & \text{if } i_{k - 1} + s_{k - 1} + 1 = j_{k - 1} + 2, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\end{align*}
where all bit arithmetic is taken over the integers.

Each of the four bit-pattern conditions on the top bits $(i_{k-1}, j_{k-1}, s_{k-1}) \in \BB_1^3$ admits a $2$-multiplication arithmetization over $\FF{2}$:
\begin{align*}
  i + s = j &\iff 1 + j + s + i \cdot \bigl( 1 + s \cdot (1 + j) \bigr) = 1, \\
  i + s + 1 = j &\iff (1 - i) \cdot j \cdot (1 - s) = 1, \\
  i + s = j + 2 &\iff i \cdot (1 - j) \cdot s = 1, \\
  i + s + 1 = j + 2 &\iff i + s + j \cdot \bigl( i + s \cdot (1 + i) \bigr) = 1.
\end{align*}
Substituting these into the recursion yields multilinear formulas for $\mle{\mathsf{s}}_k$ and $\mle{\mathsf{s}}'_k$ in terms of $\mle{\mathsf{s}}_{k - 1}$ and $\mle{\mathsf{s}}'_{k - 1}$. Together, the two formulas advance a pair of registers using at most $12$ multiplications per level; over $k = 1, \ldots, 6$ this gives at most $72$ multiplications in $K$ to evaluate the pair $(\mle{\mathsf{s}}_6, \mle{\mathsf{s}}'_6)$ at any point of $K^{18}$. In particular, $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\textsf{srl}$, $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\textsf{sll}$, and $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\textsf{ror}$ are all succinct.

\subsection{Arithmetic Right Shift}
\label{sec:arithmetic-shifts}

The arithmetic right shift $\textsf{sra}$ behaves like $\textsf{srl}$ except that the sign bit of $v$ (bit $63$) is replicated into the vacated high positions. Concretely, $\textsf{sra}(v, s)_i = v_{i + s}$ if $i + s < 64$, and $\textsf{sra}(v, s)_i = v_{63}$ otherwise. Hence
\[
  \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{sra}(i, j, s) = \textsf{shift-ind}_\textsf{srl}(i, j, s) + \Bigl( \prod_{k = 0}^5 j_k \Bigr) \cdot \mathsf{a}(i, s),
\]
where the indicator $\prod_{k = 0}^5 j_k$ selects $j = 63$ and the auxiliary function $\mathsf{a} \colon \BB_6 \times \BB_6 \to \FF{2}$ is defined by $\mathsf{a}(i, s) = 1$ iff $i \geq 64 - s$ (equivalently, iff $i + s$ overflows modulo $64$).

We arithmetize $\mathsf{a}$ via the sequence of functions $\mathsf{a}_k \colon \BB_k \times \BB_k \to \FF{2}$, $k \in \set{0, \ldots, 6}$, defined by $\mathsf{a}_k(i, s) = 1$ iff $i \geq 2^k - s$, so that $\mathsf{a} = \mathsf{a}_6$. The base case is $\mathsf{a}_0 = 0$, and for $k \geq 1$,
\[
  \mathsf{a}_k(i, s) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i_{k - 1} = s_{k - 1} = 1, \\ \mathsf{a}_{k - 1}(i_{<k-1}, s_{<k-1}) & \text{if } i_{k - 1} \neq s_{k - 1}, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
\]
The arithmetization of this recursion is the single-line update
\[
  \mle{\mathsf{a}}_k(I, S) = I_{k - 1} \cdot S_{k - 1} + (I_{k - 1} + S_{k - 1}) \cdot \mle{\mathsf{a}}_{k - 1}(I, S),
\]
costing at most two multiplications per level, hence at most $12$ multiplications in $K$ for $\mle{\mathsf{a}}_6$. Together with the $72$ multiplications for $\mle{\mathsf{s}}_6$, evaluating $\mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_\textsf{sra}$ at any point of $K^{18}$ costs at most $84$ multiplications plus a $6$-way product for $\prod_{k = 0}^5 J_k$.

\subsection{Parallel 32-bit Shifts}
\label{sec:parallel-32bit-shifts}

The four parallel $32$-bit operations $\textsf{sll32}, \textsf{srl32}, \textsf{sra32}, \textsf{ror32}$ treat each $64$-bit word as two independent $32$-bit halves and apply the corresponding $32$-bit shift to both halves. The shift amount $s$ is taken modulo $32$, i.e., only the low $5$ bits $S_0, \ldots, S_4$ are used; the top bit $S_5$ is ignored.

Each $32$-bit indicator is a product of a $5$-bit version of the corresponding $64$-bit indicator (acting on $I_0, \ldots, I_4$ and $J_0, \ldots, J_4$) with the equality indicator $\mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5)$, which restricts movement to within a single half. Explicitly, writing $I_{<5} \coloneqq (I_0, \ldots, I_4)$, $J_{<5} \coloneqq (J_0, \ldots, J_4)$, and $S_{<5} \coloneqq (S_0, \ldots, S_4)$:
\begin{align*}
  \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\textsf{srl32}}(I, J, S) &= \mle{\mathsf{s}}_5(I_{<5}, J_{<5}, S_{<5}) \cdot \mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5), \\
  \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\textsf{sll32}}(I, J, S) &= \mle{\mathsf{s}}_5(J_{<5}, I_{<5}, S_{<5}) \cdot \mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5), \\
  \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\textsf{ror32}}(I, J, S) &= \bigl( \mle{\mathsf{s}}_5(I_{<5}, J_{<5}, S_{<5}) + \mle{\mathsf{s}}'_5(I_{<5}, J_{<5}, S_{<5}) \bigr) \cdot \mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5), \\
  \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\textsf{sra32}}(I, J, S) &= \mle{\textsf{shift-ind}}_{\textsf{srl32}}(I, J, S) + \mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5) \cdot \Bigl( \prod_{k = 0}^4 J_k \Bigr) \cdot \mle{\mathsf{a}}_5(I_{<5}, S_{<5}).
\end{align*}
The sign-bit selector $\prod_{k = 0}^4 J_k$ in the $\textsf{sra32}$ formula identifies $j_{<5} = (1, 1, 1, 1, 1)$, which is bit $31$ when $J_5 = 0$ and bit $63$ when $J_5 = 1$, i.e., the sign bit of whichever half $j$ lies in; the factor $\mle{\eq}(I_5, J_5)$ then forces $i$ to lie in the same half.

Each $5$-bit auxiliary function is succinct by the same recursion as in \S\ref{sec:logical-shifts} and \S\ref{sec:arithmetic-shifts}, truncated to $k = 5$: $\mle{\mathsf{s}}_5$ and $\mle{\mathsf{s}}'_5$ cost at most $60$ multiplications, and $\mle{\mathsf{a}}_5$ costs at most $10$ multiplications. Hence each of the four $32$-bit shift indicators is succinct.

\section{Batched Product Checks of Unequal Depths}
\label{app:padded-prodcheck}

The binary-tree grand-product argument~\cite{Thaler:2013aa} recalled in \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols} reduces an evaluation claim on the root of a depth-$k$ product tree to an evaluation claim on its leaf vector. Several such trees of a \emph{common} depth batch readily: interpolating the trees along a selector index merges them into a single tree over an enlarged content domain, and one run of the layer loop discharges them all, at the cost of a claim-combination step at the start and a claim-separation step at the end. This appendix specifies the batched product check for trees of \emph{unequal} depths. The device is the multiplicative analog of the variable padding of \S\ref{sec:variable-padding}: each shallower tree is padded up to the maximal depth with constant-$1$ leaves, which leaves its products unchanged, and---the substance of this appendix---the padding commutes with the product-tree layer structure, so that the padded prover messages are cheap corrections of the unpadded ones. The verifier's control flow depends only on the maximal depth, the transcript having exactly the shape of an equal-depth batch, and the prover's extra work is a low-order term.

\subsection{Setting}
\label{app:padded-prodcheck-setting}

Fix a content dimension $\ell \geq 0$ and a selector dimension $\kappa \geq 0$. There are $N \leq 2^\kappa$ \emph{instances}, indexed by $u \in \set{0, \ldots, N - 1} \subseteq \BB_\kappa$. Instance $u$ consists of a depth $k_u \geq 0$ and a leaf vector $L_u \in K^{\BB_\ell \times \BB_{k_u}}$; as in \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}, its product-tree layers $L^{(j)}_u \in K^{\BB_\ell \times \BB_{k_u - j}}$, for $j = 0, \ldots, k_u$, are defined by $L^{(0)}_u \coloneqq L_u$ and
\[
  L^{(j)}_u[x, i] \coloneqq L^{(j - 1)}_u[x, (i, 0)] \cdot L^{(j - 1)}_u[x, (i, 1)], \qquad x \in \BB_\ell, \quad i \in \BB_{k_u - j},
\]
the trailing bit of the node index selecting the child. We write $p_u \coloneqq L^{(k_u)}_u \in K^{\BB_\ell}$ for the root layer, whose entries are the products $p_u[x] = \prod_{z \in \BB_{k_u}} L_u[x, z]$.

Set $n \coloneqq \max_u k_u$, assumed $\geq 1$, and $\eta_u \coloneqq n - k_u$. The protocol input is one evaluation claim per instance, all at a \emph{common} content point $\tau \in K^\ell$:
\[
  s_u \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{p}_u(\tau), \qquad u \in \set{0, \ldots, N - 1};
\]
its output is one evaluation claim per instance on the leaf multilinear $\mle{L}_u$, at a family of nested points described below. (When $\ell = 0$ the content index is empty and the input claims are the products themselves.)

\subsection{One-Padding}
\label{app:one-padding}

Recall the selector $\sel(s, v) = 1 + (v - 1) s$ of \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}. For $\nu \geq 0$ and a function $f \in K^{\BB_\ell \times \BB_m}$, the \emph{$\nu$-fold one-padding} $\textsf{OnePad}_\nu(f) \in K^{\BB_\ell \times \BB_{\nu + m}}$ is defined by
\[
  \textsf{OnePad}_\nu(f)[x, (i', i)] \coloneqq \sel\bigl( \mle{\eq}_\nu(0^\nu, i'),\, f[x, i] \bigr) = \begin{cases} f[x, i] & \text{if } i' = 0^\nu, \\ 1 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\]
where the node index is split into its low $\nu$ bits $i'$ and high $m$ bits $i$. Under the integer identification of node indices, one-padding places the entry at node index $i$ of $f$ at node index $2^\nu \cdot i$ and fills the remaining positions with ones; in particular it preserves the products over the node index. On the level of multilinear extensions,
\begin{equation}
  \mle{\textsf{OnePad}_\nu(f)}(X, Z', Z) = \sel\bigl( \mle{\eq}_\nu(0^\nu, Z'),\, \mle{f}(X, Z) \bigr) = 1 + \bigl( \mle{f}(X, Z) - 1 \bigr) \cdot \mle{\eq}_\nu(0^\nu, Z'),
  \label{eq:onepad-mle}
\end{equation}
since the right-hand side is multilinear in all variables and agrees with $\textsf{OnePad}_\nu(f)$ on the cube.

Define the padded leaf vectors $L'_u \coloneqq \textsf{OnePad}_{\eta_u}(L_u) \in K^{\BB_\ell \times \BB_n}$, with product-tree layers $L'^{(j)}_u$ for $j = 0, \ldots, n$ as above. All padded trees have depth $n$, and the padding threads through their layers as follows.

\begin{lemma}[Padded layers]
\label{lem:padded-layers}
For every instance $u$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for $0 \leq j \leq k_u$: \quad $L'^{(j)}_u = \textsf{OnePad}_{\eta_u}\bigl( L^{(j)}_u \bigr)$;
\item for $k_u \leq j \leq n$: \quad $L'^{(j)}_u = \textsf{OnePad}_{n - j}\bigl( p_u \bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular the padded root agrees with the original one, $L'^{(n)}_u = p_u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Upward induction on $j$, the base case $j = 0$ being the definition of $L'_u$. For $1 \leq j \leq k_u$, split the layer-$(j - 1)$ node index as $(i', i, b)$ with $i' \in \BB_{\eta_u}$ the low bits, $i \in \BB_{k_u - j}$ the remaining middle bits, and $b$ the trailing bit; by the induction hypothesis,
\[
  L'^{(j)}_u[x, (i', i)] = \prod_{b \in \BB_1} \sel\bigl( \mle{\eq}_{\eta_u}(0^{\eta_u}, i'),\, L^{(j - 1)}_u[x, (i, b)] \bigr),
\]
which equals $1$ when $i' \neq 0^{\eta_u}$, and equals $L^{(j - 1)}_u[x, (i, 0)] \cdot L^{(j - 1)}_u[x, (i, 1)] = L^{(j)}_u[x, i]$ when $i' = 0^{\eta_u}$; this is $\textsf{OnePad}_{\eta_u}(L^{(j)}_u)[x, (i', i)]$. For $k_u < j \leq n$, the layer-$(j - 1)$ node index $(i', b)$ consists of padding bits only, and
\[
  L'^{(j)}_u[x, i'] = \prod_{b \in \BB_1} \sel\bigl( \mle{\eq}_{n - j + 1}(0^{n - j + 1}, (i', b)),\, p_u[x] \bigr);
\]
the $b = 1$ factor is identically $1$, and the $b = 0$ factor is $\sel(\mle{\eq}_{n - j}(0^{n - j}, i'), p_u[x])$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:padded-layers} is why the padding bits occupy the \emph{low} end of the node index: the layer loop below runs from the root down and appends one node variable per layer at the end of the evaluation point, so the variables introduced by its first $\eta_u$ reductions---which act on the pure-padding layers $\textsf{OnePad}_{n - j}(p_u)$---occupy the low positions, and only afterwards does the real tree of instance $u$ begin, wrapped throughout in the fixed one-padding $\textsf{OnePad}_{\eta_u}(\cdot)$.

\subsection{The Protocol}
\label{app:padded-prodcheck-protocol}

Combine the padded trees along the selector index into the single function $\mathbf{L} \in K^{\BB_\kappa \times \BB_\ell \times \BB_n}$,
\[
  \mathbf{L}[u, x, z] \coloneqq \begin{cases} L'_u[x, z] & \text{if } u < N, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\]
and let $\mathbf{L}^{(j)} \in K^{\BB_\kappa \times \BB_\ell \times \BB_{n - j}}$ be its product-tree layers \emph{over the node index only}---$\mathbf{L}^{(j)}[u, x, i] = \mathbf{L}^{(j - 1)}[u, x, (i, 0)] \cdot \mathbf{L}^{(j - 1)}[u, x, (i, 1)]$---the selector and content indices being carried along passively. By construction,
\begin{equation}
  \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j)}(U, X, Z) = \sum_{u = 0}^{N - 1} \mle{\eq}_\kappa(u, U) \cdot \mle{L'}^{(j)}_u(X, Z).
  \label{eq:combined-layers}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Claim combination.}
The verifier samples $\sigma \gets K^\kappa$, and both parties form the combined root claim
\[
  y^{(n)} \coloneqq \sum_{u = 0}^{N - 1} \mle{\eq}_\kappa(\sigma, u) \cdot s_u \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(n)}(\sigma, \tau),
\]
the identification of the right-hand side being \eqref{eq:combined-layers} at $j = n$ together with $L'^{(n)}_u = p_u$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:padded-layers}). Since the weights $\mle{\eq}_\kappa(\sigma, u)$ are the values at $\sigma$ of the $\kappa$-variate multilinear Lagrange basis, a family containing a false input claim yields a true combined claim with probability at most $\kappa / |K|$ over $\sigma$ (Schwartz--Zippel, as for the batching of \S\ref{sec:var-base-exp}).

\paragraph{Layer loop.}
For $j = n, n - 1, \ldots, 1$, the parties hold a claim $y^{(j)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j)}(\sigma^{(j)}, \tau^{(j)}, \rho^{(j)})$, initialized with $\sigma^{(n)} \coloneqq \sigma$, $\tau^{(n)} \coloneqq \tau$, and $\rho^{(n)}$ empty. Expanding the layer relation,
\[
  \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j)}(\sigma^{(j)}, \tau^{(j)}, \rho^{(j)}) = \sum_{u \in \BB_\kappa} \sum_{x \in \BB_\ell} \sum_{i \in \BB_{n - j}} \mle{\eq}_\kappa(\sigma^{(j)}, u) \, \mle{\eq}_\ell(\tau^{(j)}, x) \, \mle{\eq}_{n - j}(\rho^{(j)}, i) \cdot \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j - 1)}(u, x, i, 0) \, \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j - 1)}(u, x, i, 1),
\]
the parties run a $\bigl( \kappa + \ell + (n - j) \bigr)$-round MLE-check (Protocol~\ref{prot:mle-check}) whose composition has individual degree $\leq 2$, exactly as in \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}, producing challenges $(\sigma^{(j - 1)}, \tau^{(j - 1)}, \rho') \in K^\kappa \times K^\ell \times K^{n - j}$. The prover sends the two child evaluations
\[
  \beta_b \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(j - 1)}(\sigma^{(j - 1)}, \tau^{(j - 1)}, \rho', b), \qquad b \in \set{0, 1};
\]
the verifier checks that $\beta_0 \cdot \beta_1$ equals the MLE-check's reduced evaluation, and a line reduction~\cite{Goldwasser:2015aa} collapses the pair: the verifier samples $r_b \gets K$ and sets $y^{(j - 1)} \coloneqq (1 - r_b)\, \beta_0 + r_b\, \beta_1$ and $\rho^{(j - 1)} \coloneqq (\rho', r_b)$, appending the new node coordinate at the end. Neither the round count, nor the message degrees, nor any verifier check depends on the individual depths $k_u$: the transcript has the shape of an equal-depth batch of depth $n$.

\paragraph{Claim separation and unpadding.}
After $n$ layers the parties hold $y^{(0)} \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{\mathbf{L}}^{(0)}(\sigma^{(0)}, \tau^{(0)}, \rho^{(0)})$ with $\rho^{(0)} \in K^n$. The prover sends the padded per-instance leaf evaluations
\[
  \alpha'_u \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L'}_u(\tau^{(0)}, \rho^{(0)}), \qquad u \in \set{0, \ldots, N - 1},
\]
and the verifier checks that they interpolate the folded claim,
\[
  \sum_{u = 0}^{N - 1} \mle{\eq}_\kappa(\sigma^{(0)}, u) \cdot \alpha'_u \stackrel{?}{=} y^{(0)},
\]
as in the leaf extrapolation of \S\ref{sec:exp-subprotocols}. Finally the verifier removes the padding using \eqref{eq:onepad-mle}: splitting $\rho^{(0)} = (\rho_{< \eta_u}, \rho_{\geq \eta_u}) \in K^{\eta_u} \times K^{k_u}$ and writing $e_u \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_{\eta_u}(0^{\eta_u}, \rho_{< \eta_u})$, it computes
\[
  \alpha_u \coloneqq 1 + (\alpha'_u - 1) \cdot e_u^{-1},
\]
rejecting in the event $e_u = 0$, which requires some coordinate of $\rho^{(0)}$ to equal $1$ and so occurs with probability at most $n / |K|$. The protocol output is the family of leaf claims
\[
  \alpha_u \stackrel{?}{=} \mle{L}_u\bigl( \tau^{(0)}, \rho_{\geq \eta_u} \bigr), \qquad u \in \set{0, \ldots, N - 1}.
\]
The evaluation points are \emph{nested}: every instance's node point is a suffix of the common point $\rho^{(0)}$ shared by the deepest instances, and all instances share the content point $\tau^{(0)}$.

\paragraph{Completeness and soundness.}
For an honest prover every check passes and every output claim is true, except that the verifier rejects with probability at most $n / |K|$ (the event $e_u = 0$ above). Conversely, if some input claim $s_u \neq \mle{p}_u(\tau)$, then except with probability $O\bigl( n (\kappa + n + \ell) \bigr) / |K|$ over the verifier's challenges---the claim combination, the $n$ MLE-checks and line reductions, and the final interpolation check---either the verifier rejects or some output claim $\alpha_u \neq \mle{L}_u(\tau^{(0)}, \rho_{\geq \eta_u})$ is false, to be caught downstream when the output claims are discharged against committed data.

\subsection{Prover Algorithm}
\label{app:padded-prodcheck-prover}

The protocol above is verbatim the equal-depth batched product check applied to the padded trees; the content of this subsection is that the prover need never materialize the padded trees. Throughout, fix a layer $j$ and abbreviate $\eta \coloneqq \eta_u$ and $m \coloneqq k_u - j$.

\paragraph{Per-instance decomposition.}
The layer-$j$ MLE-check binds variables from the highest index down: its first $n - j$ rounds bind the node variables from the top---for instance $u$ in a layer $j \leq k_u$, first the $m \coloneqq k_u - j$ real bits and then the $\eta$ padding bits---followed by $\ell$ content rounds and finally $\kappa$ selector rounds. While the selector variables are unbound, the round polynomial of Protocol~\ref{prot:mle-check} decomposes by \eqref{eq:combined-layers} as
\[
  R^{(i)}(X) = \sum_{u = 0}^{N - 1} \mle{\eq}_\kappa\bigl( \sigma^{(j)}, u \bigr) \cdot R^{(i)}_u(X),
\]
where $R^{(i)}_u$ is the corresponding round polynomial of the (unbatched) layer MLE-check of the padded instance $u$; the final $\kappa$ selector rounds operate on length-$2^\kappa$ tables holding the per-instance child-evaluation pairs, costing $O(2^\kappa)$ operations per layer. It remains to describe $R^{(i)}_u$, in the three phases of a \emph{real} layer ($1 \leq j \leq k_u$) and then in the \emph{padding} layers ($k_u < j \leq n$). Write $\rho^{(j)} = (\rho_\text{pa}, \rho_\text{re}) \in K^\eta \times K^m$ for the split of the node claim point into padding and real coordinates, and $q_u \coloneqq \mle{\eq}_\eta(0^\eta, \rho_\text{pa})$.

\paragraph{Real rounds.}
In the first $m$ rounds, every padding variable of the summation is still unbound. Splitting the node sum on whether the padding bits $i'$ vanish: on the slab $i' = 0^\eta$ the two $\sel$-factors of \eqref{eq:onepad-mle} reduce to the unpadded children and the padding equality-weights contribute the constant $q_u$; off the slab both factors equal $1$ and the weights sum to $1 - q_u$. Hence
\[
  R^{(i)}_u(X) = 1 + q_u \cdot \bigl( \bar{R}^{(i)}_u(X) - 1 \bigr),
\]
where $\bar{R}^{(i)}_u$ is the round polynomial of the \emph{unpadded} layer-$j$ MLE-check of instance $u$, run on the claim point $(\tau^{(j)}, \rho_\text{re})$ with the shared transcript challenges. The correction is an affine map of the message coefficients, costing $O(1)$ per round, and it commutes with the verifier's per-round consistency check.

\paragraph{Padding rounds.}
Let $r_\text{re} \in K^m$ denote the real-node challenges just bound, and let $g_{u, b} \in K^{\BB_\ell}$, for $b \in \set{0, 1}$, be the partially specialized children
\[
  g_{u, b}[x] \coloneqq \mle{L}^{(j - 1)}_u\bigl( x, (r_\text{re}, b) \bigr), \qquad x \in \BB_\ell,
\]
which are exactly the tables the unpadded prover holds after its node rounds. At the phase boundary the prover computes three scalars: the running unpadded claim $z_u \coloneqq \sum_{x \in \BB_\ell} \mle{\eq}_\ell(\tau^{(j)}, x) \, g_{u, 0}[x] \, g_{u, 1}[x]$ (available from the message chain), the evaluation sum $S_u \coloneqq \mle{g}_{u, 0}(\tau^{(j)}) + \mle{g}_{u, 1}(\tau^{(j)})$ (two $O(2^\ell)$ evaluations), and $Q_u \coloneqq z_u - S_u + 1$. In padding round $a \in \set{0, \ldots, \eta - 1}$, define
\[
  e_{u, a} \coloneqq \prod_{c = 0}^{\eta - a - 2} \mle{\eq}(0, \rho_{\text{pa}, c}), \qquad E_{u, a}(X) \coloneqq \mle{\eq}(0, X) \cdot \prod_{a' = 0}^{a - 1} \mle{\eq}\bigl( 0, r^\text{pa}_{a'} \bigr),
\]
the equality-weights of the still-unbound and already-bound padding coordinates respectively, with $r^\text{pa}_{a'}$ the challenge of padding round $a'$. Splitting the residual node sum on whether the unbound padding bits vanish, expanding the product $\prod_b \bigl( 1 + (g_{u, b}[x] - 1) E_{u, a}(X) \bigr)$ on the slab, and summing the content index against its equality-weights yields the closed form
\[
  R^{(m + a)}_u(X) = 1 - e_{u, a} + e_{u, a} \cdot \Bigl[ 1 + (S_u - 2) \, E_{u, a}(X) + Q_u \, E_{u, a}(X)^2 \Bigr]:
\]
the coefficients of $E^0, E^1, E^2$ are the equality-weighted moments $1$, $S_u - 2$, and $Q_u$ of the children. Each padding round costs $O(1)$ operations, only $e_{u, a}$ and $E_{u, a}$ updating between rounds.

\paragraph{Content rounds.}
Once all node variables are bound, the factor $E$ collapses to the constant $e^*_u \coloneqq \prod_{a = 0}^{\eta - 1} \mle{\eq}(0, r^\text{pa}_a)$, and the residual summand is the bivariate product of the transformed tables
\[
  \tilde{g}_{u, b} \coloneqq \sel\bigl( e^*_u, g_{u, b} \bigr) = 1 + (g_{u, b} - 1) \cdot e^*_u.
\]
The remaining $\ell$ content rounds therefore coincide with those of the unpadded MLE-check run on tables obtained by one affine pass ($O(2^\ell)$ operations), with no further per-round correction, and the layer's child evaluations are the $\sel(e^*_u, \cdot)$-images of the unpadded ones. One checks directly that the messages of the three phases chain through the verifier's per-round consistency checks; for instance, at the real-to-padding boundary both the last real-round message and the first padding-round message reduce the claim to $1 + q_u (z_u - 1)$, using $(S_u - 2) + Q_u = z_u - 1$.

\paragraph{Padding layers.}
For $k_u < j \leq n$ the layer is $\textsf{OnePad}_{n - j}(p_u)$ and its $b = 1$ child is identically $1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:padded-layers} and its proof), so the composition is \emph{affine} in $p_u$ and all messages have degree $\leq 1$. The three phases specialize with $m = 0$ and padding count $n - j$ in place of $\eta$: there are no real rounds; the padding rounds open the layer with the children $g_{u, 0} = p_u$ and $g_{u, 1}$ the all-ones table, whence $z_u = \mle{p}_u(\tau^{(j)})$ (the instance's running claim), $S_u = \mle{p}_u(\tau^{(j)}) + 1$, and $Q_u = 0$; and the content rounds run the linear MLE-check on the transformed table $\sel(e^*_u, p_u)$, a fresh length-$2^\ell$ copy per padding layer ($O(2^\ell)$ operations). The child evaluations are $\beta_0 = 1 + (\mle{p}_u(\tau^{(j - 1)}) - 1) \cdot \mle{\eq}_{n - j}(0^{n - j}, \rho')$ and $\beta_1 = 1$.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Summing over the layers, instance $u$ costs its unpadded prover---$O(2^{k_u + \ell})$ operations for the whole tree---plus $O(2^\ell)$ per layer (the boundary evaluations and table transformation in a real layer; the fresh table of $p_u$ in a padding layer) and $O(1)$ per round, for a padding overhead of $O(n \cdot 2^\ell)$. The batch as a whole additionally pays $O(2^\kappa)$ per layer for the selector rounds.

\section{Zero-Knowledge of the Sumcheck Machinery}
\label{app:zk-sumcheck}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:zk-mle-check}. The success of the masked multilinear-extension check (\S\ref{sec:zk-mle-check}), the success of the bare-sumcheck masking over the infinity hypercube, and the failure of the bare-sumcheck masking over the Boolean hypercube in characteristic $2$ (\S\ref{sec:libra}) all follow from the distribution of the prover's masking messages, which we compute below.

\paragraph{The infinity hypercube.}
We record the bare-sumcheck masking messages with the sum running over $\set{0, \infty}^n$ (\S\ref{sec:notation}). Evaluation on $\set{0, \infty}^n$ extracts monomial coefficients rather than point values, so we work with the per-coordinate homogenization of $g$. Write each coordinate projectively as a pair $(X_i, Z_i)$ with the affine chart at $Z_i = 1$, and the two hypercube points as $0 = (0 : 1)$ and $\infty = (1 : 0)$; homogenizing $g$ to degree $d$ in each pair sends the monomial $X_j^{k}$ to $X_j^{k} Z_j^{d - k}$, giving
\[
  g\bigl( (X_0, Z_0), \ldots, (X_{n - 1}, Z_{n - 1}) \bigr) = g_c \prod_{i = 0}^{n - 1} Z_i^{d} + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} \Bigl( \prod_{i \neq j} Z_i^{d} \Bigr) X_j \sum_{k = 0}^{d-1} g_{j, k}\, X_j^k Z_j^{d - 1 - k}.
\]
Summing over $\set{0, \infty}^n$ evaluates each coordinate at $0 = (0 : 1)$ or $\infty = (1 : 0)$. A homogeneous factor $X_i^{a} Z_i^{d - a}$ contributes $1$ at $0$ only when $a = 0$, contributes $1$ at $\infty$ only when $a = d$, and vanishes otherwise; index each vertex by its set of infinite coordinates $S \coloneqq \set{i : v_i = \infty}$. The constant term $g_c \prod_i Z_i^{d}$ survives only at the all-zero vertex $S = \emptyset$, where it contributes $g_c$. In the summand for $j$, the prefactor $\prod_{i \neq j} Z_i^{d}$ forces $v_i = 0$ for all $i \neq j$, while the leading $X_j$ makes the coordinate-$j$ factor vanish at $v_j = 0$; the summand is therefore nonzero only at $S = \set{j}$, where the coordinate-$j$ factor reduces to its top monomial $g_{j, d - 1} X_j^{d}$ and evaluates to $g_{j, d - 1}$. Hence
\[
  s_g = \sum_{v \in \set{0, \infty}^n} g(v) = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{n - 1} g_{j, d - 1},
\]
with no powers of two, in contrast to the Boolean $2^n g_c + 2^{n - 1} \sum_j g_j(1)$ of \S\ref{sec:libra}. In round $i$, with $m \coloneqq n - i - 1$, the current variable $X_m = X$ stays affine, the high variables are fixed to $r_{m + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}$, and the $m$ low variables are summed over $\set{0, \infty}^m$ by the same vertex analysis:
\[
  R_g^{(i)}(X) = \sum_{v \in \set{0, \infty}^m} g(v, X, r_{m + 1}, \ldots, r_{n - 1}) = g_c + \sum_{j = 0}^{m - 1} g_{j, d - 1} + g_{m}(X)\, X + \sum_{j = m + 1}^{n - 1} g_j(r_j)\, r_j.
\]
A quantity independent of the low coordinates passes through the sum unchanged, since $\sum_{v_i \in \set{0, \infty}} 1 = 1$, so the constant $g_c$, the fresh univariate $g_m(X)\, X$, and the high-variable terms are carried with unit coefficient, and each low term contributes only its top coefficient. The degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients of $R_g^{(i)}$ that enter the transmitted $R^{(i)}$ are therefore precisely $g_{n - i - 1, 0}, \ldots, g_{n - i - 1, d - 1}$, with no power of two to vanish in characteristic $2$.

\paragraph{The masking messages.}
Fix the challenges $\tau, r$. The masking messages -- $s_g$ together with, for each round $i$, the degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients of $R_g^{(i)}$ that enter the sent $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha\, R_g^{(i)}$ -- number $1 + nd$ and form a fixed $K$-linear image of the coefficient vector $c_g = (g_c, (g_{j, k})_{j < n,\, k < d})$ of the mask.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:masking-uniform}
For the multilinear-extension check over the Boolean hypercube, and for the bare sumcheck over the infinity hypercube, this map is a bijection of $K^{1 + nd}$ in every characteristic; for the bare sumcheck over the Boolean hypercube it is a bijection if and only if $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq 2$. Whenever it is a bijection and $g'$ is uniform, the masking messages are uniform on $K^{1 + nd}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In every case the degree-$\ge 1$ coefficients of $R_g^{(i)}$ come entirely from the fresh univariate $g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X$, scaled by a constant that depends on the domain, so the map is a bijection exactly when that constant is a unit in each round: reading the round messages in order $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$ then recovers $g_{n - 1}, g_{n - 2}, \ldots, g_0$ in turn, after which $s_g$ recovers $g_c$. By the closed form of \S\ref{sec:zk-mle-check}, the multilinear-extension check has $R_g^{(i)}(X) = \bigl( g_c + \sum_{j > n - i - 1} g_j(r_j) r_j + \sum_{j < n - i - 1} \tau_j g_j(1) \bigr) + g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X$, and by the computation above the bare sumcheck over the infinity hypercube has $R_g^{(i)}(X) = \bigl( g_c + \sum_{j < n - i - 1} g_{j, d - 1} + \sum_{j > n - i - 1} g_j(r_j) r_j \bigr) + g_{n - i - 1}(X)\, X$; in both the constant is $1$, so the map is a bijection in every characteristic, with no condition on the challenges. For the bare sumcheck over the Boolean hypercube (\S\ref{sec:libra}) the corresponding coefficients are $2^{n - i - 1} g_{n - i - 1, k}$, so the round-$i$ block is this same bijection scaled by $2^{n - i - 1}$ -- invertible exactly when $2^{n - i - 1} \neq 0$ for every $i$, i.e.\ when $\operatorname{char}(K) \neq 2$. In characteristic $2$ the blocks with $i < n - 1$ vanish, the rank falls to $1 + d$, and the non-constant coefficients of the first $n - 1$ round polynomials lie outside the image. A bijection carries the uniform distribution on $g$ to the uniform distribution on the messages.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Simulator.}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:zk-mle-check}]
The simulator $\mathcal{S}$ receives the public claim $(\tau, s_f)$ and the reduced value $f^*$ -- in the IronSpartan composition $f^*$ is itself furnished by the surrounding honest-verifier simulator (\S\ref{sec:zk-compiler}) -- and has no access to $f$ or the witness. It runs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sample the oracle $\pi_g = g' \gets K^{\BB_{\nu + \delta}}$ uniformly, as the honest prover does.
\item Sample $s_g \gets K$; receive $\alpha$; set $s^{(0)} \coloneqq s_f + \alpha\, s_g$.
\item For $i = 0, \ldots, n - 1$: sample the transmitted coefficients of $R^{(i)}$ uniformly; let the verifier recover the remaining coefficient and sample $r_{n - i - 1}$; set $s^{(i + 1)} \coloneqq R^{(i)}(r_{n - i - 1})$.
\item Set $g^* \coloneqq (s^{(n)} - f^*)/\alpha$ and program the response to the single query $\langle g', \textsf{libra-eval}_r \rangle$ to equal $g^*$.
\end{enumerate}
In an honest run the messages are $s^{(0)} = s_f + \alpha\, s_g$ and $R^{(i)} = R_f^{(i)} + \alpha\, R_g^{(i)}$. Conditioned on the challenges, $f$ fixes $s_f$ and the $R_f^{(i)}$, while the mask supplies $s_g$ and the $R_g^{(i)}$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform} these are jointly uniform over the uniform choice of $g'$, so for $\alpha \neq 0$ the sent $(s^{(0)}, (R^{(i)})_i)$ are uniform, matching $\mathcal{S}$ exactly. The lone opening of $\pi_g$ lands at the point encoded by $\textsf{libra-eval}_r$, where $\mathcal{S}$ programs the response to satisfy $f^* = s^{(n)} - \alpha\, g^*$; as $g'$ is committed and opened nowhere else, this is undetectable. The two transcripts coincide except on the event $\alpha = 0$, of probability $1/|K|$, giving statistical distance $O(1)/|K|$. The same simulator serves the bare sumcheck over the infinity hypercube, where Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform} again gives uniform masking messages. The argument fails for the bare sumcheck over the Boolean hypercube in characteristic $2$, where by Lemma~\ref{lem:masking-uniform} the masking messages are confined to a proper subspace and so cannot match the uniform simulation.
\end{proof}
